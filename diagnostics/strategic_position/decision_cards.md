# Strategic posture decision cards

Each card follows one node (a country x technology x supply-chain stage) through four steps:

1. **Strategic Position**: what the evidence says. Competitive Position and Strategic Need are percentiles across countries in the cell; Revealed Policy Priority is a percentile across the country's own cells; evidence specificity says how much of the score rests on stage-specific observations.
2. **Strategic Posture**: what kind of problem the node is. Secure (an acute consequence meets an exploitable vulnerability), Compete (a producing node with a capability case, a credible strategy and a proportionate cost) or Deploy (neither: openness, low cost and diversified supply). Five tri-state tests decide it; unknown inputs are bracketed, and the recommendation is the strict reading, so an unproven case for intervention is not met.
3. **Policy Design**: the fence (what is restricted, and how narrowly), the gate (what stays open, and to whom) and the build (what capacity is created, and where), with the delivery mode.
4. **Evaluation**: observed policy compared with the recommendation, and the benchmark, exit condition and review date that say when to reclassify.

The posture is categorical: no score or index is computed for it. Observed policy never enters a test, and concentration alone never makes a node Secure.

**Caveat on observed policy.** Revealed Policy Priority rests on GTA NIPO and IEA PAMS records. A low observed policy priority is low observed NIPO/PAMS intervention, not proof that the government gives the node no priority: GTA does not record standards, technical regulation, most domestic regulation or agreed international measures, and NIPO has no Deployment evidence.

The table holds 14454 nodes: 18 Secure, 215 Compete, 13891 Deploy, 330 not classified.

## Secure

_A node where an acute consequence meets an exploitable vulnerability (Tests 1 and 2)._

### Japan - Electric Vehicles - Refining

**Recommended posture: SECURE** (secure_input) | Confidence: Moderate (90% of relevant inputs known) | Primary rationale: Security (secondary: capability) | Delivery: Mixed

- **Evidence:** Competitive Position 96th percentile (across countries) | Strategic Need 99th percentile (across countries) | Observed Policy Priority 40th percentile (within the country) | Evidence specificity: High
- **Supply:** world share 2.6% | largest potentially coercive supplier China (CHN) with 94% of world supply | trusted supply (home and same-bloc partners) 4.0% of world supply

**Decision tests**

- Critical consequence: **Yes**. Disruption would have a high consequence (Magnet and battery materials for vehicle production) and the country depends on the node.
- Exploitable vulnerability (physical path): **Yes** (marginal). CHN holds 94% of world supply, the country takes 88% of its imports from coercive suppliers, substitution is hard, and trusted supply could not be restored before harm becomes intolerable.
- Competitive capability: **Strong** (marginal). A strong capability case: established position, strong transferable learning on a domestic base, or two rationales together.
- Credible strategy: **No**. No credible path: no present position, no established adjacent stage, and no catch-up or leap case.
- Proportionality: **Pass**. Premium not measured; trusted partners hold only 1% of world supply.

**Alignment**

- Competitive-policy alignment (diagnostic): Latent advantage (position high, observed priority low)
- Policy alignment (observed vs recommended): Mixed: NIPO and PAMS point in opposite directions, or a Compete node is fenced without a build.

**Policy design**

- Fence (moderate, input): A targeted fence that limits a coercive supplier's leverage over the input, without cutting supply before trusted alternatives qualify.
- Gate (selective): Selective: open to allied and other trusted producers, their investment and equipment, and to alternative inputs; the coercive supplier's share falls as trusted supply qualifies. Delivery (mixed): The gate stays open to allied partners' technology, equipment and investment for the capacity not built at home.
- Build: Repair the chokepoint until a prolonged cutoff could be survived: qualified trusted supply (domestic or allied), stockpiles, surge and redundancy. The goal is mitigation, not maximum localisation. Delivery (mixed): No source holds enough trusted supply to repair the vulnerability alone: create new trusted capacity at home (where a foothold exists or can be built) and with allied partners through co-investment and offtake, and bridge the years until it qualifies with stockpiles.

**Evaluation**

- **Benchmark:** Critical production could continue through a prolonged cutoff on inventories plus qualified trusted supply.
- **Exit condition:** Reclassify to Compete or Deploy when a prolonged cutoff could be survived (trusted supply, stockpiles and surge cover the restore time) or the largest coercive supplier falls below the screening line. Announced capacity does not count until it is qualified.
- **Review:** every 2 years
- **Success metrics:**
  - Months critical production could continue through a cutoff (inventories plus qualified alternative supply)
  - Qualified trusted capacity as a share of critical demand, and how fast it can surge
  - Share of supply held by the largest potentially coercive supplier
  - Liquidity and survival of sub-tier suppliers through a price war or cutoff

## Compete

_A producing node with a capability case, a credible strategy and a proportionate cost (Tests 3 to 5)._

### China - Batteries - Extraction

**Recommended posture: COMPETE** (compete_preserve) | Confidence: High (91% of relevant inputs known) | Primary rationale: Capability (secondary: security) | Compete strategy: Preserve | Delivery: Domestic

- **Evidence:** Competitive Position 99th percentile (across countries) | Strategic Need 98th percentile (across countries) | Observed Policy Priority 48th percentile (within the country) | Evidence specificity: High
- **Supply:** world share 54% | largest potentially coercive supplier Australia (AUS) with 34% of world supply | trusted supply (home and same-bloc partners) 32% of world supply

**Decision tests**

- Critical consequence: **No**. A moderate consequence does not reach the acute threshold, however concentrated supply is.
- Exploitable vulnerability (no path): **No** (marginal). The country itself holds 54% of world supply: it is not exposed through its imports.
- Competitive capability: **Strong**. A strong capability case: established position, strong transferable learning on a domestic base, or two rationales together.
- Credible strategy: **Preserve**. Preserve: the country already holds a meaningful share of world supply that could be hollowed out.
- Proportionality: **Pass**. Premium not measured; trusted partners hold only 9% of world supply.

**Alignment**

- Competitive-policy alignment (diagnostic): Latent advantage (position high, observed priority low)
- Policy alignment (observed vs recommended): Mixed: NIPO and PAMS point in opposite directions, or a Compete node is fenced without a build.

**Policy design**

- Fence (moderate, product): Calibrated, temporary protection only where unfair competition threatens an established base.
- Gate (open): Open with conditions: allied and conditional investment, equipment imports, talent and import competition keep the industry at the frontier. Delivery (domestic): The gate stays open to foreign technology, capital and talent on terms that deepen domestic capability.
- Build: Keep the established position competitive: process and next-generation R&D, workforce and supplier development, and finance for expansion, tied to benchmarks. Delivery (domestic): Build at home: production support, finance, workforce and supplier development for the domestic industry, tied to benchmarks.

**Evaluation**

- **Benchmark:** Yields, cost and quality stay at the global frontier while public support per unit falls.
- **Exit condition:** Support sunsets at each review unless the benchmarks are met. Move to Deploy if the capability case fails, and re-test for Secure if a control layer or coercive chokepoint is identified.
- **Review:** every 3 years
- **Success metrics:**
  - Yields, costs and quality relative to the global frontier
  - Domestic engineers able to diagnose and modify the process without outside assistance
  - Domestic suppliers qualifying for more demanding parts of the value chain
  - Process improvements and successor-technology R&D generated domestically
  - Public support per unit falling as competitiveness improves

## Deploy

_A node with neither a security case nor a capability case: openness is the recommendation._

### Germany - Gas - Deployment

**Recommended posture: DEPLOY** (deploy) | Confidence: High (100% of relevant inputs known) | Primary rationale: Openness | Delivery: Diversified global

- **Evidence:** Competitive Position 69th percentile (across countries) | Strategic Need 28th percentile (across countries) | Observed Policy Priority 84th percentile (within the country) | Evidence specificity: High

**Decision tests**

- Critical consequence: **Yes**. Disruption would have a severe consequence (Heating, power and industry supplied from the gas network) and the country depends on the node.
- Exploitable vulnerability (no path): **No**. Deployment is where the technology is used: its supply vulnerability is assessed at the producing stages and in its control layers.
- Competitive capability: **None**. Deployment is where the technology is used, not produced: no industrial-capability case is tested.
- Credible strategy: **Not applicable**. Not tested at Deployment.
- Proportionality: **Not applicable**. Not tested at Deployment.

**Alignment**

- Competitive-policy alignment (diagnostic): Policy-led bet (observed priority high, position low)
- Policy alignment (observed vs recommended): Aligned: support for deployment itself is what a Deploy recommendation asks for.

**Policy design**

- Fence (low, cross cutting): A low fence: cross-cutting safeguards only (genuine trade remedies, forced-labour restrictions, basic security and safety standards).
- Gate (wide open): Wide open: diversified imports, broad supplier access and competition keep the good cheap and fast to deploy. Delivery (diversified global): The gate stays wide open to a broad supplier base.
- Build: Build what enables deployment (infrastructure, permitting, interoperability, procurement efficiency, supply-chain transparency), not domestic production. Delivery (diversified global): Supply comes from a diversified global base; the build is enabling infrastructure, not production.

**Evaluation**

- **Benchmark:** Delivered cost and deployment pace match or beat peers, with diversified supply and adequate resilience. Domestic production share is not a benchmark.
- **Exit condition:** Re-test if concentration rises (the largest coercive supplier or the import HHI crosses its watch line), a control layer or physical chokepoint is identified, or a credible capability case emerges.
- **Review:** every 5 years
- **Success metrics:**
  - Delivered cost and pace of deployment
  - Supplier diversity (HHI) and the share held by any potentially coercive supplier
  - Compliance with cross-cutting safeguards (forced labour, safety, trade law)

## High need, low position, allied delivery

_High Strategic Need with a low domestic Competitive Position, delivered with allies: capacity is built with partners, not at home._

### Australia - Electric Grid - Assembly - Smart meters and advanced metering head-end systems

**Recommended posture: SECURE** (secure_integrated) | Confidence: Moderate (67% of relevant inputs known) | Primary rationale: Security | Delivery: Allied

- **Evidence:** Competitive Position 64th percentile (across countries) | Strategic Need 62nd percentile (across countries) | Observed Policy Priority 69th percentile (within the country) | Evidence specificity: High
- **Supply:** world share 0.2% | largest potentially coercive supplier China (CHN) with 23% of world supply | trusted supply (home and same-bloc partners) 52% of world supply

**Decision tests**

- Critical consequence: **Yes**. Disruption would have a high consequence (Customer supply (remote disconnect) and demand data) and the country depends on the node.
- Exploitable vulnerability (digital path): **Yes**. An outside actor could command, update or disable this layer, and 49% of the country's supply of the carrying hardware comes from potentially coercive suppliers.
- Competitive capability: **None**. Little present position (0% of world supply) and no base for a learning or leap case.
- Credible strategy: **No**. No credible path: no present position, no established adjacent stage, and no catch-up or leap case.
- Proportionality: **Pass**. Premium not measured; the good is not deployment-critical.

**Alignment**

- Competitive-policy alignment (diagnostic): Policy-led bet (observed priority high, position low)
- Policy alignment (observed vs recommended): Aligned: observed policy priority matches the Secure recommendation.

**Policy design**

- Fence (high, control plus integrated): A high fence around the control layer and the hardware it is tied to, narrowed as the control layer is separated.
- Gate (selective): Selective: closed to untrusted vendors on the control layer and its integrated hardware, open to allied and other trusted vendors and to the rest of the product from any lawful source. Delivery (allied): The gate stays open to those partners' products, investment and equipment, and trusted-source rules recognise allied suppliers.
- Build: Repair the vulnerability and shrink the fence: trusted replacements now, and redesigns that separate the control layer from its hardware. Delivery (allied): Build trusted capacity with partners, not at home: compacts, offtake agreements, co-investment and shared stockpiles with allied producers.

**Evaluation**

- **Benchmark:** The control layer can be separated from its hardware, and the installed base runs certified trusted control software or is isolated from untrusted remote access.
- **Exit condition:** Narrow to secure_control once the control layer is separated from its hardware, and reclassify to Compete or Deploy when the command path is closed.
- **Review:** every 2 years
- **Success metrics:**
  - Share of the installed base running certified, trusted control software or isolated from remote access
  - Time to detect and patch a firmware vulnerability across the fleet
  - Availability of certified trusted replacements at deployment scale
  - Whether the control layer can be swapped without replacing the hardware (the fence can shrink)

**Sensitive layers in this cell**

- Power conversion hardware (inverters and converters) (hardware): Deploy (deploy_diversify)
- Smart meters and advanced metering head-end systems (control): Secure (secure_integrated)
- Cables and conductors (hardware): Deploy (deploy_diversify)
- Utility-scale inverter firmware and remote-access functions (control): Secure (secure_unclear)
- Grid-control software (SCADA, EMS, DERMS) (control): Secure (secure_control)
- Large power transformers (hardware): Deploy (deploy_diversify)
- HVDC converter-station control and protection (control): Deploy (deploy_diversify)

A layer's posture never changes the cell's posture: the layer gets its own fence.
