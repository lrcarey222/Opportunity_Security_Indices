# United States–Australia Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 30 October 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: AC generators (US->Australia). Battery Materials & Cells: Li-ion cells & packs (US->Australia); Other accumulators (joint). Wind & Hydropower Equipment: Wind turbine generating sets (US->Australia). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Wind assembly (US->Australia). Electric Vehicles: Electric Vehicles assembly (US->Australia).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of batteries extraction (cobalt ores, lithium minerals, natural graphite); oil extraction in procurement and content rules. Bind market access for li-ion cells & packs, batteries assembly (li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators), batteries components (accumulator parts & separators), wind turbine generating sets, wind components (ac generators, ferrite & other magnets, insulators & bushings), wind assembly (electricity meters, overhead conductors, power cables), ac generators, solar assembly (electricity meters, overhead conductors, power cables). Keep nuclear special materials; transformers; uranium conversion & enrichment open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade US-Australia Critical Minerals and Rare Earths Framework (Oct 2025): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Coal extraction capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Gas extraction capacity. Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 142 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair, 24 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 2 direct competition and 92 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Australia Resource & Processing Anchor (primary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Australia supplies rare-earth metals, lithium minerals, uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores) where the US is short; the US supplies li-ion cells & packs, wind turbine generating sets, ac generators, battery recycling feed where Australia is short; the two share exposure to the same coercive supplier in other accumulators, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Australia brings resources and processing. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and EFA, NRFC, CEFC, ARENA on the Australia side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Australia's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP, U.S. FTA. [1]
- ASPI lists the MSP, which Korea chairs in 2025, among the frameworks for Australia-Korea minerals cooperation; Australian membership is not stated in that article. [2]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [3]
- The Response Group was established in March 2026 after a Tokyo investment ministerial on 14 March; the US launched the FORGE forum in February 2026; the group's outputs and meeting record are not published in these sources. [4]
- Quad foreign ministers adopted a framework to mobilise up to $20bn for secure critical-mineral supply chains; a separate July 2025 Quad initiative is mentioned only in passing and not sourced. [5]
- Launched July 2025 and framework adopted May 2026 to mobilise up to US$20bn of public and private support for mining, processing and recycling; no project list. [6]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, US imported US$1.3bn from Australia and Australia imported US$7.3bn from US (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- AUSFTA is in force with a Joint Committee supervising implementation and committees on goods, agriculture and SPS; it gave no exemption from the 2025-26 US tariffs; entry-into-force date not stated on the page. [7]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- Signed 20 May 2023; set up a Clean Energy Industrial Transformation forum and a ministerial Taskforce on Critical Minerals led by Industry, Science and Resources and the US NSC; no dollar amounts. [8]
- Signed 20 Oct 2025; each government to provide at least US$1bn within six months toward a US$8.5bn pipeline of priority projects in both countries; creates a Supply Security Response Group; release names no price floor. [9]
- Australia's US$3bn contribution to the US submarine industrial base funded Electric Boat's first AUKUS contract in April 2026; sources differ on how much has been paid and on whether Australia is assured a Virginia-class boat; no clean-energy supply link identified. [10]

**Investment vehicle, project pipeline and financing**

- At signing EXIM issued seven non-binding letters of interest totalling over US$2.2bn, including Northern Minerals, Graphinex and La Trobe Magnesium; these are preliminary and not finalised loans. [11]
- OSC announced a conditional commitment of up to US$400m for a proposed 25-year loan to Sunrise Energy Metals' Syerston scandium project in New South Wales, subject to conditions; August date approximate. [12]

**Corporate relationships in the recommended compacts**

- The trilateral Wagerup gallium project reportedly involves Sojitz and the Japanese government with an estimated $174M US Dept of War equity investment and groundbreaking on 2026-08-24; the equity announcement year is unclear. [13]
- Lynas said in August 2025 it was negotiating a DoD offtake for Seadrift output on commercial terms and flagged uncertainty over whether the plant would proceed. [14]
- Australia offered up to US$200m concessional equity with an offtake right and the US also takes equity with an offtake right; trilateral with Japan, which has funded 50% of costs; could supply up to 10% of world gallium. [15]
- A US$100m Australian equity investment in the Nolans project in the Northern Territory, expected to supply about 5% of world rare earths once operating; US side of financing not stated in the release. [16]
- US-based Albemarle idled its last Kemerton, Western Australia lithium hydroxide train in February 2026 after putting Train 2 on care and maintenance in 2024; Greenbushes and Wodgina stakes are unaffected. [17]
- Lynas USA signed a binding letter of intent for DoW to buy about US$96m of light and heavy rare earth oxides over four years with a US$110/kg NdPr floor, replacing the earlier Seadrift-linked arrangement; final supply terms and tonnages not stated. [18]
- Lynas has said there is significant uncertainty whether Seadrift will be built and in what form, and supplies the US from Malaysia; the March 2026 DoW deal replaced the Seadrift-linked purchase; no cancellation or construction start confirmed. [19]
- In April 2026 EXIM and EFA issued coordinated letters for up to about US$1bn for Ardea Resources' Kalgoorlie nickel project and about US$849m for Tronox's rare earths refinery; letters are not committed loans; exact date not given. [20]
- Australia's non-recourse loan to Iluka totals A$1.65bn after a A$400m top-up in May 2026, with commissioning planned mid-2027; the loan is Australian-funded and no US offtake or EXIM role was found; May date approximate. [21]
- DoW announced an estimated US$174m equity financing on 31 Aug 2026 after groundbreaking on 24 Aug; the plant is designed for 100 t a year of gallium; secondary report, DoW release not read. [22]
- Final investment decision in May 2026 after an Australian offtake commitment under the Strategic Reserve; construction began 8 Oct 2026; credit agencies from the US, Canada, Korea and Germany support it; the article's A$1.2bn figure is ambiguous. [23]

- Unverified items are left out of this section; they stay in the evidence register (evidence_register.csv) and the evidence layer. [1]

- *Inference. *Working channels today: US-Australia Critical Minerals and Rare Earths Framework (Oct 2025) (a project pipeline and finance). What the relationship lacks: no cross-sector KPI dashboard or review cycle; USAU-M2 has no published project pipeline.
