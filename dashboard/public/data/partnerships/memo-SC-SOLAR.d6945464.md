# Solar Manufacturing Compact

*Where coordinated industrial integration across allied economies creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Wafers & silver paste: Wafers. Polysilicon: Polysilicon. Modules: PV modules & generators. Cells & cover glass: PV cover glass.
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise member supply of wafers, polysilicon and PV cover glass in procurement and content rules. Bind market access among members for PV modules & generators and solar cells.
- **Institutions.** Give Quad Clean Energy Supply Chains Initiative and Diversification Program a compact mandate: pipeline, reciprocal commitments, KPIs and an annual review. No new body.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new silver powder capacity among members. Do not run parallel national subsidy races for solar cells. Do not create a new compact secretariat or treaty body.

**Why this compact matters.**

- Of 6 electro-industrial nodes in the compact, 5 show an active compact relation (member supply meeting member need, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a shared chokepoint), 1 member competition and 0 no strategic case. Nearly every step is under stress, so a compact is the right scale of response; the rules then rank where it acts first (4 nodes need new capacity, the rest coordinated trade). [1]
- A coercive supplier holds at least 50% of world supply at 5 nodes: wafers (CHN 98%); polysilicon (CHN 97%); PV modules & generators (CHN 84%); PV cover glass (CHN 61%); solar cells (CHN 92%). [1]
- AIB node classes under the China shock: BUILD 4, LOW INTERVENTION 2; allied demand across the compact's nodes US$67.4bn. [1]
- AIB headline: Managed Interdependence: 28% of the compact's allied demand (US$18.5bn) has to shift away from suppliers above the cap; coverage 74% with existing relationships, 74% with coordinated trade. [1]
- 24 member economies; core (AIB): United States (demand anchor); Vietnam (scale manufacturer); Japan (specialized technology supplier); India (demand anchor); Germany (processing anchor); Korea (specialized technology supplier). [1]

**The central compact bargain.**

- *Inference. *By 2024 trade, the largest member suppliers (Vietnam, Japan, Malaysia, Korea and 2 others) supply wafers, polysilicon, PV modules & generators, PV cover glass and solar cells, and the largest member buyers are United States, Korea, Singapore, India and 6 others; no node is a shared vulnerability that no member can supply.
- *Inference. *The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity: suppliers commit capacity and priority supply, buyers commit offtake and procurement recognition, and the compact keeps the remaining nodes open.

**Do not prioritise** (Section 10):

- *Recommendation. *Do not co-subsidise new silver powder capacity among members: United States and Japan are already strong suppliers. Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. *Do not run parallel national subsidy races for solar cells: 3 members (Thailand, Malaysia and Korea) are already strong suppliers; agree in the compact who expands where.
- *Recommendation. *Do not create a new compact secretariat or treaty body: Quad Clean Energy Supply Chains Initiative and Diversification Program already exists (finance and projects but no published list); dock the compact in it.

**Existing mechanisms and the governance question** (Section 8):

- *Inference. *Governance assessment: **upgrade an existing plurilateral mechanism (CSOL-M1)**. Gaps: CSOL-M1 has no published project pipeline; CSOL-M1 has no KPI dashboard or review cycle; core members outside CSOL-M1: VNM, DEU, KOR.
- *Recommendation. *Do not create a new compact body. Give Quad Clean Energy Supply Chains Initiative and Diversification Program a compact mandate for these nodes: a shared project pipeline, reciprocal supplier and buyer commitments and a KPI dashboard, reviewed every 12 months.

**Market structure**

- China's share of every PV manufacturing stage exceeds 80% and was heading to almost 95% for polysilicon, ingots and wafers; Xinjiang alone held about 40% of global polysilicon output. Costs in China were 10% below India, 20% below the US and 35% below Europe, and electricity is over 40% of polysilicon production cost. [4]
- Korea's trade ministry asked Washington for flexible treatment in the polysilicon probe, saying Hanwha Qcells and OCI avoid cheap Chinese polysilicon, with Qcells sourcing mainly from OCI's Malaysian plants; US-to-Korea polysilicon volumes are not documented in this source. [5]
- US module capacity is 77.3 GW (61 GW crystalline), with 51.5 GW of production (37.5 GW crystalline). Cell capacity is 26.5 GW including thin film but only 10 GW crystalline, with 5.2 GW crystalline output. Wafer capacity is 5 GW with 3.2 GW produced. Polysilicon capacity is 36 GW against 15.5 GW of PV-allocated production. More than 40 GW of extra cell capacity is due within 18 months, and wafering is the tightest bottleneck. [6]
- The Commission's preliminary data on 2025 shares of EU supply, used for the NZIA resilience criterion, are as follows. PV modules and cells: 95% from China. PV wafers: 59% China, 11% United States, 11% Singapore. PV inverters: 66% China, 8% Japan, 3% UK. Complete PV systems: 93% China, 2% Japan. [7]

**Failure / exit conditions**

- No signed offtake or financing at a node within 24 months of launch: drop the node from the compact.
- The coercive share of world supply falls below the chokepoint line, or a node's AIB class turns LOW INTERVENTION: move that node to open-market treatment.
- Nodes already in the AIB's LOW INTERVENTION class rest on the chokepoint or capacity case alone; support ends when that case does.
- Projects proceed on commercial terms without public support: stop the support (it is not additional).

## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not co-subsidise new silver powder capacity among members: United States and Japan are already strong suppliers. Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not run parallel national subsidy races for solar cells: 3 members (Thailand, Malaysia and Korea) are already strong suppliers; agree in the compact who expands where.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not create a new compact secretariat or treaty body: Quad Clean Energy Supply Chains Initiative and Diversification Program already exists (finance and projects but no published list); dock the compact in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not put new public money behind solar cells projects that are already committed (evidence USKR-21, USIN-25, CSOL-27, CSOL-36): member suppliers already serve member buyers, so compact support is additional only beyond them.
