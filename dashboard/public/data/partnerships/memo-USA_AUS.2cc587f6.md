# United States–Australia Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-13); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 30 October 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: AC generators (US->Australia). Battery Materials & Cells: Li-ion cells & packs (joint); Other accumulators (joint). Wind & Hydropower Equipment: Wind turbine generating sets (US->Australia).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of rare-earth metals; lithium minerals; battery recycling feed in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for li-ion cells & packs. Bind market access for wind turbine generating sets, ac generators, uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores). Keep nuclear special materials; transformers; overhead conductors open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade US-Australia Critical Minerals and Rare Earths Framework (Oct 2025): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution. Do not defend Automatic controllers as a bilateral priority. Do not defend Fuel assemblies & zirconium as a bilateral priority.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 142 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair, 24 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 2 direct competition and 92 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Australia Resource & Processing Anchor (primary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Australia supplies rare-earth metals, lithium minerals, uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores) where the US is short; the US supplies wind turbine generating sets, ac generators, battery recycling feed, reactor parts where Australia is short; the two share exposure to the same coercive supplier in li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Australia brings resources and processing. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and EFA, NRFC, CEFC, ARENA on the Australia side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-13); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Australia's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP, U.S. FTA. [1]
- Quad leaders committed to identify and address gaps in manufacturing capacity for clean-energy technologies, focusing on solar PV, hydrogen electrolysers and EV batteries. They adopted joint principles for clean-energy supply chains and planned a public-private Quad Clean Energy Supply Chain Conference. [2]
- Australia was to open applications in Nov 2024 for the AUD 50m (about US$34m at Sep 2024 rates) programme supporting solar-panel, electrolyser and battery supply-chain projects in the Indo-Pacific. India committed US$2m to solar projects in Fiji, Comoros, Madagascar and Seychelles, and Japan US$122m to renewable projects. Awards and 2025-26 activity are not stated. [3]
- The October 2025 framework provides for a Critical Minerals Supply Security Response Group led by the US Secretary of Energy and the Australian Minister for Resources to identify priority minerals and supply vulnerabilities and to plan faster delivery of processed minerals. Secondary reports say it was set up in March 2026 and covers geological mapping, permitting, waste-stream recovery and unfair trade practices. Its meeting record and outputs are not published. [4]
- Announced in Toronto on 31 Oct 2025, this is the first round of the Production Alliance under the June 2025 G7 Critical Minerals Action Plan. Its 26 investments, partnerships and measures with France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, Norway, the US, Australia and Ukraine aim to unlock C$6.4bn (about US$4.6bn at 0.71 USD/CAD) of graphite, rare-earth and scandium projects. They include Government of Canada offtakes with Nouveau Monde Graphite and Rio Tinto (scandium) and up to C$20.2m for joint R&D. Canada also released a roadmap for standards-based critical-minerals markets. [5]
- ASPI lists the MSP, which Korea chairs in 2025, among the frameworks for Australia-Korea minerals cooperation; Australian membership is not stated in that article. [6]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [7]
- At the 19 Feb 2026 Ministerial, IEA ministers endorsed an expanded phase of the Critical Minerals Security Programme, which was established in 2022. The expansion covers: support for members that set up or expand stockpiles; rapid market assessments and coordinated responses during supply disruptions; regular table-top exercises (a rare-earth exercise was held in Nov 2025); a wider information dashboard; a new Technology Collaboration Programme on recycling and substitution. The release gives no budget. [8]
- Quad foreign ministers adopted a framework to mobilise up to $20bn for secure critical-mineral supply chains; a separate July 2025 Quad initiative is mentioned only in passing and not sourced. [9]
- Launched July 2025 and framework adopted May 2026 to mobilise up to US$20bn of public and private support for mining, processing and recycling; no project list. [10]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [11]

Shareholders re-approved the issue on 22 Oct 2026, and both subscriptions are expected to complete when the Nolans project debt reaches contractual close. [33]

Early construction works began in the September 2026 quarter, about A$700m of procurement scope was advanced, and a ground-breaking ceremony was held after 30 Sep. - Low-evidence units among the active states: uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores).
