# United States–Germany Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Synthetic graphite (anode-grade) † (Germany->US); Natural graphite (joint); Other accumulators (joint). Electric Vehicles: Electric & plug-in cars (Germany->US). Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Rare-earth oxides & compounds (joint).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of electric & plug-in cars; oil extraction; polysilicon † in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for rare-earth oxides & compounds and li-ion cells & packs. Keep market access open and predictable for synthetic graphite (anode-grade) † (no new tariff on the trusted supplier). Keep nuclear special materials; transformers; overhead conductors open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade US-EU critical minerals MOU and action plan (EU-level, applies to Germany): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Automatic controllers capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Electric buses, trucks & tractors capacity. Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 117 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair (not counting 25 cells whose products are read node by node), 30 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 2 direct competition and 63 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Germany Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Germany supplies synthetic graphite (anode-grade) †, electric & plug-in cars, electric motors, motor & generator parts where the US is short; the US supplies refrigerants where Germany is short; the two share exposure to coercive suppliers in rare-earth oxides & compounds, natural graphite, other accumulators, li-ion cells & packs, copper smelting & refining, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity; and they specialise at different steps of the same chains in polysilicon †, battery recycling feed, sensors & controller parts, non-oriented electrical steel (NGO).
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Germany brings capital, scale manufacturing, demand and offtake. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and KfW, KfW IPEX, Hermes, UFK, DEG on the Germany side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Germany's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: G7, MSP, NATO. [1]
- Germany and the USA are both original MSP partners and automatic members of the MSP Forum launched in 2024. [2]
- TTC (created 2021) had its future questioned under the 2025 administration; no formal termination or revival was found, so it is treated as dormant and superseded by the 2025 framework and 2026 critical minerals MOU. [3]
- G7 leaders adopted the plan at Kananaskis on 17 Jun 2025, under Canada's presidency. It tasks ministers with a roadmap for standards-based markets with traceability by year-end, calls on export credit agencies, DFIs and MDBs to scale up mining finance, and targets processing, recycling and substitution R&D. Australia, India and Korea later endorsed it. [4]
- G7 leaders (including the USA and Germany) launched the Action Plan at Kananaskis on 17 Jun 2025 to diversify production, encourage investment and local value creation; interested members support the Minerals Security Partnership; no named German-US projects. [5]
- Announced in Toronto on 31 Oct 2025, this is the first round of the Production Alliance under the June 2025 G7 Critical Minerals Action Plan. Its 26 investments, partnerships and measures with France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, Norway, the US, Australia and Ukraine aim to unlock C$6.4bn (about US$4.6bn at 0.71 USD/CAD) of graphite, rare-earth and scandium projects. They include Government of Canada offtakes with Nouveau Monde Graphite and Rio Tinto (scandium) and up to C$20.2m for joint R&D. Canada also released a roadmap for standards-based critical-minerals markets. [6]
- Adopted in Toronto on 31 Oct 2025 as an early action under the Action Plan, it aims to reduce and eventually eliminate dependence on supply chains dominated by non-market practices. It prioritises interoperable traceability systems aligned with the UN Transparency Protocol and lists price floors, procurement, tax and trade measures as tools members may use, with no deadlines. [7]
- Carney launched the Alliance at Kananaskis to use strategic investments and offtakes to bring critical-mineral projects into production for defence and advanced technology. At the 30-31 Oct 2025 Toronto ministerial, ministers agreed their critical-minerals envoys would advance the work, and Canada announced investments including offtakes with two Canadian projects. Member list, deal count, values and the US role are not stated in either source. [8]
- At the 19 Feb 2026 Ministerial, IEA ministers endorsed an expanded phase of the Critical Minerals Security Programme, which was established in 2022. The expansion covers: support for members that set up or expand stockpiles; rapid market assessments and coordinated responses during supply disruptions; regular table-top exercises (a rare-earth exercise was held in Nov 2025); a wider information dashboard; a new Technology Collaboration Programme on recycling and substitution. The release gives no budget. [9]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [10]

## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not co-subsidise new Automatic controllers capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 16%, Germany 12%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not co-subsidise new Electric buses, trucks & tractors capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 15%, Germany 11%). More trusted capacity is needed, so agree who specialises in what rather than racing.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution: USDE-M3 already exists (a standing framework with no pipeline or finance yet); dock the compacts in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not put new public money behind Copper smelting & refining investment that is already committed (evidence USDE-22); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N05** Do not put new public money behind Li-ion cells & packs investment that is already committed (evidence USDE-23); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N06** Do not defend Solar extraction as a bilateral priority: trusted supply is adequate (a large-yard node), yet US's observed protection or attention is high.

**Where the findings are weak or counterintuitive**

- Joint bets resting on a node need inherited from its cell (a proxy): Synthetic graphite (anode-grade) †; Electric & plug-in cars; Rare-earth oxides & compounds.
- Evidence-led compacts (OSI cannot see the partner's capability in trade data): Grid Equipment & Conductors.
- Germany has no listed trade position (under 1% of world supply) at nodes where the US has material demand: rare-earth oxides & compounds, piezo-electric quartz, blade core wood (balsa), uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores), overhead conductors, aluminium wire rod, direct-reduced iron (DRI/HBI). Trade data miss capability that is owned abroad or sold at home, so these nodes cannot show complementarity even where corporate evidence suggests it.
- Product-definition caveats (†) at 5 nodes: the notes are under the Section 2 node table.
- Low-evidence units among the active states: none.
- Trade data understate concentration at uranium conversion & enrichment: enrichment is sold as a service under long-term contracts, so goods trade understates Russia's share of world enrichment capacity (roughly 40%), and the 2024 US ban on Russian low-enriched uranium imports makes trusted capacity the binding constraint.
- Trade data understate concentration at gallium, germanium & indium: China's export controls since 2023 cut its recorded exports, so 2024 trade understates its share of primary gallium and germanium output (the large majority of world supply).
- Fuel-trade lanes reach tier 1 (oil extraction; buyer: Germany): real energy-security lanes, but outside the electro-industrial compacts and with no market failure beyond the supply concentration the engine measures.
