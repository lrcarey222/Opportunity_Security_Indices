# Battery Materials & Cells Compact

*Where coordinated industrial integration across allied economies creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Electrode materials & components: Synthetic graphite (anode-grade). Refined chemicals: Cobalt intermediates & chemicals. Minerals & recycled feed: Natural graphite. Electrode materials & components: Battery materials (foil, electrolyte). Refined chemicals: Manganese dioxide (EMD).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise member supply of synthetic graphite (anode-grade), natural graphite, manganese dioxide (EMD) and 7 others in procurement and content rules. Bind market access among members for cobalt intermediates & chemicals and battery materials (foil, electrolyte).
- **Institutions.** Give Minerals Security Partnership and its successor FORGE a compact mandate: pipeline, reciprocal commitments, KPIs and an annual review. No new body.
- **Stay out.** Do not run parallel national subsidy races for battery materials (foil, electrolyte). Do not create a new compact secretariat or treaty body. Do not put new public money behind battery recycling feed projects that are already committed (evidence CBAT-24).

**Why this compact matters.**

- Of 14 electro-industrial nodes in the compact, 14 show an active compact relation (member supply meeting member need, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a shared chokepoint), 0 member competition and 0 no strategic case. Nearly every step is under stress, so a compact is the right scale of response; the rules then rank where it acts first (10 nodes need new capacity, the rest coordinated trade). [1]
- A coercive supplier holds at least 50% of world supply at 6 nodes: synthetic graphite (anode-grade) (CHN 94%); cobalt intermediates & chemicals (CHN 76%); natural graphite (CHN 82%); li-ion cells & packs (CHN 84%); cathode materials & precursors (CHN 92%); lithium chemicals (CHN 74%). [1]
- AIB node classes under the China shock: BUILD 10, INSUFFICIENT DATA 1, LOW INTERVENTION 3; allied demand across the compact's nodes US$153.0bn. [1]
- AIB headline: Managed Interdependence: 33% of the compact's allied demand (US$44.8bn) has to shift away from suppliers above the cap; coverage 67% with existing relationships, 67% with coordinated trade. 5 concentrated nodes are not tested under this scenario (see Decoupling). [1]
- 42 member economies; core (AIB): United States (resource supplier); Germany (demand anchor); Korea (specialized technology supplier); Japan (processing anchor); Indonesia (processing anchor); DR Congo (resource supplier). [1]

**The central compact bargain.**

- *Inference. *By 2024 trade, the largest member suppliers (Poland, Korea, Japan, Indonesia and 12 others) supply synthetic graphite (anode-grade), cobalt intermediates & chemicals, natural graphite, battery materials (foil, electrolyte), manganese dioxide (EMD) and 9 others, and the largest member buyers are Germany, United States, Poland, Japan and 12 others; no node is a shared vulnerability that no member can supply.
- *Inference. *The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity: suppliers commit capacity and priority supply, buyers commit offtake and procurement recognition, and the compact keeps the remaining nodes open.

**Do not prioritise** (Section 10):

- *Recommendation. *Do not run parallel national subsidy races for battery materials (foil, electrolyte): 3 members (Japan, Malaysia and Taiwan) are already strong suppliers; agree in the compact who expands where.
- *Recommendation. *Do not create a new compact secretariat or treaty body: Minerals Security Partnership and its successor FORGE already exists (partial finance and projects but no published list); dock the compact in it.
- *Recommendation. *Do not put new public money behind battery recycling feed projects that are already committed (evidence CBAT-24): member suppliers already serve member buyers, so compact support is additional only beyond them.

**Existing mechanisms and the governance question** (Section 8):

- *Inference. *Governance assessment: **upgrade an existing plurilateral mechanism (CCRM-M1)**. Gaps: CCRM-M1 has no published project pipeline; CCRM-M1 has no dedicated finance; CCRM-M1 has no KPI dashboard or review cycle; CCRM-M1 covers the critical-mineral steps only; the downstream steps need their own working group; core members outside CCRM-M1: IDN, COD.
- *Recommendation. *Do not create a new compact body. Give Minerals Security Partnership and its successor FORGE a compact mandate for the critical-mineral nodes (a shared project pipeline, reciprocal supplier and buyer commitments, a KPI dashboard reviewed every 12 months), and set up a working group of the supplier and buyer members for the downstream steps, reporting to the same dashboard.

## 1. Current State of the Compact

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation; unverified items are left out here and stay in the evidence register.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Members in OSI's economic-security agreements table: G7 8 (CAN, DEU, EUU, FRA, GBR, ITA, JPN and USA); IPEF 11 (AUS, IDN, IND, JPN, KOR, MYS, PHL, SGP, THA, USA and VNM); MSP 14 (AUS, CAN, DEU, EUU, FIN, FRA, GBR, IND, ITA, JPN, KOR, NOR and 2 others); MSP Forum 6 (ARG, COD, MEX, NAM, PHL and TUR); NATO 19 (BEL, CAN, CZE, DEU, ESP, FIN, FRA, GBR, GRC, HUN, ITA, LUX and 7 others); U.S. FTA 8 (AUS, CAN, CHL, COL, KOR, MAR, MEX and SGP). - Quad leaders pledged on 21 Sep 2024 to align policies, incentives, standards and investment for diversified clean-energy supply chains, to use policy and public finance to catalyse private investment in allied supply chains, and to focus near-term efforts on mineral production, recycling and battery manufacturing, citing complementary capabilities across the battery supply chain. No battery-specific programme, finance or targets followed in the 2025-26 Quad statements. [2]
- Korean and Japanese officials agreed a close cooperation system on critical-mineral supply risks, support for company and agency cooperation on joint development in third countries (Latin America, Asia, Africa), and policy coordination in the Minerals Security Partnership and IEA; no projects were named. **Market structure**

- A 2024 report says LGES sources separators from China's SEMCORP and Senior and from Japan's Asahi Kasei and Toray, while Samsung SDI relies mainly on Korea's WCP; no Korea-based Japanese separator plant was found in 2025-26 coverage. [6]
- POSCO Future M began cathode exports to the US in October 2025 and its GM joint venture Ultium CAM is in Quebec; no Korean cathode plant on US soil was confirmed in this search; L&F targets North American LFP supply by end Q3 2026. [7]
- SGL's anode funding found is European (EUR 42.9m IPCEI, Meitingen); the March 2026 call summary reports it closed Lavradio and idled Moses Lake in 2025 (a carbon-fibre site); no 2026 US anode plant or US offtake was found. [8]
- Wesfarmers' 2026 Annual Report (year to 30 Jun 2026) says the Covalent Lithium Kwinana refinery, a Wesfarmers-SQM joint venture, achieved first lithium hydroxide production and sales in FY2026 and is still ramping up. Production rates are expected to accelerate through January-June 2027 as odour mitigation is put in place, while product qualification with key offtake partners continues. No US offtaker or investor is named. [9]
- The Matawinie mine has been under construction since 2026 (C$67m spent by 30 Sep 2026; mining lease granted Oct 2026), and the 13 ktpy Becancour anode plant is targeting FID in the coming months. Offtakers for about 70% of output are Panasonic Energy, Traxys and the Government of Canada, and the Canada Growth Fund committed about US$82m in Apr 2026; no US buyer or US government finance is named. [10]

**Trade measures, controls and frictions**

- On 10 Mar 2023 the leaders agreed to start negotiating a targeted agreement under which critical minerals extracted or processed in the EU would count toward the Section 30D clean-vehicle credit, building on the EU-US Task Force on the IRA. No agreement was concluded. The 30D credit ended for vehicles acquired after 30 Sep 2025, and the non-binding 24 Apr 2026 MOU (USDE-09) now carries the critical-minerals track. [11]
- Signed 28 Mar 2023 and in force on signature without congressional approval; covers cobalt/graphite/lithium/manganese/nickel; no export duties; consult on non-market measures and forced-labour imports; Treasury/USTR treated it as an FTA for the IRA EV credit critical-minerals test. [12]
- Indonesia has banned nickel ore exports since January 2020 to force domestic smelting, after a 2014 ban was relaxed in 2017. The ban drew a wave of foreign investment into nickel and cobalt processing, and Indonesia produced about 1.6 Mt of the world's 3.3 Mt of nickel in 2022. Cobalt by-product output reached 20,500 t in 2024. The 2026 mining-quota (RKAB) cuts and royalty changes are not covered by this source. [13]
- The regulation sets binding sustainability, safety, labelling, supply-chain due-diligence and end-of-life rules for portable, LMT, EV, industrial and SLI batteries. Due-diligence obligations were postponed from August 2025 to 18 Aug 2027, and the battery passport applies from 18 Feb 2027. The Commission page does not restate these two dates. [14]
- Under the 2024 Section 301 four-year review, tariffs on Chinese lithium-ion EV batteries and battery parts rose from 7.5% to 25% in 2024, on non-EV lithium-ion batteries from 7.5% to 25% in 2026, and on natural graphite and permanent magnets from zero to 25% in 2026; certain other critical minerals went to 25% in 2024. [15]
- A Korean report says the US has restricted exports of black mass and tungsten scrap since 27 Aug 2025, so US-origin recycling feed is unavailable to Korean recyclers. [16]
- The DRC suspended cobalt exports on 22 Feb 2025 and replaced the ban with ARECOMS-allocated quotas from 16 Oct 2025. The quotas were reported as 18,125 t for the rest of 2025 and 96,600 t a year for 2026 and 2027, with part held back as a strategic quota. These volumes come from press reports, not the ARECOMS decision. [17]
- MOFCOM's 9 Oct 2025 package included controls, due to take effect on 8 Nov 2025, on high-energy-density lithium-ion cells, LFP and precursor cathode materials, artificial-graphite anode materials and related equipment and technology. After the Busan meeting China suspended the 9 Oct measures for one year, to about 10 Nov 2026. On 9 Nov 2026 MOFCOM/GAC Announcement 2026 No. 58 kept the battery and graphite-anode controls suspended to 10 Jan 2027 (CCRM-01). [18]
- Section 45X advanced-manufacturing credit remains available to Korean plants but foreign-entity sourcing limits have applied since 1 Jan 2026, pushing Korean makers toward non-Chinese materials. [19]
- P.L. 119-21 (4 Jul 2025) makes components that include material assistance from a prohibited foreign entity ineligible for 45X in tax years beginning after enactment. It also denies 48E to energy storage whose construction begins after 31 Dec 2025 if the storage includes such assistance, and IRS Notice 2026-15 sets interim safe harbours for computing the material-assistance cost ratio. The statutory ratio thresholds rise each year: for qualifying battery components from 60% in 2026 to 85% from 2030, and for storage from 55% to 75%. The notice does not restate these thresholds. [20]
- Commerce found dumping margins of 93.5% (July 2025) and, in February 2026, countervailing duties of 66.8% on Chinese active anode material, about 220% combined. On 12 Mar 2026 the USITC found the imports had not materially retarded the establishment of a US industry, so no AD/CVD orders issue and cash deposits are refunded (Inv. 701-TA-752 and 731-TA-1730). The case had been brought in December 2024 by the American Active Anode Material Producers coalition. [21]

**Industrial and minerals frameworks**

- Under a June 2020 government-to-government MoU on critical and strategic minerals, KABIL and Australia's Critical Minerals Facilitation Office signed a detailed MoU on 10 Mar 2022 for joint due diligence of greenfield and brownfield lithium and cobalt assets in Australia toward joint investment and acquisition. Both sides committed an initial US$6m in a 50:50 ratio, targeted six months for due diligence and investment decisions, and allowed other Indian state companies to join as investors. [22]
- Resources Minister King and Mines Minister Joshi announced around 10 Mar 2023 that the partnership had identified five target projects, two lithium and three cobalt, for detailed due diligence, and agreed to extend its commitments; Australia had committed A$5.8m over three years to the partnership. The project names were not published. [23]
- The three leaders agreed to pilot an early warning system exchanging supply-chain information via embassies in selected countries, with critical minerals and rechargeable batteries as priority products, plus an annual commerce and industry ministers track; the system is not published as operating beyond the pilot. [24]
- Joly and Korea's Trade, Industry and Resources Minister Kim Jung-kwan signed in Ottawa; sets up an industrial cooperation committee and a forum on a Korean auto footprint, battery production, materials processing and critical-minerals recycling; no amounts or companies named. [25]
- The MoC promotes cooperation in battery-related projects and business opportunities to build a trusted, resilient and sustainable battery supply chain; the signing ministries are not named in the list of outcomes. It follows a 2025 JETRO and Government of Japan business matchmaking and roundtable in India on battery and critical-mineral supply chains with over 70 companies and government bodies. [26]

- US-based Lyten says it acquired the Northvolt Ett cell plant (Skellefteå), the Northvolt Labs R&D centre (Västerås) and the Northvolt Dwa BESS plant (Gdańsk) in the year to July 2026, and that it manufactures in Sweden and Poland. Lyten funds site preservation and targeted a definitive sale agreement in Q3 2026. Whether that agreement was signed is not stated in the source. [35]

**Recommended: give Minerals Security Partnership and its successor FORGE a compact mandate for the mineral steps, with a downstream working group**

- **Scope:** Battery Materials & Cells Compact; 14 nodes in 4 steps; no other sectors.

- **KPI dashboard:** per node, the coercive share of member buyers' supply, member buyers' imports from member suppliers, offtake signed (volume), capacity at FID and operating, and public money committed vs private.
- **Review cycle:** every 12 months, with the OSI tables re-run on new trade data.
- **Sunset / exit:** each node carries the exit conditions in Section 7; the compact mandate lapses after five years unless renewed on evidence.

## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not run parallel national subsidy races for battery materials (foil, electrolyte): 3 members (Japan, Malaysia and Taiwan) are already strong suppliers; agree in the compact who expands where.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not create a new compact secretariat or treaty body: Minerals Security Partnership and its successor FORGE already exists (partial finance and projects but no published list); dock the compact in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not put new public money behind battery recycling feed projects that are already committed (evidence CBAT-24): member suppliers already serve member buyers, so compact support is additional only beyond them.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not put new public money behind cathode materials & precursors projects that are already committed (evidence KRCA-17): member suppliers already serve member buyers, so compact support is additional only beyond them.
- *Recommendation. ***N05** Do not put new public money behind lithium chemicals projects that are already committed (evidence UJ-046, KRAU-11, KRAU-12, KRAU-13, KRAU-21, KRCA-22, CBAT-22, CCRM-30): member suppliers already serve member buyers, so compact support is additional only beyond them.
