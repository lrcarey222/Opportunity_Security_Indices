# United States–Korea Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 30 October 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Natural graphite (joint); Battery recycling feed (US->Korea); Accumulator parts & separators (Korea->US). Nuclear Fuel & Reactor Systems: Nuclear extraction (joint). Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Rare-earth oxides & compounds (US->Korea); Rare-earth permanent magnets (joint); AC generators (US->Korea). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Copper smelting & refining (joint). Solar Manufacturing: Polysilicon (US->Korea).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of electric vehicles refining (rare-earth metals, rare-earth oxides & compounds); rare-earth oxides & compounds; accumulator parts & separators in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for rare-earth permanent magnets. Bind market access for wind components (ac generators, ferrite & other magnets, insulators & bushings). Keep uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores); nuclear special materials; transformers open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade US-Korea Strategic Investment MOU and Special Act (US$350bn): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Electric Vehicles components (accumulator parts & separators, electric motors, ferrite & other magnets) capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Batteries assembly (li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators) capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Electric Vehicles assembly (electric & plug-in cars, electric buses, trucks & tractors, electric two-wheelers & works trucks) capacity.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 142 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair, 39 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 3 direct competition and 74 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Korea Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Korea supplies accumulator parts & separators, solar cells, cathode materials & precursors, gallium, germanium & indium where the US is short; the US supplies polysilicon, rare-earth oxides & compounds, battery recycling feed, ac generators where Korea is short; the two share exposure to the same coercive supplier in natural graphite, rare-earth permanent magnets, copper smelting & refining, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Korea brings specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing, coordination across allied chains. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and KEXIM, K-SURE, KOMIR, KDB on the Korea side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

- *Recommendation. *Do not co-subsidise new Electric Vehicles components (accumulator parts & separators, electric motors, ferrite & other magnets) capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 7%, Korea 2%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Korea's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP, U.S. FTA. [1]
- Senior officials meeting in New York agreed to enhance dialogue on critical minerals and cooperation in Southeast Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa through the trilateral Early Warning System. [2]
- Fact sheet states US support for a process toward Korean civil enrichment and reprocessing, subject to the 123 Agreement and US law, and approves nuclear-powered submarines; formal talks reportedly began June 2026 and were still discussing 123 revision in September 2026; no revision signed. [3]
- ASPI lists the MSP, which Korea chairs in 2025, among the frameworks for Australia-Korea minerals cooperation; Australian membership is not stated in that article. [4]
- The US-hosted ministerial in Feb 2026 was attended by Japan and Korea among others, and Korea was reported to chair the FORGE critical-minerals forum through June 2026. [5]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [6]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, US imported US$41.2bn from Korea and Korea imported US$33.1bn from US (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- KORUS has been in force since March 2012; amended in 2018 on autos, light trucks and investment rules without congressional action; the countries still use its consultative mechanisms; exact day of entry into force not stated in the source. [7]
- Westinghouse, KEPCO and KHNP settled their APR1400 intellectual-property dispute on 16 Jan 2025, dismissing all legal actions and setting up future cooperation on projects worldwide; terms are confidential, so market-allocation reports cannot be confirmed. [8]
- ICE detained 475 people at the US$4.3bn Georgia plant in September 2025, many Korean technicians on visa issues; Hyundai said opening slipped two to three months and reports in April 2026 said it was ready to open. [9]
- A January 2026 Section 301 petition targets Korea's treatment of Coupang, on which USTR has not acted; a July 2026 House Judiciary interim report called it discriminatory; the US also criticises the pace of Korean investment; reported January 2026 threat of 25% tariffs not in this source. [10]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- The three leaders agreed to pilot an early warning system exchanging supply-chain information via embassies in selected countries, with critical minerals and rechargeable batteries as priority products, plus an annual commerce and industry ministers track; the system is not published as operating beyond the pilot. [11]
- Sets a 15% US tariff on Korean goods (higher of KORUS, MFN or 15%), 15% Section 232 on autos and parts, no more than 15% on pharma, semiconductors no less favourable than comparable partners; commits US$350bn (US$200bn plus US$150bn shipbuilding) with a US$20bn annual cap; reproduced text, not the whitehouse.gov page. [12]
- Lutnick and Minister Kim signed the MOU on the US$200bn strategic-investment tranche, capped at US$20bn a year; reported profit split is 50:50 until principal and interest are repaid, then 10:90 in the US favour. [13]

**Investment vehicle, project pipeline and financing**

- The US$150bn shipbuilding half of the pledge is to be financed through direct investment, guarantees and loans led by Korean policy-finance institutions; reports say Hanwha Philly Shipyard expansion is delayed because the fund has not taken concrete shape; article date not shown. [14]
- Joint statement of 1 Oct 2026 formalises three projects: Encinal, Texas 6.3 GW gas plant (US$22.3bn), Alaska LNG (over US$50bn expected, subject to commercial reasonableness) and nuclear (up to US$120bn); the annual cap and vehicle structure are not addressed in this article. [15]
- Up to US$120bn for eight US reactors (US$100bn construction, US$20bn contingency), with a US$10bn advance payment for long-lead items targeted by year-end 2026; links to the May 2025 goal of 10 large reactors under construction by 2030; reactor designs not specified in this article. [16]
- Korean companies will pursue a significant minority stake in Westinghouse to share gains from large-reactor projects, with terms to be set in commercial talks; other reports put it at 5 to 10 percent but that figure is not in this source. [17]

**Corporate relationships in the recommended compacts**

- LGES agreed to supply Toyota's North American EVs with about 20 GWh a year of modules from 2025, with roughly 4 trillion won (about 3 billion dollars) invested in Michigan lines dedicated to Toyota. [18]
- SK On signed a conditional offtake with US graphite developer Westwater for battery-grade natural graphite in North America. [19]
- Multi-metal smelter project of about US$7.4bn through Crucible JV, with the Department of War reported as largest shareholder at 40% (US$760m in one account, US$1.4bn conditional in another) and a US$210m Commerce grant. [20]
- The 2023 Honda-LGES joint venture L-H Battery keeps operating its Ohio cell plant under a lease after LGES sold the building to Honda for about 4.22 trillion won (2.85 billion dollars) announced Dec 2025; a later report of a lower closing value of 3.74 trillion won is unconfirmed. [21]
- LGES agreed to sell the Ohio JV factory building and related assets to Honda for about $2.85B with the JV continuing via lease and closing targeted for 2026-02-28. [22]
- A US$200m JV, run by POSCO International as largest shareholder with ReElement refining technology, plans US NdPr-Dy-Tb oxides then magnets and motor cores, following a September 2025 MOU; no MP Materials link found. [23]
- Qcells began cell manufacturing at Cartersville, Georgia; by Q3 2026 it expects 3.3 GW each of ingot, wafer and cell and 3.5 GW of modules there, plus 5.1 GW modules at Dalton; ingot and wafer status not stated; investment not disclosed; a secondary report cites US$675m of 2026 AMPC. [24]
- Final investment decision in May 2026 after an Australian offtake commitment under the Strategic Reserve; construction began 8 Oct 2026; credit agencies from the US, Canada, Korea and Germany support it; the article's A$1.2bn figure is ambiguous. [25]
- LGES began mass production of LFP storage cells by repurposing part of its Holland, Michigan EV plant, targeting over 30 GWh by end-2026; a US$1.6bn LFP supply deal with DTE Energy is reported; publication date approximate. [26]
- Samsung SDI secured a KRW 1.5tn (about US$1bn) US ESS cell supply deal, starting with NCA before LFP; its Kokomo, Indiana line was reported starting ESS output in 2026; the StarPlus (Stellantis) and BlueOval SK joint ventures were not covered; date approximate. [27]
- Second Montgomery, Alabama plant (312,000 sq ft, about US$200m) due for completion in April and to lift extra-high-voltage transformer capacity about 50%, up to 765 kV; completion year inferred; other outlets give US$273.7m across Ulsan and Alabama. [28]
- Hyosung is investing about US$300m in a three-phase expansion of its ultra-high-voltage transformer plant in Memphis, Tennessee, and formed a JV with Quanta Services in June 2026 for extra-high-voltage circuit breakers; completion dates not given. [29]
- LS Electric plans US$240m of US investment by 2030, expanding its Utah switchgear plant and developing a Bastrop, Texas campus. [30]

- Unverified items are left out of this section; they stay in the evidence register (evidence_register.csv) and the evidence layer. [1]

- *Inference. *Working channels today: US-Korea Strategic Investment MOU and Special Act (US$350bn) (a project pipeline and finance); Minerals Security Partnership and FORGE (finance). ## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not co-subsidise new Electric Vehicles components (accumulator parts & separators, electric motors, ferrite & other magnets) capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 7%, Korea 2%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not co-subsidise new Batteries assembly (li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators) capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 5%, Korea 5%). More trusted capacity is needed, so agree who specialises in what rather than racing.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not co-subsidise new Electric Vehicles assembly (electric & plug-in cars, electric buses, trucks & tractors, electric two-wheelers & works trucks) capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 8%, Korea 4%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution: USKR-M2 already exists (finance and a project pipeline); dock the compacts in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N05** Do not put new public money behind Electric Vehicles extraction (cobalt ores, lithium minerals, natural graphite) investment that is already committed (evidence JPKR-11; USAU-09; USKR-29); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N06** Do not put new public money behind Solar cells investment that is already committed (evidence USKR-21); support is additional only beyond it.
