# United States–Korea Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Natural graphite (joint). Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Rare-earth oxides & compounds (joint); AC generators † (US->Korea). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Copper smelting & refining (joint).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of oil extraction; cathode materials & precursors; electric buses, trucks & tractors in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for rare-earth oxides & compounds. Keep market access open and predictable for solar cells, gas refining (no new tariff on the trusted supplier). Keep nuclear special materials; transformers; overhead conductors open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade US-Korea Strategic Investment MOU and Special Act (US$350bn): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution. Do not put new public money behind Battery recycling feed investment that is already committed (evidence CBAT-24). Do not put new public money behind Solar cells investment that is already committed (evidence USKR-21).

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 117 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair (not counting 25 cells whose products are read node by node), 23 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 0 direct competition and 69 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Korea Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Korea supplies solar cells, accumulator parts & separators, gallium, germanium & indium where the US is short; the US supplies AC generators †, battery recycling feed, electric buses, trucks & tractors, refrigerants where Korea is short; the two share exposure to coercive suppliers in rare-earth oxides & compounds, natural graphite, copper smelting & refining, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity; and they specialise at different steps of the same chains in cathode materials & precursors, sensors & controller parts, non-oriented electrical steel (NGO), automatic controllers.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Korea brings specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing, coordination across allied chains. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and KEXIM, K-SURE, KOMIR, KDB on the Korea side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Korea's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP, U.S. FTA. [1]
- A joint statement in New York on 23 Sep 2024 set up a network of MSP export-credit agencies, development-finance institutions and state-backed funds. Its purpose is to share information, coordinate and co-finance critical-minerals projects. Named members include DFC, Export Development Canada, JICA, JBIC, KEXIM and Export Finance Norway among others. [2]
- Senior officials meeting in New York agreed to enhance dialogue on critical minerals and cooperation in Southeast Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa through the trilateral Early Warning System. [3]
- The 13 Nov 2025 joint fact sheet states US support for the process that will lead to Korean civil uranium enrichment and spent-fuel reprocessing for peaceful uses, within the 123 Agreement and US law, and US approval for Korea to build nuclear-powered attack submarines. No revised 123 Agreement has been announced, and the start date of formal talks (reported as June 2026) is not confirmed in a primary source. [4]
- ASPI lists the MSP, which Korea chairs in 2025, among the frameworks for Australia-Korea minerals cooperation; Australian membership is not stated in that article. [5]
- The US-hosted ministerial in Feb 2026 was attended by Japan and Korea among others, and Korea was reported to chair the FORGE critical-minerals forum through June 2026. [6]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [7]
- At the 19 Feb 2026 Ministerial, IEA ministers endorsed an expanded phase of the Critical Minerals Security Programme, which was established in 2022. The expansion covers: support for members that set up or expand stockpiles; rapid market assessments and coordinated responses during supply disruptions; regular table-top exercises (a rare-earth exercise was held in Nov 2025); a wider information dashboard; a new Technology Collaboration Programme on recycling and substitution. The release gives no budget. [8]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [9]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, US imported US$41.2bn from Korea and Korea imported US$33.1bn from US (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- KORUS has been in force since March 2012; amended in 2018 on autos, light trucks and investment rules without congressional action; the countries still use its consultative mechanisms; exact day of entry into force not stated in the source. [10]
- Westinghouse, KEPCO and KHNP settled their APR1400 intellectual-property dispute on 16 Jan 2025, dismissing all legal actions and setting up future cooperation on projects worldwide; terms are confidential, so market-allocation reports cannot be confirmed. [11]
- ICE detained 475 people at the US$4.3bn Georgia plant in September 2025, many Korean technicians on visa issues; Hyundai said opening slipped two to three months and reports in April 2026 said it was ready to open. [12]
- Hanwha cells were reportedly detained in Aug 2025, and Qcells had resumed normal production at its Georgia module plants by 9 Mar 2026. CBP detained modules from Vietnam-based VSUN in Apr 2026, which could cut about US$30m from the company's earnings. CBP denied Maxeon's protests in Apr 2025; Maxeon's H1 2025 shipments fell to 153.2 MW (15% of H1 2024), and it applied for judicial management in Singapore in Apr 2026. [13]
- A January 2026 Section 301 petition targets Korea's treatment of Coupang, on which USTR has not acted; a July 2026 House Judiciary interim report called it discriminatory; the US also criticises the pace of Korean investment; reported January 2026 threat of 25% tariffs not in this source. [14]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- The three leaders agreed to pilot an early warning system exchanging supply-chain information via embassies in selected countries, with critical minerals and rechargeable batteries as priority products, plus an annual commerce and industry ministers track; the system is not published as operating beyond the pilot. [15]
- Fortum signed an Early Works Agreement with Westinghouse, teamed with Hyundai E&C, on 23 Jun 2025 after a two-year feasibility study. It covers early planning, site evaluation and licensing work for a possible AP1000. No investment decision or site is stated. [16]
- The joint fact sheet of 13 Nov 2025 sets the US reciprocal tariff on Korean goods at the higher of the KORUS/MFN rate or 15%. It caps Section 232 tariffs on Korean autos, auto parts, timber and lumber at 15%, keeps pharmaceuticals at or below 15%, and promises semiconductor terms no less favourable than any future deal covering a comparable trade volume. It records US$150bn of shipbuilding investment and US$200bn under the MOU, and Korea does not have to fund more than US$20bn a year. [17]
- At the 4 Feb 2026 ministerial (54 countries and the European Commission), the US signed 11 bilateral critical-minerals frameworks or MOUs: Argentina, Cook Islands, Ecuador, Guinea, Morocco, Paraguay, Peru, the Philippines, the UAE, the UK and Uzbekistan. These add to 10 signed in the previous five months, with negotiations concluded with 17 more countries; the frameworks cover pricing, development, supply gaps and access to finance. FORGE, chaired by Korea through June 2026, replaced the Minerals Security Partnership. [18]
- MOTIR and the US Commerce Department signed the KUSPI MOU on 8 May 2026 and opened the Korea-US Shipbuilding Partnership Center in Washington on 23 Jul 2026, with Minister Kim and Secretary Lutnick attending. The center is meant to link governments, shipyards, research institutes and investors, train US shipbuilding workers, advise US yards on productivity and support joint R&D; 15 company and institution MOUs were signed at the opening. [19]
- The Korea-US Strategic Investment MOU, signed 14 Nov 2025, covers US$200bn capped at US$20bn a year. MOTIR's 1 Oct 2026 release says both caps are written into a legally binding SPV operating agreement and all projects run through one umbrella I-SPV with wholly owned project SPVs. Profits split 50:50 until Korea recovers all principal and deemed interest (20-year US Treasury yield plus a project spread), then 10:90. The source does not name the signatories. [20]

**Investment vehicle, project pipeline and financing**

- On 25 Jun 2026 the Korea-US Strategic Investment Corporation (KUIC), KEXIM, KDB, K-SURE and KOBC signed an MoU with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean to implement the US$150bn shipbuilding investment under the Nov 2025 MOU. It sets up a consultative body, with KEXIM as secretariat, to identify projects and coordinate policy finance. No disbursed amount or project list is stated. [21]
- On 1 Oct 2026 (30 Sep in Washington) the two governments announced three projects under the investment MOU. Project Star is a US$22.3bn, 6,472 MW gas-fired plant for AI data centres in Encinal, Texas, led by Related Companies and NextEra Energy; it is cleared through Korea's domestic procedures as project no. 1, with phase one operating in 2029 and full output in 2032. Project Power is the nuclear framework (USKR-08). Project North, Alaska LNG, is only under joint review, with the US offering tariff cuts on project materials such as steel, a long-term offtake and priority access for Korea. [22]
- The Korea-US Nuclear Power Framework, to be signed by both governments, Westinghouse, KEPCO and KHNP, allots up to US$120bn of strategic-investment funds to eight large US reactors (two APR1400, six AP1000) built in three stages: US$100bn overnight cost plus US$20bn contingency, at most US$30bn per pair. Up to US$10bn may be advanced by end-2026 for long-lead items, subject to commercial review and National Assembly reporting; sites, structures and schedules are not yet set. [23]
- MOTIR's 1 Oct 2026 release says Korean companies will acquire a stake in Westinghouse so that commercial gains from AP1000 projects are shared, with terms to be settled in company negotiations. On 16 Sep 2026 MOTIR said no details had been decided, responding to a press report of a roughly 15% stake and a board seat. [24]

- The tariff measures in force or in dispute between the two, the baseline any binding of the nodes above works from:
- Korean autos and parts face a 15% Section 232 rate (combined with MFN) under the November 2025 deal, applied retroactively to 1 Nov 2025 for autos per implementing guidance; Section 232 is unaffected by the IEEPA ruling. [46]
- The 14 Jan 2026 Section 232 proclamation puts a 25% tariff, from 15 Jan 2026, on a narrow set of advanced computing chips such as the NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X. Imports for US data centres, R&D, start-ups, consumer, civil-industrial and public-sector uses are exempt, and broader semiconductor tariffs are deferred pending negotiations. There is no Korea-specific clause, so Korea's treatment rests on the Nov 2025 promise of terms no less favourable than comparable partners. [47]
- After the Supreme Court ruling the President lifted the EO 14257 tariffs, including Korea's 15% country rate, on 20 Feb 2026 and imposed a temporary 10% global tariff under Section 122, which expired in July 2026; refund treatment not covered. [48]
- On 23 Jul 2026 the US imposed a 12.5% tariff on most Korean goods after a Section 301 forced-labor investigation; a separate excess-capacity Section 301 probe could add tariffs and Seoul wants assurance the total stays within the agreed 15%. [49]
- The proclamation of 6 Aug 2026 sets minimum import prices of US$21/kg for polysilicon, US$100/kg for ingots and wafers, US$0.22/W for cells and US$0.38/W for modules, with a specific tariff covering any shortfall. It also adds a 15% ad valorem duty on ingots and other derivatives. Both measures apply from 4 Dec 2026.
