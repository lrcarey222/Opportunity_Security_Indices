# United States–India Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-13); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 30 October 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Li-ion cells & packs (joint); Natural graphite (joint). Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Rare-earth permanent magnets (joint); Ferrite & other magnets (joint). Wind & Hydropower Equipment: Wind blades & hubs (India->US). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Copper smelting & refining (joint).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of reactor parts; electrolysers; sensors & controller parts in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for li-ion cells & packs and rare-earth permanent magnets and ferrite & other magnets. Bind market access for wind blades & hubs. Keep uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores); transformers; blade core wood (balsa) open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade DFC operations in India under the 2022 Investment Incentive Agreement: sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution. Do not defend AC generators as a bilateral priority. Do not defend Automatic controllers as a bilateral priority.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 142 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair, 24 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 5 direct competition and 86 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); India Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Development & Scale-Up Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *India supplies wind blades & hubs, nuclear special materials where the US is short; the US supplies reactor parts, electrolysers, sensors & controller parts, electric furnaces & heating where India is short; the two share exposure to the same coercive supplier in li-ion cells & packs, natural graphite, copper smelting & refining, rare-earth permanent magnets, ferrite & other magnets, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; India brings coordination across allied chains, sites for development and scale-up, specialised technology and manufacturing. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and India Exim Bank on the India side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-13); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- India's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP. [1]
- The two National Security Advisors held the inaugural iCET meeting on 31 Jan 2023, covering semiconductor supply chains, AI, quantum, space and defence innovation, and named rare-earth processing and advanced materials for future cooperation. The February 2025 leaders' statement carries this agenda forward under TRUST and does not mention iCET. [2]
- Quad leaders committed to identify and address gaps in manufacturing capacity for clean-energy technologies, focusing on solar PV, hydrogen electrolysers and EV batteries. They adopted joint principles for clean-energy supply chains and planned a public-private Quad Clean Energy Supply Chain Conference. [3]
- Australia was to open applications in Nov 2024 for the AUD 50m (about US$34m at Sep 2024 rates) programme supporting solar-panel, electrolyser and battery supply-chain projects in the Indo-Pacific. India committed US$2m to solar projects in Fiji, Comoros, Madagascar and Seychelles, and Japan US$122m to renewable projects. Awards and 2025-26 activity are not stated. [4]
- Leaders re-committed to the Energy Security Partnership covering oil, gas and civil nuclear, aimed to make the US a leading supplier of crude, petroleum products and LNG to India, backed India's full IEA membership and agreed to expand strategic oil-reserve arrangements. The statement does not mention the earlier Strategic Clean Energy Partnership. [5]
- Both countries are MSP members (India joined in June 2023) and committed in February 2025 to promote investment along the critical-mineral value chain through it. The MSP was succeeded by FORGE at the February 2026 Critical Minerals Ministerial. [6]
- India joined the US-led Pax Silica initiative on AI, semiconductor and critical-mineral supply chains on 20 Feb 2026, after being left out of the December 2025 launch group; the initiative now has about 27 signatories including the EU. [7]
- Quad foreign ministers adopted a framework to mobilise up to $20bn for secure critical-mineral supply chains; a separate July 2025 Quad initiative is mentioned only in passing and not sourced. [8]
- Launched July 2025 and framework adopted May 2026 to mobilise up to US$20bn of public and private support for mining, processing and recycling; no project list. [9]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [10]
- Secretary Rubio's second India visit of 2026 focused on energy cooperation, commercial investment and economic ties: he met Jaishankar in Mumbai, toured Reliance's 1.4m b/d Jamnagar refinery and urged more refining of Venezuelan crude. No purchase commitments were announced. [11]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, US imported US$15.4bn from India and India imported US$13.6bn from US (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- Leaders set Mission 500 to more than double bilateral trade to US$500bn by 2030 and planned to negotiate the first tranche of a multi-sector BTA by fall 2025. That deadline passed without a signed tranche. [12]
- USTR Greer travelled to New Delhi in June 2026 to discuss the Joint Statement and the Interim Agreement. By late October 2026 no deal had been concluded; India's foreign ministry said both sides reaffirmed their commitment to concluding one, and a former Indian ambassador said India was waiting for the US to formalise its tariff positions. [13]
- In February 2026 Commerce set preliminary countervailing duties on solar cells and panels from India, Indonesia and Laos following a 2025 petition by US manufacturers. Company rates, the anti-dumping preliminaries and the final Commerce and ITC determinations are not stated in the source. [14]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- Leaders launched the COMPACT (Catalyzing Opportunities for Military Partnership, Accelerated Commerce & Technology) as the umbrella for defence, trade, energy and technology outcomes, and welcomed about US$7.35bn of ongoing Indian investment in the US, including Novelis aluminium in Alabama and Kentucky, JSW steel in Texas and Ohio and Epsilon Advanced Materials battery materials in North Carolina. [15]
- TRUST (Transforming the Relationship Utilizing Strategic Technology) covers AI, semiconductors, quantum, biotech, energy and space and commits to trusted supply chains for semiconductors and critical minerals. Under it the leaders launched the Strategic Mineral Recovery initiative to recover and process lithium, cobalt and rare earths from aluminium, coal-mining and oil-and-gas streams; no budget or project list is given. [16]
- India will eliminate or reduce tariffs on all US industrial goods and many farm goods. The US set an 18% reciprocal rate and, once the Interim Agreement is concluded, will remove it on generic pharmaceuticals, gems and aircraft parts, lift Section 232 tariffs on certain aircraft parts and give India a preferential quota for auto parts. Both sides agreed to align on economic security, including inbound and outbound investment reviews and export controls, and either side may modify its commitments if the other's tariffs change. [17]
- India intends to purchase US$500bn of US energy products, aircraft and parts, precious metals, technology products and coking coal over five years, and trade in GPUs and other data-centre goods is to rise significantly. No annual schedule or enforcement mechanism is stated. [18]
