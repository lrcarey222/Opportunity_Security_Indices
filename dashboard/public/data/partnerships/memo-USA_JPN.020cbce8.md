# United States–Japan Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Rare-earth oxides & compounds (joint); Rare-earth permanent magnets (Japan->US). Semiconductors & Power Electronics: Piezo-electric quartz (Japan->US). Battery Materials & Cells: Natural graphite (joint). Wind & Hydropower Equipment: Wind turbine generating sets (US->Japan).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of wind turbine generating sets; rare-earth permanent magnets; piezo-electric quartz in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for rare-earth oxides & compounds. Keep market access open and predictable for battery materials (foil, electrolyte), manganese dioxide (EMD) † (no new tariff on the trusted supplier). Keep PGMs (iridium, ruthenium); nuclear special materials; transformers open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade Japan-US Strategic Investment Initiative (MOU of 4 Sep 2025): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Automatic controllers capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Gas assembly capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Gas components capacity.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 117 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair (not counting 25 cells whose products are read node by node), 22 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 5 direct competition and 67 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Japan Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Japan supplies rare-earth permanent magnets, piezo-electric quartz, battery materials (foil, electrolyte), manganese dioxide (EMD) † where the US is short; the US supplies wind turbine generating sets, refrigerants, sensors & controller parts where Japan is short; the two share exposure to coercive suppliers in rare-earth oxides & compounds, natural graphite, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity; and they specialise at different steps of the same chains in wafers †, battery recycling feed, AC generators †.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Japan brings capital, specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and JBIC, NEXI, JOGMEC on the Japan side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

**Existing mechanisms and the MEA question** (Section 8):

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Japan's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: G7, IPEF, MSP. [1]
- Quad leaders committed to identify and address gaps in manufacturing capacity for clean-energy technologies, focusing on solar PV, hydrogen electrolysers and EV batteries. They adopted joint principles for clean-energy supply chains and planned a public-private Quad Clean Energy Supply Chain Conference. [2]
- At Wilmington on 21 Sep 2024 the Quad announced an initiative to boost energy efficiency, including deployment and manufacturing of affordable high-efficiency cooling systems to cut grid strain, with an initial US$1.25m of US technical-assistance financing. The 2025-26 Quad statements do not report on it. [3]
- Quad leaders pledged on 21 Sep 2024 to align policies, incentives, standards and investment for diversified clean-energy supply chains, to use policy and public finance to catalyse private investment in allied supply chains, and to focus near-term efforts on mineral production, recycling and battery manufacturing, citing complementary capabilities across the battery supply chain. No battery-specific programme, finance or targets followed in the 2025-26 Quad statements. [4]
- The Wilmington fact sheet (21 Sep 2024) says Quad development finance institutions and agencies decided to meet to explore investments in the Indo-Pacific including clean energy and quality infrastructure, building on a 2022 meeting of the heads of Export Finance Australia, AIFFP, India EXIM, JBIC and DFC. No later meeting or joint transaction is reported in the 2025-26 Quad statements. [5]
- A joint statement in New York on 23 Sep 2024 set up a network of MSP export-credit agencies, development-finance institutions and state-backed funds. Its purpose is to share information, coordinate and co-finance critical-minerals projects. Named members include DFC, Export Development Canada, JICA, JBIC, KEXIM and Export Finance Norway among others. [6]
- Both sides in 2024 said the 2022-launched Economic 2+2 works as an effective economic-security framework and agreed to hold it periodically; no 2026 meeting found (Feb 2026 Rubio-Motegi and Ichikawa-Rubio contacts only). [7]
- G7 leaders adopted the plan at Kananaskis on 17 Jun 2025, under Canada's presidency. It tasks ministers with a roadmap for standards-based markets with traceability by year-end, calls on export credit agencies, DFIs and MDBs to scale up mining finance, and targets processing, recycling and substitution R&D. Australia, India and Korea later endorsed it. [8]
- Quad foreign ministers met in Washington on 1 Jul 2025, voiced concern over the abrupt constriction of critical-mineral supply chains and reliance on any one country for processing and refining, launched the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative and planned to launch a Quad Ports of the Future Partnership in Mumbai in Oct 2025. They looked forward to a Leaders' Summit hosted by India later in 2025 and the next foreign ministers' meeting hosted by Australia in 2026; neither took place as planned. [9]
- Quad foreign ministers launched the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative in Washington on 1 Jul 2025 to secure and diversify critical-mineral supply chains, with priorities including e-waste critical-mineral recovery and private-sector investment. It was given a framework on 26 May 2026 with an intent to mobilise up to US$20bn for mining, processing and recycling; no project list has been published. [10]
- Senior officials meeting in New York agreed to enhance dialogue on critical minerals and cooperation in Southeast Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa through the trilateral Early Warning System. [11]
- Australia's AUD 50m programme (about US$34m at Sep 2024 rates; FY25-FY28) opened Round 1 with up to A$25m for joint Australian and Indo-Pacific R&D and feasibility projects (grants of A$0.1m-2.5m covering up to 95% of costs). At Wilmington (Sep 2024) India also committed US$2m to solar projects in Fiji, Comoros, Madagascar and Seychelles and Japan US$122m to renewable projects; delivery of those pledges is not stated. [12]
- India hosted the first Quad Ports of the Future conference in Mumbai on 29-30 Oct 2025 during India Maritime Week, with about 120 delegates from 24 Indo-Pacific countries, formally launching the partnership to mobilise government and private investment in quality port infrastructure. No funding commitments were reported from the conference. [13]
- Announced in Toronto on 31 Oct 2025, this is the first round of the Production Alliance under the June 2025 G7 Critical Minerals Action Plan. Its 26 investments, partnerships and measures with France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, Norway, the US, Australia and Ukraine aim to unlock C$6.4bn (about US$4.6bn at 0.71 USD/CAD) of graphite, rare-earth and scandium projects. They include Government of Canada offtakes with Nouveau Monde Graphite and Rio Tinto (scandium) and up to C$20.2m for joint R&D. Canada also released a roadmap for standards-based critical-minerals markets. [14]
- Adopted in Toronto on 31 Oct 2025 as an early action under the Action Plan, it aims to reduce and eventually eliminate dependence on supply chains dominated by non-market practices. It prioritises interoperable traceability systems aligned with the UN Transparency Protocol and lists price floors, procurement, tax and trade measures as tools members may use, with no deadlines. [15]
- Carney launched the Alliance at Kananaskis to use strategic investments and offtakes to bring critical-mineral projects into production for defence and advanced technology. At the 30-31 Oct 2025 Toronto ministerial, ministers agreed their critical-minerals envoys would advance the work, and Canada announced investments including offtakes with two Canadian projects. Member list, deal count, values and the US role are not stated in either source. [16]
- The US-hosted ministerial in Feb 2026 was attended by Japan and Korea among others, and Korea was reported to chair the FORGE critical-minerals forum through June 2026. [17]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [18]
- At the 19 Feb 2026 Ministerial, IEA ministers endorsed an expanded phase of the Critical Minerals Security Programme, which was established in 2022. The expansion covers: support for members that set up or expand stockpiles; rapid market assessments and coordinated responses during supply disruptions; regular table-top exercises (a rare-earth exercise was held in Nov 2025); a wider information dashboard; a new Technology Collaboration Programme on recycling and substitution. The release gives no budget. [19]
- India has held the Quad chair since 2024 and was due to host the Leaders' Summit in 2025, but none was held in 2025, after US tariffs on Indian goods rose to 50% and the New York Times reported (30 Aug 2025) that Trump no longer planned to travel to India for it. ThePrint reported on 25 May 2026 that India would pass the chair to Australia without hosting a summit and that future summits are likely to be held on the sidelines of multilateral meetings. [20]
- Quad foreign ministers adopted the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework in New Delhi on 26 May 2026, under which the four intend to mobilise up to US$20bn in government and private-sector support through new and existing efforts for mining, processing and recycling, by identifying projects with a Quad nexus (located in, operated by companies headquartered in, or supplying Quad partners) and backing them through ECAs, DFIs, guarantees, loans, equity, insurance, subsidies and offtake. No project list or split by partner has been published; the only new money named by 31 Oct 2026 was a US$10m US commitment for critical-mineral recovery from waste. [21]
- India hosted the 11th Quad foreign ministers' meeting in New Delhi on 26 May 2026, where ministers voiced grave concern over economic coercion and non-market practices including arbitrary export restrictions, price manipulation and disruptions in critical minerals, announced the Quad Critical Minerals Framework and a Quad Initiative on Indo-Pacific Energy Security, and welcomed Pax Silica as a pillar of their economic-security agenda. The statement set no date for a Leaders' Summit. [22]
- The four committed on 26 May 2026 to stable and open energy markets, diversified supply chains for energy products and fertilisers, open energy trade and free navigation including through the Strait of Hormuz, and to identify cooperation in technology, policy, market analysis and emergency-response exercises through an engagement plan, including strengthening their strategic petroleum systems. The July 2026 report sets out policy options and describes the framework's goal of mobilising up to US$20bn as an objective to mobilise government and private capital. [24]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [25]
- USTR is negotiating a binding plurilateral ATCM. Japan, Mexico and the EU are the first partners, building on action plans of 4 Feb, 19 Mar and 24 Apr 2026, and the design envisages phased-in, mineral-specific, border-adjusted price floors with common standards. As of 28 Sep 2026 no text was public, a reported initial scope of 5-10 minerals was unconfirmed and US implementing legislation would be needed. - Quad foreign ministers met in Atlanta on 31 Oct 2026 on the sidelines of the G20 foreign ministers' meeting, expressed grave concern over economic coercion through arbitrary export restrictions and non-market policies that cause overcapacity in critical industries, and announced the first Quad Business Leaders' Forum to promote commercial deals in key sectors. They reported progress on the critical-minerals framework, fuel security, Fiji ports, AI and undersea cables. [27]
- The Quad convened its first Fuel Security Forum in September 2026 and discussed advancing fuel security in the Indo-Pacific, including by strengthening the four countries' strategic petroleum reserves and stockholdings; press reports place it at the G20 energy meetings in Houston, which the official statement does not state. [28]
- Quad ministers said on 31 Oct 2026 they will launch a new effort to identify semiconductor and AI-hardware supply-chain chokepoints before they cause disruptions, as part of building trusted supply chains for advanced and leading-edge chips. No lead agency, funding or timeline is stated. [29]
- Trump, Modi, Takaichi and Albanese met in Manila on 17 Nov 2026 on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit, the first Quad leaders' meeting since Sep 2024; their joint press release voiced grave concern over economic coercion including arbitrary export restrictions, committed to securing critical supply chains, announced the first Quad Business Leaders' Forum for early 2027 and endorsed the Quad AI Accelerator. - Greer announced the Action Plan on 19 Mar 2026: the two countries will develop strategic trade policies and border mechanisms to protect downstream industries and lay the foundation for a binding plurilateral agreement supported by price floors; trade press adds deep-sea R&D (not read from primary). [49]
- Under the framework's second pillar the four aim to share permitting and licensing practices to shorten approval timelines, develop or strengthen tools to review critical-mineral transactions that threaten national security, cooperate on geological mapping and resource assessment, and consider coordinated measures against non-market policies such as high-standards marketplaces and price mechanisms. These are stated intentions; no joint price mechanism or marketplace has been announced. [50]
- The framework commits the four to improve recovery of critical minerals from e-waste and other scrap, encourage investment in recycling technologies, processes and collection networks in Quad countries with the private sector, promote recovery innovation, and explore streamlining export and import procedures for relevant waste and scrap among the partners. **Where the findings are weak or counterintuitive**

- Joint bets resting on a node need inherited from its cell (a proxy): Rare-earth oxides & compounds; Piezo-electric quartz; Natural graphite; Wind turbine generating sets.
- Evidence-led compacts (OSI cannot see the partner's capability in trade data): Nuclear Fuel & Reactor Systems; Grid Equipment & Conductors.
- Japan has no listed trade position (under 1% of world supply) at nodes where the US has material demand: solar cells, PV modules & generators, uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores), uranium conversion & enrichment, nuclear reactors & enrichment machinery, lithium chemicals, PV cover glass, wind towers, heat pumps & AC units, solar thermal water heaters, electricity meters, transformers, hydro turbine parts, blade core wood (balsa), overhead conductors, aluminium wire rod, direct-reduced iron (DRI/HBI). Trade data miss capability that is owned abroad or sold at home, so these nodes cannot show complementarity even where corporate evidence suggests it.
- Product-definition caveats (†) at 5 nodes: the notes are under the Section 2 node table.
- OSI reads hydrogen-ammonia-and-methanol as low strategic case, but the evidence layer records a capacity problem the annual trade data cannot see: METI's first international awards under the JPY3tn (about US$19.2bn) 15-year hydrogen price-gap CfD, executed through JOGMEC, went in Jan 2026 to JERA and Mitsui for about 772,000 t/yr of blue ammonia from the Blue Point plant in Louisiana from 2030-31 (Wood Mackenzie estimates about US$6.8bn). No Australian hydrogen or ammonia supply project is among the awards reported to date, and about 65% of the funding pool remains. Treat it as an emerging bottleneck to re-test, not a settled open-market node. [99]
- Low-evidence units among the active states: none.
- Trade data understate concentration at uranium conversion & enrichment: enrichment is sold as a service under long-term contracts, so goods trade understates Russia's share of world enrichment capacity (roughly 40%), and the 2024 US ban on Russian low-enriched uranium imports makes trusted capacity the binding constraint.
- Trade data understate concentration at gallium, germanium & indium: China's export controls since 2023 cut its recorded exports, so 2024 trade understates its share of primary gallium and germanium output (the large majority of world supply).
- Fuel-trade lanes reach tier 1 (oil extraction; buyer: Japan): real energy-security lanes, but outside the electro-industrial compacts and with no market failure beyond the supply concentration the engine measures.
