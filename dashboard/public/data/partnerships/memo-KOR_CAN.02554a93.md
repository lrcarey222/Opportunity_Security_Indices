# Korea–Canada Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 30 October 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Electric Vehicles refining (joint). Nuclear Fuel & Reactor Systems: Uranium conversion & enrichment (Canada->Korea). Battery Materials & Cells: Accumulator parts & separators (Korea->Canada). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Solar refining (joint).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of batteries extraction (cobalt ores, lithium minerals, natural graphite); accumulator parts & separators; oil extraction in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for electric vehicles refining (rare-earth metals, rare-earth oxides & compounds). Keep nuclear special materials; battery recycling feed; semiconductor manufacturing equipment open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade Critical-minerals and energy-security MOU (2023) and 2026 stockpiling track: sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Batteries assembly (li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators) capacity. Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution. Do not put new public money behind Cathode materials & precursors investment that is already committed (evidence KRCA-17.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 142 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair, 22 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 1 direct competition and 94 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: Korea Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary); Canada Resource & Processing Anchor (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary), Demand & Market Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Canada supplies uranium conversion & enrichment, nickel intermediates & chemicals, aluminium wire rod where Korea is short; Korea supplies accumulator parts & separators, cathode materials & precursors, non-oriented electrical steel (ngo), power cables where Canada is short; they share no node-level chokepoint where neither supplies.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, Korea brings specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing, coordination across allied chains; Canada brings resources and processing, capital, demand and offtake. Public finance runs through KEXIM, K-SURE, KOMIR, KDB on the Korea side and ISED, EDC, CIB, CGF, CMIF, FinDev on the Canada side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Canada's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: G7, MSP, NATO, U.S. FTA. [1]
- Economic-security 2+2 (Joly, Champagne, Park Jin, Ahn Dukgeun) launched Sep 2022 and first met in Seoul on 16 May 2023; priorities were critical minerals, clean energy, supply chains, AI and economic coercion. [2]
- Second 2+2 (first was Nov 2024) agreed to pursue an update of the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Action Plan, start Defence Cooperation Agreement steps and coordinate on economic security; the statement does not mention critical minerals or energy; next meeting in Korea in 2028. [3]
- On 2 Jun 2026 Canada and Korea agreed to develop a joint critical-minerals stockpiling plan by end-2026, discussed strategic-resource investments, and Korea agreed to facilitate CKFTA preferential tariffs for Canadian crude; no volumes or amounts given. [4]
- Lee and Carney agreed on 16 Jun 2026 to strengthen cooperation in defence, security, energy and critical minerals; an informal meeting followed at the NATO summit; no deliverables or amounts reported. [5]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, Korea imported US$3.7bn from Canada and Canada imported US$3.4bn from Korea (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- Signed in Ottawa 22 Sep 2014 and in force 1 Jan 2015; Canada's first FTA in Asia-Pacific, covering goods, services, investment and procurement; nearly 90% of Canadian exports to Korea duty-free at entry into force. [6]
- From 26 Dec 2025 Canada cut FTA-partner steel quotas to 75% of 2024 volumes with a 50% over-quota surtax; Korea protested that this breaches the CKFTA and sought an exemption, none found. [7]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- Non-binding MOU (2023, not 2022) covering critical-mineral value chains, clean fuels and CCS, and clean-energy, ZEV and semiconductor manufacturing, with an action plan and investment-screening cooperation; it lets Korea count Canada toward the US EV-credit minerals test. [9]
- Carney and Lee welcomed the partnership at the Gyeongju APEC summit on 30 Oct 2025, Canada's first in the Indo-Pacific, and finalised a classified-information agreement that supports defence procurement; the readout does not mention energy, critical minerals or LNG. [10]
- Joly and Korea's Trade, Industry and Resources Minister Kim Jung-kwan signed in Ottawa; sets up an industrial cooperation committee and a forum on a Korean auto footprint, battery production, materials processing and critical-minerals recycling; no amounts or companies named. [11]
- The Geological Survey of Canada and Korea's KIGAM signed an implementation agreement on naturally occurring hydrogen research and development, announced with the 2 Jun 2026 energy and critical-minerals meeting. [12]

## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not co-subsidise new Batteries assembly (li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators) capacity: both are strong suppliers (Korea 5%, Canada 1%). More trusted capacity is needed, so agree who specialises in what rather than racing.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution: KRCA-M2 already exists (a standing framework with no pipeline or finance yet); dock the compacts in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not put new public money behind Cathode materials & precursors investment that is already committed (evidence KRCA-17; KRCA-18); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not put new public money behind Electric Vehicles extraction (cobalt ores, lithium minerals, natural graphite) investment that is already committed (evidence USAU-09); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N05** Do not put new public money behind Nickel intermediates & chemicals investment that is already committed (evidence KRCA-23); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N06** Do not defend AC generators as a bilateral priority: trusted supply is adequate (AIB LOW INTERVENTION), yet Canada's observed protection or attention is high.
