# United States–Australia Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Li-ion cells & packs (joint); Other accumulators (joint). Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: AC generators † (US->Australia). Wind & Hydropower Equipment: Wind turbine generating sets (US->Australia).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of oil extraction; lithium minerals; battery recycling feed in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for li-ion cells & packs. Keep market access open and predictable for wind turbine generating sets, AC generators † (no new tariff on the trusted supplier). Keep nuclear special materials; transformers; overhead conductors open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade US-Australia Critical Minerals and Rare Earths Framework (Oct 2025): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Coal extraction capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Gas extraction capacity. Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 117 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair (not counting 25 cells whose products are read node by node), 10 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 2 direct competition and 82 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Australia Resource & Processing Anchor (primary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Australia supplies nothing at the top two tiers that the US is short of; the US supplies wind turbine generating sets, AC generators † where Australia is short; the two share exposure to coercive suppliers in li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity; and they specialise at different steps of the same chains in lithium minerals, battery recycling feed.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Australia brings resources and processing. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and EFA, NRFC, CEFC, ARENA on the Australia side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

**Do not prioritise** (Section 10):

- *Recommendation. *Do not co-subsidise new Coal extraction capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 9%, Australia 33%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.

**Existing mechanisms and the MEA question** (Section 8):

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Australia's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP, U.S. FTA. [1]
- Quad leaders committed to identify and address gaps in manufacturing capacity for clean-energy technologies, focusing on solar PV, hydrogen electrolysers and EV batteries. They adopted joint principles for clean-energy supply chains and planned a public-private Quad Clean Energy Supply Chain Conference. [2]
- At Wilmington on 21 Sep 2024 the Quad announced an initiative to boost energy efficiency, including deployment and manufacturing of affordable high-efficiency cooling systems to cut grid strain, with an initial US$1.25m of US technical-assistance financing. The 2025-26 Quad statements do not report on it. [3]
- Quad leaders pledged on 21 Sep 2024 to align policies, incentives, standards and investment for diversified clean-energy supply chains, to use policy and public finance to catalyse private investment in allied supply chains, and to focus near-term efforts on mineral production, recycling and battery manufacturing, citing complementary capabilities across the battery supply chain. No battery-specific programme, finance or targets followed in the 2025-26 Quad statements. [4]
- The Wilmington fact sheet (21 Sep 2024) says Quad development finance institutions and agencies decided to meet to explore investments in the Indo-Pacific including clean energy and quality infrastructure, building on a 2022 meeting of the heads of Export Finance Australia, AIFFP, India EXIM, JBIC and DFC. No later meeting or joint transaction is reported in the 2025-26 Quad statements. [5]
- Quad foreign ministers met in Washington on 1 Jul 2025, voiced concern over the abrupt constriction of critical-mineral supply chains and reliance on any one country for processing and refining, launched the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative and planned to launch a Quad Ports of the Future Partnership in Mumbai in Oct 2025. They looked forward to a Leaders' Summit hosted by India later in 2025 and the next foreign ministers' meeting hosted by Australia in 2026; neither took place as planned. [6]
- Quad foreign ministers launched the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative in Washington on 1 Jul 2025 to secure and diversify critical-mineral supply chains, with priorities including e-waste critical-mineral recovery and private-sector investment. It was given a framework on 26 May 2026 with an intent to mobilise up to US$20bn for mining, processing and recycling; no project list has been published. [7]
- Australia's AUD 50m programme (about US$34m at Sep 2024 rates; FY25-FY28) opened Round 1 with up to A$25m for joint Australian and Indo-Pacific R&D and feasibility projects (grants of A$0.1m-2.5m covering up to 95% of costs). At Wilmington (Sep 2024) India also committed US$2m to solar projects in Fiji, Comoros, Madagascar and Seychelles and Japan US$122m to renewable projects; delivery of those pledges is not stated. [8]
- The October 2025 framework provides for a Critical Minerals Supply Security Response Group led by the US Secretary of Energy and the Australian Minister for Resources to identify priority minerals and supply vulnerabilities and to plan faster delivery of processed minerals. Secondary reports say it was set up in March 2026 and covers geological mapping, permitting, waste-stream recovery and unfair trade practices. Its meeting record and outputs are not published. [9]
- India hosted the first Quad Ports of the Future conference in Mumbai on 29-30 Oct 2025 during India Maritime Week, with about 120 delegates from 24 Indo-Pacific countries, formally launching the partnership to mobilise government and private investment in quality port infrastructure. No funding commitments were reported from the conference. [10]
- Announced in Toronto on 31 Oct 2025, this is the first round of the Production Alliance under the June 2025 G7 Critical Minerals Action Plan. Its 26 investments, partnerships and measures with France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, Norway, the US, Australia and Ukraine aim to unlock C$6.4bn (about US$4.6bn at 0.71 USD/CAD) of graphite, rare-earth and scandium projects. They include Government of Canada offtakes with Nouveau Monde Graphite and Rio Tinto (scandium) and up to C$20.2m for joint R&D. Canada also released a roadmap for standards-based critical-minerals markets. [11]
- ASPI lists the MSP, which Korea chairs in 2025, among the frameworks for Australia-Korea minerals cooperation; Australian membership is not stated in that article. [12]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [13]
- At the 19 Feb 2026 Ministerial, IEA ministers endorsed an expanded phase of the Critical Minerals Security Programme, which was established in 2022. The expansion covers: support for members that set up or expand stockpiles; rapid market assessments and coordinated responses during supply disruptions; regular table-top exercises (a rare-earth exercise was held in Nov 2025); a wider information dashboard; a new Technology Collaboration Programme on recycling and substitution. The release gives no budget. [14]
- India has held the Quad chair since 2024 and was due to host the Leaders' Summit in 2025, but none was held in 2025, after US tariffs on Indian goods rose to 50% and the New York Times reported (30 Aug 2025) that Trump no longer planned to travel to India for it. ThePrint reported on 25 May 2026 that India would pass the chair to Australia without hosting a summit and that future summits are likely to be held on the sidelines of multilateral meetings. [15]
- Quad foreign ministers adopted the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework in New Delhi on 26 May 2026, under which the four intend to mobilise up to US$20bn in government and private-sector support through new and existing efforts for mining, processing and recycling, by identifying projects with a Quad nexus (located in, operated by companies headquartered in, or supplying Quad partners) and backing them through ECAs, DFIs, guarantees, loans, equity, insurance, subsidies and offtake. No project list or split by partner has been published; the only new money named by 31 Oct 2026 was a US$10m US commitment for critical-mineral recovery from waste. [16]
- India hosted the 11th Quad foreign ministers' meeting in New Delhi on 26 May 2026, where ministers voiced grave concern over economic coercion and non-market practices including arbitrary export restrictions, price manipulation and disruptions in critical minerals, announced the Quad Critical Minerals Framework and a Quad Initiative on Indo-Pacific Energy Security, and welcomed Pax Silica as a pillar of their economic-security agenda. The statement set no date for a Leaders' Summit. [17]
- The four committed on 26 May 2026 to stable and open energy markets, diversified supply chains for energy products and fertilisers, open energy trade and free navigation including through the Strait of Hormuz, and to identify cooperation in technology, policy, market analysis and emergency-response exercises through an engagement plan, including strengthening their strategic petroleum systems. The statement cites Japan's POWERR Asia, Australia's $2 billion Southeast Asia Investment Financing Facility and A$30m budget support to Fiji as related regional efforts. [18]
- The USSC, in partnership with the US Department of State, is convening two expert workshops in 2026 on how the four can operationalise the 2025 initiative and May 2026 framework: the first was held in Feb 2026, the second was scheduled for Oct 2026, and a final outcomes report is due in Jan 2027. The July 2026 report sets out policy options and describes the framework's goal of mobilising up to US$20bn as an objective to mobilise government and private capital. [19]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [20]
- Quad foreign ministers met in Atlanta on 31 Oct 2026 on the sidelines of the G20 foreign ministers' meeting, expressed grave concern over economic coercion through arbitrary export restrictions and non-market policies that cause overcapacity in critical industries, and announced the first Quad Business Leaders' Forum to promote commercial deals in key sectors. They reported progress on the critical-minerals framework, fuel security, Fiji ports, AI and undersea cables. [21]
- The Quad convened its first Fuel Security Forum in September 2026 and discussed advancing fuel security in the Indo-Pacific, including by strengthening the four countries' strategic petroleum reserves and stockholdings; press reports place it at the G20 energy meetings in Houston, which the official statement does not state. [22]
- Quad ministers said on 31 Oct 2026 they will launch a new effort to identify semiconductor and AI-hardware supply-chain chokepoints before they cause disruptions, as part of building trusted supply chains for advanced and leading-edge chips. No lead agency, funding or timeline is stated. [23]
- Trump, Modi, Takaichi and Albanese met in Manila on 17 Nov 2026 on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit, the first Quad leaders' meeting since Sep 2024; their joint press release voiced grave concern over economic coercion including arbitrary export restrictions, committed to securing critical supply chains, announced the first Quad Business Leaders' Forum for early 2027 and endorsed the Quad AI Accelerator. The details come from ANI and Reuters reporting, not the official text. [24]
- Quad leaders in Manila (17 Nov 2026) welcomed an expansion of Australia's contribution to the Quad Partnership for Cable Connectivity and Resilience to include electricity cables to strengthen regional energy security, alongside upcoming undersea cable projects in the Indian Ocean. The partnership previously covered telecom cables, with over US$140m committed to Pacific cable builds by Sep 2024 and a goal of connecting all Pacific Islands Forum countries by 2026; routes and funding for the electricity-cable work are not stated. - OSI reads hydrogen-ammonia-and-methanol as low strategic case, but the evidence layer records a capacity problem the annual trade data cannot see: METI's first international awards under the JPY3tn (about US$19.2bn) 15-year hydrogen price-gap CfD, executed through JOGMEC, went in Jan 2026 to JERA and Mitsui for about 772,000 t/yr of blue ammonia from the Blue Point plant in Louisiana from 2030-31 (Wood Mackenzie estimates about US$6.8bn). Treat it as an emerging bottleneck to re-test, not a settled open-market node. [66]
- Low-evidence units among the active states: none.
- Trade data understate concentration at uranium conversion & enrichment: enrichment is sold as a service under long-term contracts, so goods trade understates Russia's share of world enrichment capacity (roughly 40%), and the 2024 US ban on Russian low-enriched uranium imports makes trusted capacity the binding constraint.
- Trade data understate concentration at gallium, germanium & indium: China's export controls since 2023 cut its recorded exports, so 2024 trade understates its share of primary gallium and germanium output (the large majority of world supply).
