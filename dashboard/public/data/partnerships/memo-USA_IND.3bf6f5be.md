# United States–India Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Li-ion cells & packs (joint); Natural graphite (joint). Wind & Hydropower Equipment: Wind blades & hubs (India->US). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Copper smelting & refining (joint).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of oil extraction; reactor parts; coal components in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for li-ion cells & packs. Keep market access open and predictable for wind blades & hubs (no new tariff on the trusted supplier). Keep transformers; blade core wood (balsa); overhead conductors open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade DFC operations in India under the 2022 Investment Incentive Agreement: sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution. Do not defend AC generators as a bilateral priority. Do not defend Automatic controllers as a bilateral priority.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 117 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair (not counting 25 cells whose products are read node by node), 14 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 0 direct competition and 77 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); India Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Development & Scale-Up Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *India supplies wind blades & hubs where the US is short; the US supplies electrolysers †, sensors & controller parts, electric furnaces & heating where India is short; the two share exposure to coercive suppliers in li-ion cells & packs, natural graphite, copper smelting & refining, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity; and they specialise at different steps of the same chains in reactor parts, nuclear special materials.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; India brings coordination across allied chains, sites for development and scale-up, specialised technology and manufacturing. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and India Exim Bank, ECGC on the India side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- India's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP. [1]
- The two National Security Advisors held the inaugural iCET meeting on 31 Jan 2023, covering semiconductor supply chains, AI, quantum, space and defence innovation, and named rare-earth processing and advanced materials for future cooperation. The February 2025 leaders' statement carries this agenda forward under TRUST and does not mention iCET. [2]
- Quad leaders committed to identify and address gaps in manufacturing capacity for clean-energy technologies, focusing on solar PV, hydrogen electrolysers and EV batteries. They adopted joint principles for clean-energy supply chains and planned a public-private Quad Clean Energy Supply Chain Conference. [3]
- At Wilmington on 21 Sep 2024 the Quad announced an initiative to boost energy efficiency, including deployment and manufacturing of affordable high-efficiency cooling systems to cut grid strain, with an initial US$1.25m of US technical-assistance financing. The 2025-26 Quad statements do not report on it. [4]
- Quad leaders pledged on 21 Sep 2024 to align policies, incentives, standards and investment for diversified clean-energy supply chains, to use policy and public finance to catalyse private investment in allied supply chains, and to focus near-term efforts on mineral production, recycling and battery manufacturing, citing complementary capabilities across the battery supply chain. No battery-specific programme, finance or targets followed in the 2025-26 Quad statements. [5]
- The Wilmington fact sheet (21 Sep 2024) says Quad development finance institutions and agencies decided to meet to explore investments in the Indo-Pacific including clean energy and quality infrastructure, building on a 2022 meeting of the heads of Export Finance Australia, AIFFP, India EXIM, JBIC and DFC. No later meeting or joint transaction is reported in the 2025-26 Quad statements. [6]
- Leaders re-committed to the Energy Security Partnership covering oil, gas and civil nuclear, aimed to make the US a leading supplier of crude, petroleum products and LNG to India, backed India's full IEA membership and agreed to expand strategic oil-reserve arrangements. The statement does not mention the earlier Strategic Clean Energy Partnership. [7]
- Both countries are MSP members (India joined in June 2023) and committed in February 2025 to promote investment along the critical-mineral value chain through it. The MSP was succeeded by FORGE at the February 2026 Critical Minerals Ministerial. [8]
- Quad foreign ministers met in Washington on 1 Jul 2025, voiced concern over the abrupt constriction of critical-mineral supply chains and reliance on any one country for processing and refining, launched the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative and planned to launch a Quad Ports of the Future Partnership in Mumbai in Oct 2025. They looked forward to a Leaders' Summit hosted by India later in 2025 and the next foreign ministers' meeting hosted by Australia in 2026; neither took place as planned. [9]
- Quad foreign ministers launched the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative in Washington on 1 Jul 2025 to secure and diversify critical-mineral supply chains, with priorities including e-waste critical-mineral recovery and private-sector investment. It was given a framework on 26 May 2026 with an intent to mobilise up to US$20bn for mining, processing and recycling; no project list has been published. [10]
- Australia's AUD 50m programme (about US$34m at Sep 2024 rates; FY25-FY28) opened Round 1 with up to A$25m for joint Australian and Indo-Pacific R&D and feasibility projects (grants of A$0.1m-2.5m covering up to 95% of costs). At Wilmington (Sep 2024) India also committed US$2m to solar projects in Fiji, Comoros, Madagascar and Seychelles and Japan US$122m to renewable projects; delivery of those pledges is not stated. [11]
- India hosted the first Quad Ports of the Future conference in Mumbai on 29-30 Oct 2025 during India Maritime Week, with about 120 delegates from 24 Indo-Pacific countries, formally launching the partnership to mobilise government and private investment in quality port infrastructure. No funding commitments were reported from the conference. [12]
- India joined the US-led Pax Silica initiative on AI, semiconductor and critical-mineral supply chains on 20 Feb 2026, after being left out of the December 2025 launch group; the initiative now has about 27 signatories including the EU. [13]
- India has held the Quad chair since 2024 and was due to host the Leaders' Summit in 2025, but none was held in 2025, after US tariffs on Indian goods rose to 50% and the New York Times reported (30 Aug 2025) that Trump no longer planned to travel to India for it. ThePrint reported on 25 May 2026 that India would pass the chair to Australia without hosting a summit and that future summits are likely to be held on the sidelines of multilateral meetings. [14]
- Quad foreign ministers adopted the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework in New Delhi on 26 May 2026, under which the four intend to mobilise up to US$20bn in government and private-sector support through new and existing efforts for mining, processing and recycling, by identifying projects with a Quad nexus (located in, operated by companies headquartered in, or supplying Quad partners) and backing them through ECAs, DFIs, guarantees, loans, equity, insurance, subsidies and offtake. No project list or split by partner has been published; the only new money named by 31 Oct 2026 was a US$10m US commitment for critical-mineral recovery from waste. [15]
- India hosted the 11th Quad foreign ministers' meeting in New Delhi on 26 May 2026, where ministers voiced grave concern over economic coercion and non-market practices including arbitrary export restrictions, price manipulation and disruptions in critical minerals, announced the Quad Critical Minerals Framework and a Quad Initiative on Indo-Pacific Energy Security, and welcomed Pax Silica as a pillar of their economic-security agenda. The statement set no date for a Leaders' Summit. [16]
- The four committed on 26 May 2026 to stable and open energy markets, diversified supply chains for energy products and fertilisers, open energy trade and free navigation including through the Strait of Hormuz, and to identify cooperation in technology, policy, market analysis and emergency-response exercises through an engagement plan, including strengthening their strategic petroleum systems. The statement cites Japan's POWERR Asia, Australia's $2 billion Southeast Asia Investment Financing Facility and A$30m budget support to Fiji as related regional efforts. [17]
- The USSC, in partnership with the US Department of State, is convening two expert workshops in 2026 on how the four can operationalise the 2025 initiative and May 2026 framework: the first was held in Feb 2026, the second was scheduled for Oct 2026, and a final outcomes report is due in Jan 2027. The July 2026 report sets out policy options and describes the framework's goal of mobilising up to US$20bn as an objective to mobilise government and private capital. [18]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [19]
- Secretary Rubio's second India visit of 2026 focused on energy cooperation, commercial investment and economic ties: he met Jaishankar in Mumbai, toured Reliance's 1.4m b/d Jamnagar refinery and urged more refining of Venezuelan crude. No purchase commitments were announced. [20]
- Quad foreign ministers met in Atlanta on 31 Oct 2026 on the sidelines of the G20 foreign ministers' meeting, expressed grave concern over economic coercion through arbitrary export restrictions and non-market policies that cause overcapacity in critical industries, and announced the first Quad Business Leaders' Forum to promote commercial deals in key sectors. They reported progress on the critical-minerals framework, fuel security, Fiji ports, AI and undersea cables. [21]
- The Quad convened its first Fuel Security Forum in September 2026 and discussed advancing fuel security in the Indo-Pacific, including by strengthening the four countries' strategic petroleum reserves and stockholdings; press reports place it at the G20 energy meetings in Houston, which the official statement does not state. [22]
- Quad ministers said on 31 Oct 2026 they will launch a new effort to identify semiconductor and AI-hardware supply-chain chokepoints before they cause disruptions, as part of building trusted supply chains for advanced and leading-edge chips. No lead agency, funding or timeline is stated. [23]
- Trump, Modi, Takaichi and Albanese met in Manila on 17 Nov 2026 on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit, the first Quad leaders' meeting since Sep 2024; their joint press release voiced grave concern over economic coercion including arbitrary export restrictions, committed to securing critical supply chains, announced the first Quad Business Leaders' Forum for early 2027 and endorsed the Quad AI Accelerator. The details come from ANI and Reuters reporting, not the official text. [24]
- Quad leaders in Manila (17 Nov 2026) welcomed an expansion of Australia's contribution to the Quad Partnership for Cable Connectivity and Resilience to include electricity cables to strengthen regional energy security, alongside upcoming undersea cable projects in the Indian Ocean. The partnership previously covered telecom cables, with over US$140m committed to Pacific cable builds by Sep 2024 and a goal of connecting all Pacific Islands Forum countries by 2026; routes and funding for the electricity-cable work are not stated. [25]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, US imported US$15.4bn from India and India imported US$13.6bn from US (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- Leaders set Mission 500 to more than double bilateral trade to US$500bn by 2030 and planned to negotiate the first tranche of a multi-sector BTA by fall 2025. That deadline passed without a signed tranche. [26]

By late October 2026 no deal had been concluded; India's foreign ministry said both sides reaffirmed their commitment to concluding one, and a former Indian ambassador said India was waiting for the US to formalise its tariff positions. [27]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- EXIM, Export Finance Australia, ECGC of India and NEXI of Japan signed a Memorandum of Cooperation in Oslo on 23 Apr 2024, committing to cooperate on supply-chain resilience, critical and emerging technologies, quality infrastructure including renewable energy, and low-carbon technologies and critical-minerals supply, and to pursue joint business promotion. The four ECAs are to meet in Tokyo on 18 Nov 2026 to identify practical cooperation; JBIC and India EXIM are not signatories. [29]
- Quad leaders welcomed on 21 Sep 2024 the finalisation of a Memorandum of Cooperation for a Semiconductor Supply Chains Contingency Network to collaborate on addressing semiconductor supply-chain risks; its 2025-26 activity is not stated in later Quad statements. [30]
- QUIN is a nonprofit launched at the 2023 Quad Leaders' Summit to accelerate investment in critical and emerging technologies; in 2024 it supported ten strategic investments and partnerships across the Quad in critical minerals, renewable energy, cybersecurity and aerospace. The 2025-26 Quad statements do not mention it, and the investments are not named. [31]
- Leaders launched the COMPACT (Catalyzing Opportunities for Military Partnership, Accelerated Commerce & Technology) as the umbrella for defence, trade, energy and technology outcomes, and welcomed about US$7.35bn of ongoing Indian investment in the US, including Novelis aluminium in Alabama and Kentucky, JSW steel in Texas and Ohio and Epsilon Advanced Materials battery materials in North Carolina. [32]
- TRUST (Transforming the Relationship Utilizing Strategic Technology) covers AI, semiconductors, quantum, biotech, energy and space and commits to trusted supply chains for semiconductors and critical minerals. Under it the leaders launched the Strategic Mineral Recovery initiative to recover and process lithium, cobalt and rare earths from aluminium, coal-mining and oil-and-gas streams; no budget or project list is given. [33]
- India will eliminate or reduce tariffs on all US industrial goods and many farm goods. The US set an 18% reciprocal rate and, once the Interim Agreement is concluded, will remove it on generic pharmaceuticals, gems and aircraft parts, lift Section 232 tariffs on certain aircraft parts and give India a preferential quota for auto parts. Both sides agreed to align on economic security, including inbound and outbound investment reviews and export controls, and either side may modify its commitments if the other's tariffs change. [34]
- India intends to purchase US$500bn of US energy products, aircraft and parts, precious metals, technology products and coking coal over five years, and trade in GPUs and other data-centre goods is to rise significantly. No annual schedule or enforcement mechanism is stated. [35]
- Under the framework's second pillar the four aim to share permitting and licensing practices to shorten approval timelines, develop or strengthen tools to review critical-mineral transactions that threaten national security, cooperate on geological mapping and resource assessment, and consider coordinated measures against non-market policies such as high-standards marketplaces and price mechanisms. These are stated intentions; no joint price mechanism or marketplace has been announced. [36]
- The framework commits the four to improve recovery of critical minerals from e-waste and other scrap, encourage investment in recycling technologies, processes and collection networks in Quad countries with the private sector, promote recovery innovation, and explore streamlining export and import procedures for relevant waste and scrap among the partners. [37]
- At the Manila meeting on 17 Nov 2026 Trump said the United States, Australia, India and Japan had signed several agreements on critical minerals and rare earths so that no Quad member is exposed to coercion by a single foreign supplier. The parties, instruments, projects and amounts of these agreements are not stated in the source. [38]

- Evidence-led compacts (OSI cannot see the partner's capability in trade data): Solar Manufacturing.
- India has no listed trade position (under 1% of world supply) at nodes where the US has material demand: li-ion cells & packs, natural graphite, electrolysers †, uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores), uranium conversion & enrichment, nuclear reactors & enrichment machinery, accumulator parts & separators, lithium chemicals, PV cover glass, rare-earth permanent magnets, ferrite & other magnets, heat pumps & AC units, solar thermal water heaters, gallium, germanium & indium, power semiconductors, electric two-wheelers & works trucks, blade core wood (balsa), PGMs (iridium, ruthenium). Trade data miss capability that is owned abroad or sold at home, so these nodes cannot show complementarity even where corporate evidence suggests it.
- Product-definition caveats (†) at 2 nodes: the notes are under the Section 2 node table.
- Low-evidence units among the active states: none.
- Trade data understate concentration at uranium conversion & enrichment: enrichment is sold as a service under long-term contracts, so goods trade understates Russia's share of world enrichment capacity (roughly 40%), and the 2024 US ban on Russian low-enriched uranium imports makes trusted capacity the binding constraint.
- Trade data understate concentration at gallium, germanium & indium: China's export controls since 2023 cut its recorded exports, so 2024 trade understates its share of primary gallium and germanium output (the large majority of world supply).
- Fuel-trade lanes reach tier 1 (oil extraction; buyer: India): real energy-security lanes, but outside the electro-industrial compacts and with no market failure beyond the supply concentration the engine measures.
