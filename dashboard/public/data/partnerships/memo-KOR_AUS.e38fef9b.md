# Korea–Australia Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Wind & Hydropower Equipment: Wind towers † (Korea->Australia). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Power cables (Korea->Australia).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of cathode materials & precursors; accumulator parts & separators; coal components in procurement and content rules. Keep market access open and predictable for wind towers †, power cables, coal extraction (no new tariff on the trusted supplier). Keep nuclear special materials; battery recycling feed; semiconductor manufacturing equipment open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade EFA-KEXIM co-financing channel for critical minerals: sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution. Do not put new public money behind Lithium minerals investment that is already committed (evidence KRAU-14, KRAU-19, KRAU-20). Do not defend Automatic controllers as a bilateral priority.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 117 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair (not counting 25 cells whose products are read node by node), 10 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 0 direct competition and 81 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: Korea Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary); Australia Resource & Processing Anchor (primary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Australia supplies lithium minerals where Korea is short; Korea supplies wind towers †, power cables where Australia is short; they share no node-level chokepoint where neither supplies; and they specialise at different steps of the same chains in cathode materials & precursors, accumulator parts & separators.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, Korea brings specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing, coordination across allied chains; Australia brings resources and processing. Public finance runs through KEXIM, K-SURE, KOMIR, KDB on the Korea side and EFA, NRFC, CEFC, ARENA on the Australia side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Australia's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP, U.S. FTA. [1]
- DFAT notes RCEP entered into force for Korea on 1 Feb 2022 and may be used by exporters as an alternative to KAFTA. [2]
- Sixth 2+2 met 1 May 2024 with a joint statement; no 2025-26 meeting was found, and the biennial cadence implies the next falls around 2026. [3]
- Lee noted large Korean investment in Australian lithium, critical-minerals and rare-earth projects; no joint statement or deliverable found. [4]
- ASPI lists the MSP, which Korea chairs in 2025, among the frameworks for Australia-Korea minerals cooperation; Australian membership is not stated in that article. [5]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [6]
- At the 19 Feb 2026 Ministerial, IEA ministers endorsed an expanded phase of the Critical Minerals Security Programme, which was established in 2022. The expansion covers: support for members that set up or expand stockpiles; rapid market assessments and coordinated responses during supply disruptions; regular table-top exercises (a rare-earth exercise was held in Nov 2025); a wider information dashboard; a new Technology Collaboration Programme on recycling and substitution. The release gives no budget. [7]
- On 24 Jun 2026 Korea Zinc's chair met Albanese in Canberra on supply chains and smelting competitiveness and agreed to explore cooperation; Project Crucible is the US$7.4bn Tennessee smelter, not an Australian investment. [8]
- Albanese called Korea a trusted partner in critical minerals and clean energy at a Sep 2026 UNGA meeting, the third bilateral after the G7 and APEC; no deliverable reported; day approximate. [9]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [10]
