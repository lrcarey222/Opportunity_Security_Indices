# The Quad: Energy and Industrial Partnership of the United States, Japan, India and Australia

*Country priorities, AIB roles and partnership opportunities: where the group integrates, through which instruments, and where it stays out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Solar Manufacturing: Cells & cover glass: Solar cells. Battery Materials & Cells: Cells & packs: Li-ion cells & packs. Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Separated oxides: Rare-earth oxides & compounds. Battery Materials & Cells: Refined chemicals: Cobalt intermediates & chemicals. Battery Materials & Cells: Minerals & recycled feed: Natural graphite.
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise member supply of other accumulators and accumulator parts & separators in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for solar cells, li-ion cells & packs, rare-earth oxides & compounds and 3 others. Keep market access open among members for manganese dioxide (EMD) and rare-earth permanent magnets. Keep copper ores & concentrates, nickel intermediates & chemicals, copper wire & rod and 1 other open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Do not create a new Quad body. Give Quad foreign ministers' meetings and Leaders' Summit cycle a mandate for the nodes in Section 5: a shared project pipeline, reciprocal supplier and buyer commitments and a KPI dashboard, reviewed every 12 months. Run the sector work through the Quad bodies already set up for it: Quad Clean Energy Supply Chains Initiative and Diversification Program (CSOL-M1, operating), Quad Partnership for Cable Connectivity and Resilience (extended to electricity cables) (QUAD-M5, operating), Quad semiconductor supply-chain cooperation (Contingency Network MoC and chokepoint effort) (QUAD-M6, signed) and Quad Critical Minerals Initiative (Jul 2025) and Framework (May 2026) (QUAD-M7, announced). Run its finance through Quad export credit agency cooperation (EFA-ECGC-NEXI-EXIM MoC) and Quad DFI track (QUAD-M3).
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new automatic controllers capacity among members. Do not create a new Quad secretariat or treaty body. Do not treat copper ores & concentrates as a Quad priority.

**Why the Quad matters.**

- Of 76 electro-industrial nodes across the AIB compacts, 30 show an active Quad relation (a member supplying another member's need, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier, or a chokepoint at least two members share with no member supplier), 3 member competition and 43 no Quad case. [1]
- A coercive supplier holds at least 50% of world supply at 21 nodes; the AIB classes 42 nodes BUILD (allied capacity short of demand under the China shock). [1]
- *Inference. *What each member brings (AIB strategic roles): US demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital and outward investment; Japan capital and outward investment, specialised technology and manufacturing, manufacturing at scale; India coordination across allied chains, sites for development and scale-up, specialised technology and manufacturing; Australia resources and processing.

**Frameworks between members**

- On 4 May 2026 Prime Ministers Takaichi and Albanese committed to share information and consult on economic-security contingencies (geopolitical tensions, economic coercion, market interruptions), align policy through the Economic Security Dialogue, Economic Dialogue and Energy and Resources Dialogue, and enhance coordination among JOGMEC, JBIC and Export Finance Australia. The declaration welcomes Australia's Critical Minerals Strategic Reserve, criticises critical-mineral export restrictions and non-market overcapacity, backs cooperation on standards for low-emissions aluminium and steel, and names Australia's General Purpose Frigate programme as a catalyst for defence-industry supply-chain cooperation. [14]
- During Takaichi's 1-3 Jul 2026 visit the leaders adopted a Joint Declaration on Economic Security Cooperation for project-based collaboration in semiconductors, critical minerals, ICT including AI, clean energy and pharmaceuticals, with a Fact Sheet 2.0, after the second round of the Economic Security Dialogue earlier in 2026 and an inaugural private-sector dialogue; they voiced grave concern over arbitrary export restrictions, particularly in critical minerals, and agreed to ease export-control challenges between them. The summit produced 16 outcome documents; an MEA briefing reportedly cited more than 120 B2B MoUs since August 2025 with about US$10bn of commitments, a figure not stated in the joint statement. [15]
- The CEPA was signed in February 2011 and entered into force on 1 August 2011, cutting tariffs on over 94% of traded products. Noting that more than 15 years have passed since signature, the 2025 and 2026 summits agreed to accelerate the review of its implementation and full utilisation to make it more forward-looking; no concluded review or new tariff schedule has been published. [16]
- The 2026 summit agreed to improve the investment environment using the fast-track mechanism under the IJICP and endorsed cooperation in logistics, textiles, food processing, agriculture, automotives and industrial capital goods, after the 2025 summit added support for Japan Industrial Townships. METI reports an IJICP roadmap from the March 2022 summit and a seventh meeting in Tokyo on 17 Sep 2025. [17]
- The Clean Energy Partnership launched in 2022 works through the annual India-Japan Energy Dialogue, whose 11th round issued a joint statement in August 2025. At the July 2026 summit MoPNG and METI adopted a Joint Statement on Energy Resilience on crude and product stockpiling and joint investment in maritime energy transport, the two sides signed a Cooperative Biogas for Growth MoC aimed at 1,000 biogas plants, and Japan backed India's IEA membership. [18]
- Signed at the 16th summit, the MoC strengthens cooperation in upstream critical-minerals exploration through exchange of technical expertise. Press reports name the Geological Survey of India and JOGMEC as the cooperating agencies and add work on an e-waste recycling ecosystem; no projects or funding are stated. [19]
- The MoC promotes cooperation in battery-related projects and business opportunities to build a trusted, resilient and sustainable battery supply chain; the signing ministries are not named in the list of outcomes. It follows a 2025 JETRO and Government of Japan business matchmaking and roundtable in India on battery and critical-mineral supply chains with over 70 companies and government bodies. [20]
- At the Third Annual Summit in Melbourne on 9 Jul 2026 the leaders reaffirmed the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership (agreed in June 2020), committed to an ambitious CECA, and called for partnerships to secure long-term critical-mineral supply and offtake and to build processing and value-addition capability. They also agreed the PACTS partnership, welcomed the Australia-Canada-India Technology and Innovation Partnership MOU and announced A$10m for Maitri grants. [21]
- Issued on 9 Jul 2026 amid Middle East supply disruption, the statement names Australia an important LNG supplier to India and India an important supplier of liquid fuels to Australia (its 4th largest source of refined petroleum), and commits both to keep supplies of coal, diesel, other liquid fuels and natural gas stable and to advance energy trade through ECTA and CECA. It also covers low-carbon fuels and electrification but sets no volumes or targets. [22]
- The leaders confirmed on 9 Jul 2026 a refreshed MoU between Geoscience Australia and the Geological Survey of India that provides a framework for science exchanges on resource mapping and mineral exploration, aimed at finding new critical-mineral deposits. No funding is attached in the release. [23]
- Signed at the Third Annual Summit on 9 Jul 2026, the Arrangement sets the procedures for implementing the 2015 agreement and enables long-term Australian uranium exports to India for exclusively peaceful purposes under IAEA safeguards. No volumes, prices, buyers or start date were announced; India runs 24 reactors (8.78 GW) and targets 100 GW of nuclear capacity by 2047. [24]
- Signed at the Australian Embassy in Tokyo on 2 Oct 2026 under the economic-security declaration, the non-binding MoC sets a framework for rapid, coordinated action by Japanese (JBIC, JICA, JOGMEC, NEXI) and Australian (EFA, ARENA, AIFFP, NRFC) strategic financing agencies on supply-chain resilience including critical minerals and energy. It commits to early joint discussion of co-investment projects, regular meetings of policy and finance agencies on priority projects, shared risk assessments and use of JBIC's Overseas Economic Security Arrangement account alongside EFA's National Interest Account, building on the inaugural Strategic Investment Networking Forum; it sets no amounts. **Trade measures and frictions between members**

- EO 14329, signed 6 Aug 2025, added a 25% ad valorem duty on goods of India because India imported Russian oil; it applied from 27 Aug 2025 and lifted the combined US tariff on many Indian goods to 50% with the 25% reciprocal rate. EO 14384 terminated it from 7 Feb 2026. [26]
- From 12:01 a.m. EST on 7 Feb 2026 products of India are no longer subject to the 25% Russian-oil duty and HTS headings 9903.01.84-89 are terminated. Commerce, with State and Treasury, monitors whether India resumes direct or indirect imports of Russian oil and may recommend reimposing the 25% duty. [27]
- A Section 122 proclamation of 20 Feb 2026 imposed a 10% import duty for 150 days from 24 Feb 2026 on all trading partners, Australia included. It exempted certain critical minerals, energy and energy products, goods under Section 232 actions, USMCA-compliant goods and some CAFTA-DR textiles, with no AUSFTA exemption. The duty ran out after 150 days in July 2026 and Section 301 tariffs took over from 24 Jul 2026. [28]
- Axios (30 Jun 2026) says Nippon reaffirmed an $11bn investment pledge with allocation of $7.8bn unspecified and mostly still promised, the golden share has not been used on management decisions, and leverage rose with an S&P downgrade; the deal closed 18 Jun 2025. [29]
- USTR took final Section 301 action on 60 economies on 23 Jul 2026. India is one of 17 economies at 10% (those with, or committed to, a forced-labour import ban) rather than the 12.5% default. Exemptions cover scarce raw materials and products whose tariffing would disrupt the economy. The duties replaced the Section 122 surcharge from 24 Jul 2026. [30]
- Jiji reports the US imposed new 12.5% Section 301 tariffs on Japan and dozens of partners after the Section 122 surcharge expired 24 Jul 2026; Japan called it regrettable and the 15% auto rate is said to be maintained; a separate excess-capacity 301 probe (launched Mar 2026) includes Japan. [31]
- Section 232 tariffs on steel, aluminium, copper and automobiles continued unchanged beside the Section 122 and Section 301 measures. [32]
- USTR's Section 301 notice (91 FR 47318, 28 Jul 2026) imposes a 12.5% tariff on products of Australia from 24 Jul 2026 because Australia was found not to impose and enforce a forced-labour import ban; the rate is 10% for economies with a ban or an ART commitment. Goods subject to Section 232 tariffs and listed raw materials and products are exempt. The 60 investigations began on 12 Mar 2026. [33]

**Where the findings are weak or counterintuitive**

- Group bets rest on a node need inherited from each buyer's technology x stage cell (a proxy): Solar cells, Li-ion cells & packs, Rare-earth oxides & compounds, Cobalt intermediates & chemicals and Natural graphite.
- At solar cells, li-ion cells & packs, rare-earth oxides & compounds, cobalt intermediates & chemicals, natural graphite, other accumulators and 12 others the largest trusted supplier is outside the group (Thailand, Poland, Malaysia, Finland, Madagascar, Vietnam and 5 others), so group-only rules understate the trusted supply that exists; buy from it or bring it in, do not try to replace it.
- Country priorities come from each member's own engine and are not negotiated positions; the partner counts in Section 2 read the engine's recommended partners, which favour trusted, capable suppliers and do not weigh politics.
- † AC generators: Same HS heading family (8501) and the same manufacturers as motors, and the same magnet and NGO inputs. Most of the value is large generators for hydro, thermal and nuclear plants, which take them as inputs from this compact. Alternative: SC-WIND, rejected because wind generating sets are traded complete (850231).
- † Electrolysers: HS 854330 also covers electroplating apparatus.
- † Manganese dioxide (EMD): Mostly for primary (alkaline) cells, partly an LMO cathode precursor; kept with the refined battery chemicals.
- † Polysilicon: US polysilicon exports (31% of world exports) are mostly electronic-grade material for wafer makers, Japan's above all, while the chokepoint is solar-grade supply. The memos read the US production share (about 2%) instead, so the US is not a supplier of the node.
- † Rare-earth metals: Almost all of Australia's export share is one Malaysia-reported flow (about US$128m in 2024), most likely Lynas's Kalgoorlie intermediate entering Lynas Malaysia: feedstock, not rare-earth metal capacity. The memos read Australia's production share instead (none recorded), so Australia is not a supplier of the node.
- † Uranium/fuel Extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (Spent fuel & nuclear scrap, Uranium ores): Australia files uranium oxide concentrate under a different HS heading from most producers, so its 75% export share is a coding artefact; it mines about 8-10% of world uranium. The memos read its production share (about 7.5%) instead.
- Trade data understate concentration at uranium conversion & enrichment: enrichment is sold as a service under long-term contracts, so goods trade understates Russia's share of world enrichment capacity (roughly 40%), and the 2024 US ban on Russian low-enriched uranium imports makes trusted capacity the binding constraint.
- Trade data understate concentration at gallium, germanium & indium: China's export controls since 2023 cut its recorded exports, so 2024 trade understates its share of primary gallium and germanium output (the large majority of world supply).
- The node reading uses only evidence items whose parties are all members (a member's own project counts as partial coverage); an item that also names a non-member, such as a non-member firm's plant in a member, is left to that relationship's memo.
- Trade data miss capability owned abroad or sold at home (a member's firms' plants in another member, a large domestic market), so a member can look weaker in the node tables than its firms are.
