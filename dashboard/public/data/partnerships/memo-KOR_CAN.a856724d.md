# Korea–Canada Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 17 November 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Accumulator parts & separators (Korea->Canada). Nuclear Fuel & Reactor Systems: Uranium conversion & enrichment † (Canada->Korea).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of accumulator parts & separators; oil extraction; cathode materials & precursors in procurement and content rules. Keep market access open and predictable for non-oriented electrical steel (NGO), coal extraction (no new tariff on the trusted supplier). Keep nuclear special materials; battery recycling feed; semiconductor manufacturing equipment open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade Critical-minerals and energy-security MOU (2023) and 2026 stockpiling track: sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution. Do not put new public money behind Cathode materials & precursors investment that is already committed (evidence KRCA-17). Do not defend AC generators as a bilateral priority.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 117 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair (not counting 25 cells whose products are read node by node), 11 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 0 direct competition and 82 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: Korea Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary); Canada Resource & Processing Anchor (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary), Demand & Market Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Canada supplies uranium conversion & enrichment † where Korea is short; Korea supplies accumulator parts & separators, non-oriented electrical steel (NGO) where Canada is short; they share no node-level chokepoint where neither supplies; and they specialise at different steps of the same chains in cathode materials & precursors, nickel intermediates & chemicals, power cables, aluminium wire rod.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, Korea brings specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing, coordination across allied chains; Canada brings resources and processing, capital, demand and offtake. Public finance runs through KEXIM, K-SURE, KOMIR, KDB on the Korea side and ISED, EDC, CIB, CGF, CMIF, FinDev on the Canada side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-17); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Canada's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: G7, MSP, NATO, U.S. FTA. [1]
- Economic-security 2+2 (Joly, Champagne, Park Jin, Ahn Dukgeun) launched Sep 2022 and first met in Seoul on 16 May 2023; priorities were critical minerals, clean energy, supply chains, AI and economic coercion. [2]
- A joint statement in New York on 23 Sep 2024 set up a network of MSP export-credit agencies, development-finance institutions and state-backed funds. Its purpose is to share information, coordinate and co-finance critical-minerals projects. Named members include DFC, Export Development Canada, JICA, JBIC, KEXIM and Export Finance Norway among others. [3]
- At the 19 Feb 2026 Ministerial, IEA ministers endorsed an expanded phase of the Critical Minerals Security Programme, which was established in 2022. The expansion covers: support for members that set up or expand stockpiles; rapid market assessments and coordinated responses during supply disruptions; regular table-top exercises (a rare-earth exercise was held in Nov 2025); a wider information dashboard; a new Technology Collaboration Programme on recycling and substitution. The release gives no budget. [4]
- Second 2+2 (first was Nov 2024) agreed to pursue an update of the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Action Plan, start Defence Cooperation Agreement steps and coordinate on economic security; the statement does not mention critical minerals or energy; next meeting in Korea in 2028. [5]
- On 2 Jun 2026 Canada and Korea agreed to develop a joint critical-minerals stockpiling plan by end-2026, discussed strategic-resource investments, and Korea agreed to facilitate CKFTA preferential tariffs for Canadian crude; no volumes or amounts given. [6]
- Lee and Carney agreed on 16 Jun 2026 to strengthen cooperation in defence, security, energy and critical minerals; an informal meeting followed at the NATO summit; no deliverables or amounts reported. [7]
- On 7 Oct 2026 the trade ministers of Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye, the UK and the US signed a statement committing to new sectoral platforms on structural excess capacity. The platforms start with autos and EVs, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels, and officials are to meet at technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference and share data. [8]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, Korea imported US$3.7bn from Canada and Canada imported US$3.4bn from Korea (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]

- From 26 Dec 2025 Canada cut FTA-partner steel quotas to 75% of 2024 volumes with a 50% over-quota surtax; Korea protested that this breaches the CKFTA and sought an exemption, none found. [10]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- Carney and Lee welcomed the partnership at the Gyeongju APEC summit on 30 Oct 2025, Canada's first in the Indo-Pacific, and finalised a classified-information agreement that supports defence procurement; the readout does not mention energy, critical minerals or LNG. [13]
- Joly and Korea's Trade, Industry and Resources Minister Kim Jung-kwan signed in Ottawa; sets up an industrial cooperation committee and a forum on a Korean auto footprint, battery production, materials processing and critical-minerals recycling; no amounts or companies named. [14]
- The Geological Survey of Canada and Korea's KIGAM signed an implementation agreement on naturally occurring hydrogen research and development, announced with the 2 Jun 2026 energy and critical-minerals meeting. [15]

## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution: KRCA-M2 already exists (a standing framework with no pipeline or finance yet); dock the compacts in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not put new public money behind Cathode materials & precursors investment that is already committed (evidence KRCA-17); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not defend AC generators as a bilateral priority: trusted supply is adequate (AIB LOW INTERVENTION), yet Canada's observed protection or attention is high.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not defend Automatic controllers as a bilateral priority: trusted supply is adequate (AIB LOW INTERVENTION), yet Korea's observed protection or attention is high.
- *Recommendation. ***N05** Do not defend Coal refining as a bilateral priority: trusted supply is adequate (a large-yard node), yet Korea's observed protection or attention is high.
- *Recommendation. ***N06** Do not build a bilateral programme around Gallium, germanium & indium: one side is strong but the other has no material need for it; trade it commercially.
