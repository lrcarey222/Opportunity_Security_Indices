# Japan–Korea Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 30 October 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Solar Manufacturing: Wafers (Japan->Korea). Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Rare-earth oxides & compounds (joint); Rare-earth permanent magnets (Japan->Korea). Battery Materials & Cells: Natural graphite (joint); Cobalt intermediates & chemicals (joint); Lithium chemicals (joint); Battery materials (foil, electrolyte) (Japan->Korea). Grid Equipment & Conductors: Electric Grid refining (joint). Semiconductors & Power Electronics: Electric Grid components (Japan->Korea).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of electric vehicles refining (rare-earth metals, rare-earth oxides & compounds) in procurement and content rules. Common anti-circumvention rules for batteries refining (battery materials (foil, electrolyte), cathode materials & precursors, cobalt intermediates & chemicals) and wind refining (copper wire & rod, rare-earth metals, rare-earth oxides & compounds) and rare-earth oxides & compounds and cobalt intermediates & chemicals and lithium chemicals. Bind market access for wafers, rare-earth permanent magnets. Keep pgms (iridium, ruthenium); copper ores & concentrates; nickel intermediates & chemicals open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade Korea-Japan Supply Chain Partnership Arrangement and Industrial and Trade Policy Dialogue: sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Coal components capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Accumulator parts & separators capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Coal assembly capacity.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 142 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair, 33 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 6 direct competition and 78 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: Japan Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary); Korea Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Capital & Investment Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Korea supplies solar cells, cathode materials & precursors, gallium, germanium & indium, non-oriented electrical steel (ngo) where Japan is short; Japan supplies wafers, rare-earth permanent magnets, battery materials (foil, electrolyte), piezo-electric quartz where Korea is short; the two share exposure to the same coercive supplier in rare-earth oxides & compounds, natural graphite, cobalt intermediates & chemicals, lithium chemicals, polysilicon, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, Japan brings capital, specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing; Korea brings specialised technology and manufacturing, scale manufacturing, coordination across allied chains. Public finance runs through JBIC, NEXI, JOGMEC on the Japan side and KEXIM, K-SURE, KOMIR, KDB on the Korea side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Korea's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: IPEF, MSP, U.S. FTA. [1]
- DFAT notes RCEP entered into force for Korea on 1 Feb 2022 and may be used by exporters as an alternative to KAFTA; the member list shown here is partial. [2]
- The 16 Mar 2023 Tokyo summit restored shuttle diplomacy, with Japan pledging to lift its three-material curbs and Korea to drop its WTO case. [3]
- Korean and Japanese officials agreed a close cooperation system on critical-mineral supply risks, support for company and agency cooperation on joint development in third countries (Latin America, Asia, Africa), and policy coordination in the Minerals Security Partnership and IEA; no projects were named. [4]
- The dialogue, set up Feb 2024 with a first meeting in Jun 2024, held its second meeting in Tokyo on 26 Mar 2025 and agreed to develop a Japan-ROK cooperation platform for hydrogen and ammonia supply chains; the METI page could not be opened. [5]
- Senior officials meeting in New York agreed to enhance dialogue on critical minerals and cooperation in Southeast Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa through the trilateral Early Warning System. [6]
- At the October 2025 Tokyo conference the two business federations agreed Korea's CPTPP participation was important given US protectionism and supply-chain disruption. [7]
- The second round discussed a joint response to falling copper treatment and refining charges, cooperation within the Minerals Security Partnership and ways to develop critical minerals jointly in third countries; no agreement or named project was reported. [8]
- On 13 Jan 2026 the leaders discussed supply-chain cooperation in depth and agreed to launch agency-level talks on economic security cooperation, with rare-earth stabilisation reported as a focus; no outputs were published. [9]
- The US-hosted ministerial in Feb 2026 was attended by Japan and Korea among others, and Korea was reported to chair the FORGE critical-minerals forum through June 2026. [10]
- Korea chaired the Minerals Security Partnership until June 2026; a February 2026 report says its 17 members carried into the successor FORGE; Korean public lenders joined the MSP Finance Network; no signed US-Korea critical-minerals MOU was found. [11]
- A standing consultative body launched 14 Mar 2026 to review trade cooperation, economic security, supply chains, steel and mineral resources; the same article reports plans for joint exploration and investment in critical minerals and shared market monitoring; no schedule published. [12]
- The 19 May 2026 trade-ministry release covers crude oil swaps and mutual supply in a crisis, deeper LNG cooperation under the KOGAS-JERA agreement, SCPA crisis mechanisms and regional reserves via Japan's proposed POWERR Asia; no projects or funding named; hydrogen and critical minerals not covered. [13]
- In June 2026 Korea's trade ministry said no decision had been made on applying to CPTPP after reports of a late-June application; Japan reportedly would back accession without first requiring Korea to lift its Fukushima seafood ban; the Japan position is a press report. [14]
- The joint statement backs coordination on resource procurement and supply-chain stability, support for Korea's CPTPP accession, hydrogen certification and safety standards, critical-mineral stockpiling and crisis response through the SCPA, and an early resumption of the Korea-US-Japan Business Dialogue; business statement, not binding. [15]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, Japan imported US$10.2bn from Korea and Korea imported US$10.7bn from Japan (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- RCEP entered into force for Japan on 1 Jan 2022 and for Korea on 1 Feb 2022, giving the two countries a shared preferential trade framework with no separate bilateral FTA; tariff schedules not reviewed. [16]
- From July 2019 Japan required individual licences for fluorinated polyimide, photoresist and hydrogen fluoride exports to Korea and removed Korea from its white list; Korea removed Japan from its own list in Aug 2019 and filed a WTO complaint; Japan lifted the three-item curbs in Mar 2023. [17]
- Korea completed procedures to drop its 2019 WTO complaint on 23 Mar 2023, a week after the 16 Mar Yoon-Kishida summit; Japan had pledged to lift the three-material curbs; the article does not confirm the curbs had by then been lifted. [18]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- The three leaders agreed to pilot an early warning system exchanging supply-chain information via embassies in selected countries, with critical minerals and rechargeable batteries as priority products, plus an annual commerce and industry ministers track; the system is not published as operating beyond the pilot. [19]
- KNOC reportedly signed an MOU with JOGMEC in February 2025 on cooperation in hydrogen, ammonia and CCS. [20]
- Ministers Kim Jung-kwan and Akazawa signed the SCPA in Tokyo on 14 Mar 2026: early notification of disruptions, an emergency meeting within five days of a request, and prior consultation before measures that could harm the other side; no amounts or named projects. [21]

## 7. Recommended Sectoral Compacts

## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not co-subsidise new Coal components capacity: both are strong suppliers (Japan 9%, Korea 6%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not co-subsidise new Accumulator parts & separators capacity: both are strong suppliers (Japan 11%, Korea 13%). More trusted capacity is needed, so agree who specialises in what rather than racing.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not co-subsidise new Coal assembly capacity: both are strong suppliers (Japan 5%, Korea 4%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution: JPKR-M1 already exists (a standing framework with no pipeline or finance yet); dock the compacts in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N05** Do not put new public money behind Batteries assembly (li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators) investment that is already committed (evidence UJ-037; JPKR-29; JPKR-30); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N06** Do not put new public money behind Integrated circuits investment that is already committed (evidence JPKR-05; JPKR-06; JPKR-07; JPKR-31; JPKR-32); support is additional only beyond it.
