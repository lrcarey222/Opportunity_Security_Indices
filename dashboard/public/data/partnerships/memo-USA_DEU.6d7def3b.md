# United States–Germany Strategic Energy & Industrial Partnership

*Where deeper industrial integration creates strategic value, through which instruments, and where to stay out*

*Generated from Allied Industrial Base Explorer data and a curated external evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); evidence, sources and caveats in the appendix. Data as of 30 October 2026.*

## Executive Decision Summary

- **Build together.** Battery Materials & Cells: Synthetic graphite (anode-grade) (Germany->US); Natural graphite (joint); Other accumulators (joint). Electric Vehicles: Electric & plug-in cars (Germany->US). Nuclear Fuel & Reactor Systems: Nuclear extraction (joint). Permanent Magnets & Drive Systems: Rare-earth oxides & compounds (joint); Ferrite & other magnets (Germany->US); Electric motors (Germany->US); Motor & generator parts (Germany->US). Semiconductors & Power Electronics: Power electronics components (Germany->US).
- **Trade and standards.** Recognise trusted partner supply of electric vehicles refining (rare-earth metals, rare-earth oxides & compounds); electric & plug-in cars; ferrite & other magnets in procurement and content rules. Bind market access for synthetic graphite (anode-grade). Keep uranium/fuel extraction: aggregated low-coverage sub-sectors (spent fuel & nuclear scrap, uranium ores); nuclear special materials; transformers open to global sourcing.
- **Institutions.** Upgrade US-EU critical minerals MOU and action plan (EU-level, applies to Germany): sector strategy, reciprocal commitments, a KPI dashboard and an annual review. No new MOU.
- **Stay out.** Do not co-subsidise new Electric Vehicles components (accumulator parts & separators, electric motors, ferrite & other magnets) capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Automatic controllers capacity. Do not co-subsidise new Electric buses, trucks & tractors capacity.

**Why this partnership matters.**

- Of 142 technology x stage cells and electro-industrial nodes assessed for the pair, 45 show an active complementarity (a supplier meeting the other's need, a shared chokepoint, a capacity-constrained trusted supplier or a chain specialisation), 7 direct competition and 67 no strategic case. The relationship is not one of general similarity: most of the electro-industrial base needs no bilateral intervention. [1]
- AIB strategic roles: US Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (primary), Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary); Germany Capital & Investment Anchor (primary), Scale Manufacturing & Integration Platform (primary), Demand & Market Anchor (primary), Coordination / Bridge Hub (secondary), Specialized Technology & Manufacturing Anchor (secondary). [1]

**The central bilateral bargain.**

- *Inference. *Germany supplies synthetic graphite (anode-grade), electric & plug-in cars, ferrite & other magnets, electric motors where the US is short; the US supplies battery recycling feed, refrigerants, sensors & controller parts, ac generators where Germany is short; the two share exposure to the same coercive supplier in rare-earth oxides & compounds, natural graphite, other accumulators, li-ion cells & packs, rare-earth permanent magnets, copper smelting & refining, where neither can supply the other and the answer is joint demand, finance and third-country capacity.
- *Inference. *From the AIB roles, the US brings demand and offtake, coordination across allied chains, capital; Germany brings capital, scale manufacturing, demand and offtake. Public finance runs through DOE LPO, DoW OSC, DFC, EXIM on the US side and KfW, KfW IPEX, Hermes, UFK, DEG on the Germany side. The trade is market access and demand certainty for trusted capacity.

## 1. Current State of the Relationship

This section sets out what already exists before anything new is proposed. Facts are from the curated evidence layer (accessed 2026-11-06); items marked partial or unverified rest on secondary sources and should be confirmed before citation.

**Alliance and plurilateral architecture**

- Germany's memberships in OSI's economic-security agreements table: G7, MSP, NATO. [1]
- Germany and the USA are both original MSP partners and automatic members of the MSP Forum launched in 2024. [2]
- TTC (created 2021) had its future questioned under the 2025 administration; no formal termination or revival was found, so it is treated as dormant and superseded by the 2025 framework and 2026 critical minerals MOU. [3]
- G7 leaders (including the USA and Germany) launched the Action Plan at Kananaskis on 17 Jun 2025 to diversify production, encourage investment and local value creation; interested members support the Minerals Security Partnership; no named German-US projects. [4]

**Trade architecture and frictions**

- In the OSI energy basket, US imported US$27.8bn from Germany and Germany imported US$26.8bn from US (bilateral flows 2024, summed over the technology x stage cells). [1]
- Parliament approved the main regulation on 16 Jun 2026 (440-151), governments adopted it on 25 Jun and EU tariff cuts took effect 1 Jul 2026; Parliament's position carried suspension and sunset clauses (sunset 31 Mar 2028). [5]
- Parliament suspended work on approving the deal on 21 Jan 2026 over US Greenland demands and tariff threats and unfroze it in early Feb 2026; the freeze is lifted, so status shown is historical. [6]
- Supreme Court held on 20 Feb 2026 that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs; a Section 122 surcharge (reported at 10%, then 15%) ran from 24 Feb for 150 days and could exceed the Turnberry 15% ceiling where MFN is zero (Bruegel); it expired 24 Jul 2026; sources conflict on the rate. [7]
- On 31 Jul 2026 the Commission removed the 6 Aug expiry and extended indefinitely the suspension of EUR 93bn of countermeasures (Regulation 2025/1564), under continuous review with the option to reactivate; page seen only as a search result. [8]
- GM terminated its long-term magnet supply agreement with e-VAC (reported 22 Oct 2026); the parties are reportedly in arbitration and the dispute dates from April 2026; one German source dates the notice to July 2026; reasons not stated by the parties. [9]

**Industrial and energy agreements**

- Germany and the US signed an energy and climate partnership in May 2022 with work on LNG, hydrogen and offshore wind; no 2025-26 bilateral energy, hydrogen or critical-minerals agreement was found, and current activity is unconfirmed. [10]
- US applies the higher of MFN or a 15% all-in tariff on EU goods; the EU intends US$750bn of US LNG, oil and nuclear purchases through 2028 and US$40bn of AI chips, and European firms are expected to invest US$600bn more in the US; commitments mostly non-binding. [11]
- Rubio and Sefcovic signed a non-binding MOU on 24 Apr 2026 to diversify mining, processing and refining and coordinate on third-country export controls; a USTR action plan explores border-adjusted price floors and a later plurilateral club; no binding agreement or projects found. [12]

## 10. What Not to Do

- *Recommendation. ***N01** Do not co-subsidise new Electric Vehicles components (accumulator parts & separators, electric motors, ferrite & other magnets) capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 7%, Germany 8%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N02** Do not co-subsidise new Automatic controllers capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 16%, Germany 12%). Trusted supply is adequate: this is commercial competition.
- *Recommendation. ***N03** Do not co-subsidise new Electric buses, trucks & tractors capacity: both are strong suppliers (US 15%, Germany 11%). More trusted capacity is needed, so agree who specialises in what rather than racing.
- *Recommendation. ***N04** Do not create a new bilateral energy MOU or a free-standing MEA institution: USDE-M3 already exists (a standing framework with no pipeline or finance yet); dock the compacts in it.
- *Recommendation. ***N05** Do not put new public money behind Batteries assembly (li-ion cells & packs, other accumulators) investment that is already committed (evidence USDE-23; USDE-25); support is additional only beyond it.
- *Recommendation. ***N06** Do not put new public money behind Copper smelting & refining investment that is already committed (evidence USDE-22); support is additional only beyond it.
