## Bottom line — Germany's strategic role

Germany's distinctive role in the Allied Industrial Base is as its integrated equipment maker. It holds a role in all {{aib.n_compact_roles|nw}} strategic compacts and a core seat in {{aib.core_in|nw}}, and {{aib.recommended_economies}} economies count it as their best-placed trusted partner somewhere in the chain. Allies rely on it as their largest holder of vehicle supply and, on mixed trade codes, of drivetrain, grid-equipment and wind-part supply. Its exposure lies in imported fuel, minerals it does not mine, refined rare earths and battery cells, where {{cells.batteries-assembly.top_supplier}} supplies {{cells.batteries-assembly.china_share}} of cell and pack imports. Its industrial effort should keep EV making, drivetrains and grid equipment at the frontier, and catch up in battery cells and components, where its own need is high. For minerals it should depend deliberately on trusted producers, and development cooperation can add drivetrain and battery capacity with developing partners. At home, storage, EVs and solar need to be deployed faster. The high fence belongs around fuel supply, the rare-earth chain, battery control software and transformer steel.

- **Wafers and PV cells.** Need is low ({{cells.solar-components.need}}), yet protection ranks {{cells.solar-components.protection}} out of a hundred among Germany's own steps, on codes that include inverters and chip-making machines. ## Strategic agenda — what should Germany do next?

### 1. Hold fuel security through stocks and trusted contracts

Gas, oil and coal supply takes {{cells.gas-extraction.reconstitution}} to rebuild: size strategic stocks to that time. Reserve capacity with trusted producers such as {{cells.gas-extraction.partners}} for gas, and with {{cells.oil-extraction.partners}} for oil, where allied supply falls short. Partner evidence is {{cells.oil-extraction.partner_evidence}}, so test the contract terms and stress-test the loss of the largest supplier. Treat grain-oriented electrical steel the same way, with stocks and capacity reservations.

### 2. Secure the rare-earth chain with allied partners

Qualify trusted refining and magnet supply through offtake, public finance and allied agreements with refiners such as {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.partners}} and magnet makers such as {{aib.compacts.SC-MAGNETS.core_makers}}. Bridge the years until it qualifies with stockpiles, and phase in limits on adversary sources in critical uses only as it does.

### 3. Catch up across the battery chain, and fence its control software

Tie production incentives, first-of-a-kind finance and workforce and supplier development to benchmarks, with sunsets, in cells, components and materials refining. Build at home where the base is established and with allied partners for the rest. Require certified, trusted battery firmware, and replace untrusted control in the installed base on a deadline.

### 4. Keep vehicles, drivetrains and grid equipment at the frontier

Back next-generation R&D, workforce and supplier development and finance for expansion, tied to benchmarks under which support per unit falls. Keep the gate open to allied investment and import competition, with trade remedies only where unfair competition threatens the base. Allies rely on this supply, so reliability is part of the case.

### 5. Build trusted drivetrain and battery capacity with developing partners

Start with {{dev.rows.r2.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r2.capability}}, starting with project preparation. Test {{dev.rows.r3.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r3.capability}} before committing, and {{dev.rows.r1.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r1.capability}}, which rests on German need alone: its Preserve design sets no partner finance. The objective is both an additional trusted supplier and a durable capability in the partner economy.

### 6. Accelerate storage, EVs and solar while keeping sourcing open

Deploy faster through infrastructure, permitting and procurement, buying commodity hardware competitively with security conditions on connected equipment. Shift effort from protecting wafers, cells and grid components to deployment.

## Appendix — evidence and method

**How this brief is built.** Every number comes from the Country page data of the Allied Industrial Base Explorer (the data release of {{meta.as_of_label}}) and from the Allied Industrial Base analysis. Each technology is split into supply-chain stages from extraction to recycling, and each step gets a strategic choice by fixed rules applied in order:

- Secure where an acute vulnerability is measured;
- at deployment, Accelerate where deployment need reaches {{meta.bars.deployment}};
- Watch where the step is a large, commoditized yard;
- Preserve where Competitive Position is in the top quarter ({{meta.bars.cp}}) and either Strategic Industrial Value (bar {{meta.bars.prize}}) or supply-chain need (bar {{meta.bars.need}}) is high;
- Catch Up where the value is high, the position is not, and a capability anchor is corroborated by other evidence;
- Partner where supply-chain need is high but there is neither a top-quarter position nor, for a high-value step, a credible pathway;
- Watch otherwise.

Named layers inside a step, such as control software or magnets, take their own choice, and a step whose parts sit behind different fences is mixed. A value that rests on market size alone is capped just under the bar. Observed policy never decides a choice. The high fence (Secure) protects what cannot be allowed to fail; the medium fence covers capabilities worth building, keeping or sourcing deliberately and deployment to accelerate; the low fence (Watch) leaves a step open to competitive purchase.

**Review cases.** Some calls rest on low confidence, an engine review flag, a thin capability anchor, a known data artifact or, at deployment, a single indicator of low confidence. They stay on the map but never enter the Bottom line, the Agenda, the policy table or a headline mark. {{counts.review|cap}} steps are review cases.
