## Bottom line — the United States' strategic role

In the Allied Industrial Base the United States is the demand and market anchor, with a core seat in all {{aib.n_compacts|nw}} strategic compacts. It also supplies fab equipment, automatic controllers and AC generators, and it is the largest allied holder of gas liquids, refined fuels and gas-turbine and drilling components. Nationally, its task is to keep the broad manufacturing base where it holds a top-quarter position, from battery cells and EV drivetrains to wind turbines and PV modules. It should catch up where its position is below the top quarter but anchored: battery materials refining, reactors and nuclear fuel, and large power transformers. The fence that matters is narrow: the rare-earth chain, the electrical steel inside transformers, and the control software of the battery systems it imports mostly from {{cells.batteries-assembly.coercive_top}}. It should source battery and EV minerals from trusted producers, and use development cooperation to add trusted capacity in EV drivetrains and battery cells. Much of the map is left open: mining, fuels and basic processing are large, commoditized yards, and only battery storage and gas networks clear the bar for faster deployment.

:::choices

## Strategic agenda — what should the United States do next?

### 1. Secure the rare-earth chain through a mixed domestic and allied build-out

Build separation and metal-making at home where a foothold exists or can be built; the step's export share, {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.export_share}}, is the only measured foothold, against {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.share}} of world supply. Add trusted refining and magnet capacity with allies through co-investment and offtake, hold stocks until it qualifies, and phase in adversary-source restrictions as it does. The allied route runs through refiners such as {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.partners}} and makers such as {{aib.compacts.SC-MAGNETS.core_makers}}, and through new capacity in {{dev.by_step.electric-vehicles-refining.hosts}} on commercial terms.

### 2. Put electrical steel and large transformers on a resilience footing

Hold stocks and spares of grain-oriented electrical steel sized to the rebuild time, set long-lead procurement frameworks, and sign capacity-reservation agreements with trusted producers. Catch up in large power transformers at home where the base is established, and with allied makers such as {{aib.compacts.SC-GRID.core_makers}} through co-investment and offtake.

### 3. Keep battery manufacturing at home and fence its control software

Expand cell and pack production with time-limited support tied to milestones, open to allied and conditional investment. Require certified, trusted firmware and communications for battery systems, and replace untrusted control in the installed base on a deadline. In battery materials refining, tie time-limited incentives to benchmarks and welcome joint ventures that transfer capability.

### 4. Catch up in reactors and nuclear fuel with allied partners

Build reactors, fuel and reactor parts at home where the base is established, and with the compact's processors and makers, {{aib.compacts.SC-NUCLEAR.core_industry}}, through co-investment and offtake. Enrichment is outside this call: it rests on a small export share with no capacity measured, so it waits until domestic capacity shows in the data.

### 5. Build trusted equipment capacity with developing partners in EV drivetrains and battery cells

Start with {{dev.rows.r2.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r2.capability}} and {{dev.rows.r3.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r3.capability}}, where US need is high. Lead with development finance, guarantees and offtake, and use project preparation and skills where the constraint is a public good. The objective is both an additional trusted supplier and a durable manufacturing capability in the partner economy. This supplements the home build rather than replacing it. The United States Preserves both steps with domestic delivery, and the policy design for Preserve sets no partner-country finance, so the case rests on high US need, not on a Preserve mechanism.

### 6. Accelerate storage and gas networks, back wind turbines, and keep commodity yards open

Deploy storage and gas networks faster through infrastructure, permitting and procurement, with trusted-supplier and security conditions on public support. Give the wind-turbine base the build a Preserve call needs. Keep the Watch mining steps, fuels processing and grid components open. Keep supply reliable in gas liquids, which allies rely on most, and in refined fuels, which they rely on less.

## Appendix — evidence and method

**How this brief is built.** Every number comes from the Country page data of the Allied Industrial Base Explorer (the data release of {{meta.as_of_label}}) and from the Allied Industrial Base analysis. Each technology is split into six supply-chain stages, and each step gets a strategic choice by fixed rules applied in order:

- Secure where an acute vulnerability is measured;
- at deployment, Accelerate where deployment need reaches {{meta.bars.deployment}};
- Watch where the step is a large, commoditized yard;
- Preserve where Competitive Position is in the top quarter ({{meta.bars.cp}}) and either Strategic Industrial Value (bar {{meta.bars.prize}}) or supply-chain need (bar {{meta.bars.need}}) is high;
- Catch Up where the value is high, the position is not, and a capability anchor is corroborated by other evidence;
- Partner where supply-chain need is high but there is neither a top-quarter position nor, for a high-value step, a credible pathway;
- Watch otherwise.

Named layers inside a step, such as control software or magnets, take their own choice, and a step whose parts sit behind different fences is mixed. A value that rests on market size alone is capped just under the bar. Observed policy never decides a choice. The high fence (Secure) protects what cannot be allowed to fail; the medium fence covers capabilities worth building, keeping or sourcing deliberately and deployment to accelerate; the low fence (Watch) leaves a step open to competitive purchase.

**Review cases.** Some calls rest on low confidence, an engine review flag, a thin capability anchor, a known data artifact or, at deployment, a single indicator of low confidence. They stay on the map but never enter the Bottom line, the Agenda, the policy table or a headline mark. The brief treats {{counts.review|nw}} steps as review cases.

A partner that already hosts the step's new capacity in the AIB, or Preserves it, is a commercial case. A constraint on a partner is named only where the data observe its input; otherwise the table says the data do not cover it. In the relationship table an observed zero is stated as nothing recorded, data that do not cover a pair are left out, and the direct investment position is economy-wide, not tied to the step.
