## Bottom line — Canada's strategic role

Canada's distinctive role in the Allied Industrial Base is as a resource and processing anchor: it ranks first among allies on {{aib.first_on}}, and {{aib.recommended_economies}} economies count it as their best-placed trusted partner somewhere in the chain. That role is allied before it is national: at home, uranium mining reads Watch, and battery and EV mineral mining, where Canada holds only {{cells.batteries-extraction.share}} of world supply, read Partner. Its industrial effort should go into batteries, where it makes cells and packs, has committed investment in components and holds a supplier role in nickel chemicals. The fence that matters is narrow: the rare-earth chain, to be secured with allied partners. Only gas networks need faster deployment, and recorded protection should shift away from steps the strategy leaves open.

:::choices
- **Secure.** The rare-earth chain from refining to traction magnets, where {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.coercive_top}} holds {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.coercive_top_share}} of world supply and trusted suppliers only {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.trusted_share}} (shares that battery materials refining repeats). The answer is qualified trusted supply built with allies, stocks until it qualifies and phased limits on adversary sources, not self-sufficiency.
- **Build / preserve.** Preserve {{cells.batteries-assembly.name}}. Catch Up in battery components, on committed investment, and in battery materials refining, on its export share of refined battery materials; build EV manufacturing on committed investment and extend gas-turbine equipment. Keep uranium, nickel chemicals and aluminium wire rod reliable for allies, and above all nuclear special materials: an allied commitment, not a domestic fence.
- **Partner.** Battery and EV minerals from trusted producers such as {{cells.batteries-extraction.partners}}, where the allied model finds supply covers the need; coal-plant equipment from allied makers; solar-grade polysilicon. With developing partners, add trusted battery capacity through development finance and offtake.
- **Deploy / stay open.** Accelerate gas networks, buying equipment competitively from trusted suppliers, and build storage, grid, wind and solar on a market footing. Leave open wafers and PV cells, grid metals refining and grid components, and gas liquids processing.
:::

### Medium fence — where industrial capability matters

Here Canada makes its real industrial choices: is a capability worth having (Strategic Industrial Value), and how strong is Canada today (Competitive Position)? Most of its {{counts.catch_up}} Catch Up steps rest on small shares of world supply in mixed trade codes, so this brief builds on few.

**Preserve.** {{cells.batteries-assembly.name|cap}} is the only Preserve call that clears the evidence bar: a top-quarter position ({{cells.batteries-assembly.cp}}) on operating capacity, with high value and need. With {{cells.batteries-assembly.share}} of world supply, keeping the position means expanding it. {{cells.oil-extraction.name|cap}} also reads Preserve but is {{cells.oil-extraction.review}}, a review case, although {{aib.recommended_by.oil-extraction.n}} economies have Canada as their best-placed trusted partner for it.

**Catch Up.** {{cells.batteries-components.name|cap}} rests on {{cells.batteries-components.anchors|nw}} anchors, including committed investment, with a position of {{cells.batteries-components.cp}} and high need ({{cells.batteries-components.need}}); {{cells.batteries-components.top_supplier}} provides {{cells.batteries-components.china_share}} of Canada's imports. {{cells.batteries-refining.name|cap}} has {{cells.batteries-refining.confidence}} confidence but a single anchor, {{cells.batteries-refining.export_share}} of world exports, and a position of {{cells.batteries-refining.cp}}: a trade position to build from, not proven refining capacity. The AIB's share of nickel intermediates and chemicals ({{aib.contributes.nickel-intermediates-and-chemicals.share}}) corroborates the nickel side, not lithium, cathode or anode materials.

{{cells.electric-vehicles-assembly.name|cap}} adds committed investment to small trade shares, from a low position ({{cells.electric-vehicles-assembly.cp}}). {{cells.gas-assembly.name|cap}} ({{cells.gas-assembly.cp}}) and {{cells.gas-components.name}} ({{cells.gas-components.cp}}) extend existing positions with high confidence. ### Low fence — what not to prioritize

Watch is not a failure: affordable, diversified access matters more than domestic production. Of the {{counts.watch}} Watch steps, {{lists.watch_deploy|n}} are deployments below the Accelerate bar.

- **Uranium mining.** A top-quarter position ({{cells.nuclear-extraction.cp}}) and {{cells.nuclear-extraction.share}} of world supply, but need and value sit below their bars: no domestic fence, and reliable supply to allies as an allied commitment.
- **Gas liquids processing and oil refining.** Large, commoditized yards, where Canada holds {{cells.gas-refining.share}} and {{cells.oil-refining.share}} of world supply.
- **Grid metals refining and grid components.** A commoditized yard and a low-need step ({{cells.electric-grid-components.need}}), yet both rank high in Canada's protection measures. Electrical steel reads Catch Up only by inheriting the step's export anchor; aluminium wire rod is a supplier role to keep reliable.
- **Wafers and PV cells.** Low need ({{cells.solar-components.need}}) and almost no position ({{cells.solar-components.cp}}); the cell layers read Catch Up only on that thin anchor.

## Canada's allied bargain

**What Canada contributes.** In the Allied Industrial Base analysis Canada is a {{aib.role}}, first among allies on {{aib.first_on}}. It supplies {{aib.contributes.nickel-intermediates-and-chemicals.share}} of world exports or production of nickel intermediates and chemicals and {{aib.contributes.aluminium-wire-rod.share}} of aluminium wire rod. It holds a core seat in {{aib.core_in}}, with {{aib.compacts.SC-NUCLEAR.lead_share}} of world exports or production of nuclear special materials: heavy water and enriched isotopes. The AIB also names it the largest allied holder of {{aib.division.nuclear-refining.name}} ({{aib.division.nuclear-refining.share}}, with {{aib.division.nuclear-refining.backups}} next), but that rests on concentrate exports, not conversion.

**What Canada needs.** Trusted refined rare earths and magnets, battery minerals and battery components. If {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.coercive_top}}'s supply stopped, as in the {{aib.scenarios.decoupling.label}} scenario, Canada would lose {{aib.lost_usd}} a year of imports across the Allied Industrial Base basket. New capacity at the best-placed allied hosts, led by {{aib.investment_decoupling.top_host}}, would restore about {{aib.investment_decoupling.total}} of it a year. Under {{aib.scenarios.managed.label}}, the default scenario, Canada sits in the minimum coalition of {{aib.ws_managed|nw}} Sectoral Compacts; under {{aib.scenarios.decoupling.label}}, {{aib.ws_decoupling|nw}}, each as a demand anchor.

## Strategic agenda — what should Canada do next?

### 1. Secure the rare-earth chain with allied partners

Build trusted separation, metal and magnet capacity with allies such as {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.partners}} through co-investment and offtake, and at home where a foothold can be built. Hold stocks sized to bridge the years until that supply qualifies, screen inbound investment in trusted capacity, and phase in limits on adversary sources in critical uses as it qualifies. No developing partner qualifies in this step; the AIB places new capacity in {{dev.by_step.electric-vehicles-refining.hosts}}, a commercial case.

### 2. Keep cell and pack making competitive and catch up in battery components

Expand cell and pack production with finance for expansion, workforce and supplier development, tied to benchmarks, with the gate open to allied and conditional investment. In battery components, where {{cells.batteries-components.top_supplier}} provides {{cells.batteries-components.china_share}} of imports, use time-limited production incentives and demand creation tied to benchmarks, at home and with allied partners.

### 3. Build battery materials refining on the nickel chemicals base

Canada's anchor is its export share of refined battery materials, corroborated in nickel chemicals by a supplier role in the AIB. Tie production incentives, first-of-a-kind finance and workforce development to benchmarks, with sunsets, and keep the gate open to licensing and joint ventures that transfer capability in lithium, cathode and anode materials.

### 4. Make mineral dependence deliberate and supplier roles reliable

Buy battery and EV minerals through offtake and co-investment with producers such as {{cells.batteries-extraction.partners}}, spread across several suppliers. Treat reliable supply of uranium, nickel chemicals and aluminium wire rod, and above all nuclear special materials, as an allied commitment, not a domestic fence. Source conversion and enrichment through co-investment and offtake with allied partners, such as {{aib.division.nuclear-refining.backups}}.

### 5. Build trusted battery and EV capacity with developing partners

Start with {{dev.rows.r3.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r3.capability}}, where Canadian need is high, leading with development finance, guarantees and offtake; {{dev.rows.r2.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r2.capability}} only supplements the home build. Add {{dev.rows.r5.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r5.capability}}, where recorded ties are deepest, and prepare {{dev.rows.r4.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r4.capability}} over a longer horizon. Leave coal-plant hardware to diversified contracts with allied makers. The objective is both an additional trusted supplier and a durable capability in the partner economy.

### 6. Accelerate gas networks, keep sourcing open and narrow protection

Deploy gas networks faster through infrastructure, permitting and competitive procurement, with trusted-supplier conditions on public deployment support. Build storage, grid, wind and solar without local-content rules, and narrow protection on wafers and PV cells, grid metals refining and grid components, which are Watch steps.

## Appendix — evidence and method

**How this brief is built.** Every number comes from the Country page data of the Allied Industrial Base Explorer (the data release of {{meta.as_of_label}}) and from the Allied Industrial Base analysis. Each supply-chain step gets a strategic choice by fixed rules applied in order. A step is Secure where an acute vulnerability is measured, and a deployment step Accelerate where deployment need reaches {{meta.bars.deployment}}. A large, commoditized yard is Watch. Preserve needs a top-quarter Competitive Position ({{meta.bars.cp}}) and high Strategic Industrial Value (bar {{meta.bars.prize}}) or supply-chain need (bar {{meta.bars.need}}). Catch Up needs high value, a lower position and a capability anchor corroborated by other evidence. Partner is high need with neither a top-quarter position nor, for a high-value step, a credible pathway; the rest is Watch.

Named layers, such as control software or magnets, take their own choice; a step whose parts sit behind different fences is mixed.

**Review cases.** Calls on insufficient evidence stay on the map but never enter the Bottom line, the Agenda, the policy table or a headline mark. The brief treats {{counts.review|nw}} steps as review cases.

**Evidence and caveats.** The Secure refining step and battery materials refining show the same {{cells.batteries-refining.coercive_top_share}} share of world supply, which suggests shared battery-metal supply data. The status "allied supply covers the need" for rare-earth and battery-metal refining describes the allied model, not ample trusted supply ({{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.trusted_share}} of world supply). In battery and EV mineral mining the trusted share reads {{cells.batteries-extraction.trusted_share}}, which conflicts with allied supply that covers the need; this brief relies on the partner list. {{counts.not_assessed|cap}} steps lack the data for a call, including most of geothermal, green hydrogen manufacturing and most recycling. Competitive Position ranks Canada among economies that already supply a step, so a zero means no measurable position. Partner lists name where allied capacity sits, which can include trading hubs, not who owns it.

**Development partnerships.** A developing partner (not high income) needs an anchor corroborated beyond context signals, sufficient evidence and confidence, and a place inside the AIB trust screen. On a step whose data describe different products, the anchor must measure the product the need is about. A partner that already hosts the step's new capacity in the AIB, or Preserves it, is a commercial case. Many codes name a stage rather than a technology, so aid at a step is not aid for that technology; export-credit finance is never counted as aid. In rare-earth refining, the other candidates rest on battery-metal capacity data, and {{dev.by_step.electric-vehicles-refining.review_thin_anchor}} on a thin anchor.
