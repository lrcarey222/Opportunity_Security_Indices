## Bottom line — Japan's strategic role

Japan's distinctive role in the Allied Industrial Base is as its specialist supplier and manufacturer: it holds a role in all {{aib.n_compact_roles|nw}} strategic compacts, and {{aib.recommended_economies}} economies count it as their best-placed trusted partner somewhere in the chain. Its exposure sits at the start of the chain: imported fuel, minerals it does not mine, and refined rare earths. Its industrial effort should go into battery materials refining and battery components, where its anchors are among the strongest and allies need new capacity, and into extending its positions in EV drivetrains, wind components and nuclear fuel. For minerals and wind materials it should depend deliberately on trusted producers, and its development cooperation can help developing partners add the battery and wind capacity allies lack. At home, deployment is as pressing: nuclear, solar and wind power need to be built faster. The high fence belongs around the rare-earth chain, fuel supply, and the control software that can command Japan's grid, batteries, inverters, turbines and vehicles.

### Low fence — what not to prioritize

A credible strategy also says what not to localize. Watch is not a failure: affordable, diversified access matters more than domestic production, and for Japan it often marks positions that compete without help.

- **Wafers and PV cells, and polysilicon refining.** Supply-chain need for wafers and cells is low ({{cells.solar-components.need}}), and polysilicon refining is a large, commoditized yard worth at most a stated capability floor. Wafers and cells rank among Japan's most protected steps in its own measures, but the trade codes include chip wafers and chip-machine parts, so some of that may be chip policy.
- **Gas turbines, LNG plant and their components.** Japan holds top-quarter positions ({{cells.gas-assembly.cp}} and {{cells.gas-components.cp}}) on modest need and low value; its makers compete for the gas build-out without a fence.
- **Grid metals refining and grid components.** Need is low in components ({{cells.electric-grid-components.need}}) but high in grid metals refining ({{cells.electric-grid-refining.need}}), a large, commoditized yard; the position in components is in the top quarter ({{cells.electric-grid-components.cp}}). Grain-oriented electrical steel reads Catch Up only by inheriting its step's position, while allies rely on Japan for transformer steel: a supplier role to keep reliable.

## Strategic agenda — what should Japan do next?

### 1. Secure the rare-earth chain with allied partners

Build trusted separation, metal-making and magnet capacity at home, where the base exists, and with allied partners such as {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.partners}} through co-investment and offtake. Hold stocks until that supply qualifies, and phase in limits on adversary sources in critical uses only as it does.

### 2. Hold fuel security through stocks and trusted contracts

Oil, gas and coal are Secure for resilience: they are essential and imported, and their supply takes {{cells.oil-extraction.reconstitution}} to rebuild. Size strategic stocks to that time, reserve capacity with trusted producers such as {{cells.oil-extraction.partners}} for oil (partner evidence is {{cells.oil-extraction.partner_evidence}}, so test the contract terms), and stress-test the loss of the largest supplier.

### 3. Catch up in battery materials and components with time-limited support tied to benchmarks

Tie production incentives, first-of-a-kind finance, workforce and supplier development and demand creation to benchmarks, with sunsets, and keep the gate open to licensing and joint ventures that transfer capability. Build battery components at home where the base is established and with allied partners for the rest.

### 4. Turn mineral dependence into investment bargains with trusted producers

Buy battery and EV minerals through offtake and co-investment with producers such as {{cells.batteries-extraction.partners}}, with export-credit finance for their capacity and contracts spread across several suppliers.

### 5. Build trusted battery and wind capacity with developing partners

Start with {{dev.rows.r2.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r2.capability}} and {{dev.rows.r3.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r3.capability}}. Test {{dev.rows.r4.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r4.capability}} first, where the data record no operating or committed plant and the step's trade codes also cover cables, substations and transformers. Prepare {{dev.rows.r1.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r1.capability}} over a longer horizon. Use project preparation, infrastructure and skills where the constraint is a public good, and development finance, guarantees and offtake where it is bankability.

### 6. Accelerate nuclear, solar and wind while keeping sourcing open

Deploy faster through infrastructure, permitting and procurement; buy commodity hardware competitively from trusted suppliers, and attach trusted-supplier and security conditions to public deployment support and to equipment with built-in control.

## Appendix — evidence and method

**How this brief is built.** Every number comes from the Country page data of the Allied Industrial Base Explorer (the data release of {{meta.as_of_label}}) and from the Allied Industrial Base analysis. Each technology is split into supply-chain stages from extraction to recycling, and each step gets a strategic choice by fixed rules applied in order:

- Secure where an acute vulnerability is measured;
- at deployment, Accelerate where deployment need reaches {{meta.bars.deployment}};
- Watch where the step is a large, commoditized yard;
- Preserve where Competitive Position is in the top quarter ({{meta.bars.cp}}) and either Strategic Industrial Value (bar {{meta.bars.prize}}) or supply-chain need (bar {{meta.bars.need}}) is high;
- Catch Up where the value is high, the position is not, and a capability anchor is corroborated by other evidence;
- Partner where supply-chain need is high but there is neither a top-quarter position nor, for a high-value step, a credible pathway;
- Watch otherwise.

Named layers inside a step, such as control software or magnets, take their own choice, and a step whose parts sit behind different fences is mixed. A value that rests on market size alone is capped just under the bar. Observed policy never decides a choice. The high fence (Secure) protects what cannot be allowed to fail; the medium fence covers capabilities worth building, keeping or sourcing deliberately and deployment to accelerate; the low fence (Watch) leaves a step open to competitive purchase.

**Review cases.** Some calls rest on low confidence, an engine review flag, a thin capability anchor, a known data artifact or, at deployment, a single indicator of low confidence. They stay on the map but never enter the Bottom line, the Agenda, the policy table or a headline mark. {{counts.review|cap}} steps are review cases. For Japan several sit close to the top-quarter bar, where the line between Preserve and Catch Up is thin.

**Evidence and caveats.** The rare-earth chokepoint rests on measured supply concentration, and the battery-materials Catch Up has {{cells.batteries-refining.confidence}} confidence. They overstate its position in PV cells and modules. The wind turbines step's trade codes also cover cables, substations and transformers, and battery recycling's are battery waste, scrap and metal residues. Recorded policy ranks electrolyser stacks ({{cells.green-hydrogen-components.policy_priority}}) and systems ({{cells.green-hydrogen-assembly.policy_priority}}) near the top of Japan's own measures, but those steps cannot be assessed and deployment need reads high on a single indicator, so this brief does not settle that disagreement. Several review cases also read as needing more policy attention: {{cells.nuclear-assembly.name}}, {{cells.electric-vehicles-assembly.name}}, and {{cells.electric-grid-assembly.name}}. **Development partnerships.** A pair joins a Japanese step with a strategic reason for more allied capacity and a developing economy (not high income) whose same step is a Catch Up. The partner needs an anchor corroborated beyond context signals, sufficient evidence and confidence, and a place inside the AIB trust screen; on a step whose data describe different products, the anchor must measure the product the need is about. A partner that already hosts the step's new capacity in the AIB, or Preserves it, is a commercial case. A constraint on a partner is named only where the data observe its input; otherwise the table says the data do not cover it. In the relationship table an observed zero is stated as nothing recorded, data that do not cover a pair are left out, and the direct investment position is economy-wide, not tied to the step.
