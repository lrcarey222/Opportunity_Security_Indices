## Bottom line — India's strategic role

India is a coalition partner of the Allied Industrial Base, not a core member, yet it already holds a part in almost all of it. It holds a role in {{aib.n_compact_roles|nw}} of the {{aib.n_compacts|nw}} strategic compacts and a core seat in {{aib.core_in}}. Its distinctive contribution is clean-energy equipment, where it ranks in the top quarter of suppliers although its share of world supply is small ({{cells.wind-assembly.share}} in wind turbines, {{cells.solar-assembly.share}} in PV modules and inverters). Allies rely on it for {{aib.contributes.wind-blades-and-hubs.share}} of world exports or production of wind blades and hubs, and it holds top-quarter positions in wind turbines, PV modules and EV drivetrains that it should preserve. Its main industrial build is batteries, where cells, packs and components rest on committed investment and {{cells.batteries-assembly.top_supplier}} supplies {{cells.batteries-assembly.china_share}} of cell and pack imports. Development partners with their own need for trusted battery supply can help that build reach scale. The fence that matters is high but narrow: the rare-earth chain, and the control software of the grid, inverters and batteries India installs. The larger task is deployment: storage, solar, wind, grid and nuclear power all need to be built faster.

### Medium fence — where industrial capability matters

Each step turns on whether the capability is worth having (Strategic Industrial Value) and how strong India is today (Competitive Position).

**Preserve.** {{cells.wind-assembly.name|cap}} and {{cells.wind-components.name}} hold top-quarter positions, and allies treat India as an essential supplier of blades and hubs. {{cells.solar-assembly.name|cap}} also sit in the top quarter, with operating capacity and committed investment behind them. {{cells.electric-vehicles-components.name|cap}} are strong; {{cells.electric-vehicles-assembly.name}} sits on the top-quarter line and holds no role in the {{aib.all_compacts.SC-EV}} compact, so it serves the home market more than allies. {{cells.coal-assembly.name|cap}} also reads Preserve. {{cells.nuclear-assembly.name|cap}} read Preserve too, but their trade basket mixes reactor lines with generic boiler parts, so the call is not evidence of a reactor industry. {{cells.solar-refining.name|cap}} reads Preserve but is {{cells.solar-refining.review}}, a review case listed in the appendix.

**Catch Up.** {{cells.batteries-assembly.name|cap}} is the clearest case for building: high value and a position just below the top quarter among current suppliers (India supplies {{cells.batteries-assembly.share}} of world supply). Its committed investment is corroborated by {{cells.batteries-assembly.supports}}, with {{cells.batteries-assembly.confidence}} confidence. {{cells.batteries-components.name|cap}} rest on the same kind of anchor, with less recorded policy behind them. Both are mixed in delivery: build at home where the base is established and with allied partners for the rest. {{cells.electric-grid-assembly.name|cap}} also read Catch Up, though their value sits just above the bar, and {{cells.wind-refining.name}} is the Catch Up part of the Secure rare-earth chain. Several other Catch Up calls should not carry the strategy. {{cells.batteries-refining.name|cap}} rests on {{cells.batteries-refining.export_share}} of world exports with no plant in the capacity data. {{cells.nuclear-components.name|cap}} rests on the same mixed boiler basket as reactors, and {{cells.batteries-recovery.name}} on no measured position.

**Partner.** India's Partner steps sit at the start of the chain. It holds no measurable position in battery mineral mining and only a weak one in EV mineral mining ({{cells.electric-vehicles-extraction.cp}} out of a hundred). ### Low fence — what not to prioritize

Watch is not a failure: it means affordable, diversified access matters more than domestic production.

- **Uranium conversion and enrichment.** Nuclear power must grow, but these steps have low supply-chain need and no capability anchor, and trusted suppliers hold {{cells.nuclear-refining.trusted_share}} of world supply. Keep access diversified.
- **Gas turbines, LNG plant and their components.** Gas networks read Accelerate, but the equipment behind them has low supply-chain need and is best bought competitively.
- **Grid metals refining.** Copper and aluminium processing is a commodity step with a capability floor.
- **Coal, copper and bauxite mining.** These are commodity steps: coal is best diversified toward trusted producers, and copper and bauxite need at most a specified capability floor.

## India's allied bargain

**What India contributes.** In the Allied Industrial Base analysis it is a {{aib.role|lower}}: a demand anchor and bridge in most compacts, with supplier seats that allies cannot easily replace in wind, nuclear and industrial electrification. It holds {{aib.contributes.wind-blades-and-hubs.share}} of world exports or production of wind blades and hubs, {{aib.contributes.overhead-conductors.share}} of overhead conductors and {{aib.contributes.direct-reduced-iron-dri-hbi.share}} of direct-reduced iron. Its nuclear seat rests on {{aib.compacts.SC-NUCLEAR.lead_share}} of nuclear special materials, a trade line with no physical data. Under {{aib.scenarios.managed.label}}, the default scenario, India sits in the minimum coalition of {{aib.ws_managed|nw}} Sectoral Compacts, the AIB's finer workstreams within the strategic compacts. Under {{aib.scenarios.decoupling.label}} it sits in {{aib.ws_decoupling|nw}}, as a demand anchor in {{aib.ws_demand_anchor|nw}}.

**What India needs.** Minerals, refined inputs and battery capability. If {{cells.batteries-assembly.coercive_top}}'s supply stopped, as in the {{aib.scenarios.decoupling.label}} scenario, India would lose {{aib.lost_usd}} a year of imports across the Allied Industrial Base basket. New capacity at the best-placed allied hosts, led by {{aib.investment_decoupling.top_host}}, would restore about {{aib.investment_decoupling.total}} of it a year. That counts new capacity only, not trade redirected to existing allied suppliers, so it is not a measure of how much of the loss allies could cover. The allies with the most supply restored to and from India by new and expanded trade are {{aib.partners}}. The count runs in both directions, so it includes buyers of India's output as well as suppliers.

**The dependencies to make intentional.** These bargains turn that exchange into a specialization partners can count on.

## Strategic agenda — what should India do next?

### 1. Secure the rare-earth chain through a mixed domestic and allied build-out

Build separation and metal-making at home, starting from a small export foothold (about {{cells.wind-refining.export_share}} of world exports, with no measurable share of world supply) and testing it with feasibility work before committing capacity. Build the step to magnets with allied makers such as {{aib.compacts.SC-MAGNETS.core_makers}} through co-investment and offtake. Hold stocks sized to the time it takes to restore supply until trusted supply qualifies. Recorded policy barely reaches this step today, so it is the first gap to close.

### 2. Catch up in battery cells and components with time-limited support tied to benchmarks

Tie production incentives, first-of-a-kind finance, workforce and supplier development and demand creation to benchmarks, and let support sunset if the gap does not narrow. Keep the gate open to joint ventures on terms that transfer capability. Bring support for components up to the level of cells.

### 3. Keep the wind, solar and EV equipment base competitive as India's allied contribution

Wind turbines, blades, PV modules and EV drivetrains are positions to hold, not to rebuild. Back them with R&D, workforce and supplier development and finance for expansion tied to benchmarks, and welcome allied investment in new blade capacity (in the AIB, {{aib.matrix.SC-WIND.host.text|lower}}).

### 4. Put a high, narrow fence around control software

Set trusted-source and certification rules for battery management, inverter firmware, smart meters, grid control, turbine controllers, connected vehicles and reactor control. Restrict remote access and over-the-air updates by untrusted vendors, and replace untrusted control software in the installed base. Whether India's own equipment makers can supply the certified alternative is not measured here and should be tested first.

### 5. Use development partnerships to bring battery cells and components to scale

Start with partners that share the need, such as {{dev.rows.r3.partners}} for {{dev.rows.r3.capability}} and {{dev.rows.r2.partners}} for {{dev.rows.r2.capability}}. Use project preparation, supplier development, skills and certification first, then development finance, guarantees, offtake and joint ventures as plants come on line. The objective is a trusted second source for partners and a durable industry at home.

### 6. Accelerate storage, solar, wind, grid and nuclear power while keeping sourcing open

Deploy faster through infrastructure, permitting, interoperability and procurement; buy commodity hardware competitively from trusted suppliers, with trusted-supplier and security conditions on public deployment support and connected equipment. Gas turbines, conversion and enrichment, and coal and copper mining are not localization priorities: affordable, diversified access is.

## Appendix — evidence and method

**How this brief is built.** Every number comes from the Country page data of the Allied Industrial Base Explorer (the data release of {{meta.as_of_label}}) and from the Allied Industrial Base analysis. Each technology is split into supply-chain stages, from mining to recovery, and each step gets a strategic choice by fixed rules applied in order:

- Secure where an acute vulnerability is measured;
- at deployment, Accelerate where deployment need reaches {{meta.bars.deployment}};
- Watch where the step is a large, commoditized yard;
- Preserve where Competitive Position is in the top quarter ({{meta.bars.cp}}) and either Strategic Industrial Value (bar {{meta.bars.prize}}) or supply-chain need (bar {{meta.bars.need}}) is high;
- Catch Up where the value is high, the position is not, and a capability anchor is corroborated by other evidence;
- Partner where supply-chain need is high but there is neither a top-quarter position nor, for a high-value step, a credible pathway;
- Watch otherwise.

Named layers inside a step, such as control software or magnets, take their own choice, and a step whose parts sit behind different fences is mixed. A value that rests on market size alone is capped just under the bar. Observed policy never decides a choice. The high fence (Secure) protects what cannot be allowed to fail; the medium fence covers capabilities worth building, keeping or sourcing deliberately and deployment to accelerate; the low fence (Watch) leaves a step open to competitive purchase.

**Review cases.** Some calls rest on low confidence, an engine review flag, a thin capability anchor, a known data artifact or, at deployment, a single indicator of low confidence. They stay on the map but never enter the Bottom line, the Agenda, the policy table or a headline mark. {{counts.review|cap}} steps are review cases.
