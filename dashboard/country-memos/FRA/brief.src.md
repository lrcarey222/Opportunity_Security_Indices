## Bottom line — France's strategic role

France's distinctive role in the Allied Industrial Base is as its nuclear specialist and a broad, mid-sized equipment maker. Allies rely on it for reactor parts, {{aib.contributes.reactor-parts.share}} of world exports or production, and it holds core seats in {{aib.core_in}}. Elsewhere its roles are substitutable, and the AIB assigns it no primary strategic role at its current thresholds. Its exposure is imported fuel and the control of imported equipment: oil and gas, grid and battery software, rare-earth magnets and transformer steel sit behind the High fence. Its industrial effort should keep enrichment, wind parts and battery components competitive and catch up in EVs, drivetrains and battery cells, where allies also need new capacity. Most of its manufacturing positions are a few percent of world supply, so catching up should go with allied makers. The nuclear manufacturing calls need care, because their trade baskets mix reactor parts with boiler and steam-turbine parts. At home only EV adoption needs a faster build-out, and France's own need gives its development cooperation a narrow base, battery cells.

:::choices
- **Secure.** Oil and gas supply, which takes {{cells.oil-extraction.reconstitution}} to rebuild; potentially coercive suppliers provide {{cells.oil-extraction.coercive_import_share}} of oil imports. The control software of battery, grid and inverter equipment. Rare-earth magnets for EV motors, where {{cells.electric-vehicles-refining.coercive_top}} leads world rare-earth refining, and grain-oriented electrical steel for transformers. The answer is trusted contracts, stocks, qualified suppliers and control of command paths, not self-sufficiency.
- **Build / preserve.** Preserve uranium enrichment, on {{cells.nuclear-refining.export_share}} of world exports of enriched uranium, wind parts, and battery components. Catch Up in EVs, EV drivetrain components and battery cells and packs, with allied makers where the base is thin. Judge the nuclear parts and reactor Catch Up calls on reactor-specific measures.
- **Partner.** Battery and EV minerals from trusted producers such as {{cells.batteries-extraction.partners}}; allied supply covers the need. With developing partners, add trusted battery-cell capacity through development finance and offtake.
- **Deploy / stay open.** Accelerate EV adoption and charging, buying equipment competitively from trusted suppliers, with security conditions on connected equipment. Leave open wafers and PV cells, polysilicon, plant equipment for gas, oil and coal, and uranium ore, bought from a diversified base of trusted producers.

### Low fence — what not to prioritize

A credible strategy also says what not to localize. Watch is not a failure: it means affordable, diversified access matters more than domestic production.

- **Wafers and PV cells, and polysilicon refining.** Need for wafers and cells is low ({{cells.solar-components.need}}), their trade codes include chip-industry codes, and their cell layers read Catch Up only on inherited anchors. France has no measurable position in polysilicon refining ({{cells.solar-refining.cp}}), and its solar-grade layer's Catch Up rests on the step's export share alone.
- **Gas, oil and coal plant equipment.** Need is low, value below the bar and positions below the top quarter. Yet {{aib.recommended_by.coal-assembly.n}} economies count France as their best-placed trusted partner for {{cells.coal-assembly.name}}, where it holds {{cells.coal-assembly.share}} of world supply: a supplier role to keep reliable through competition, not a fence.
- **Uranium mining.** Need is very high ({{cells.nuclear-extraction.need}}), and {{cells.nuclear-extraction.top_supplier}}, the largest potentially coercive supplier, provides {{cells.nuclear-extraction.top_supplier_share}} of imports. Yet the step reads Watch, a large, commoditized yard where France has no mines: buy from a diversified base of trusted producers.

## France's allied bargain

**What France contributes.** The Allied Industrial Base analysis assigns France no primary strategic role at its current thresholds. It holds a role in {{aib.n_compact_roles|nw}} of the {{aib.n_compacts|nw}} strategic compacts, most of them substitutable. Its supply is essential where it holds core seats, in {{aib.core_in}}: {{aib.contributes.reactor-parts.share}} of world exports or production of reactor parts and {{aib.compacts.SC-EV.lead_share}} of electric buses, trucks and tractors. It also supplies {{aib.contributes.uranium-conversion-and-enrichment.share}} of uranium conversion and enrichment products, natural uranium compounds included. {{aib.recommended_economies}} economies count France as their best-placed trusted partner somewhere in the chain, {{aib.recommended_by.coal-assembly.n}} of them for coal-plant equipment.

**What France needs.** Fuel, minerals, magnets and battery cells. If {{cells.batteries-assembly.coercive_top}}'s supply stopped, as in the {{aib.scenarios.decoupling.label}} scenario, France would lose {{aib.lost_usd}} a year of imports across the Allied Industrial Base basket. New capacity at allied hosts, led by {{aib.investment_decoupling.top_host}}, would restore only about {{aib.investment_decoupling.total}} of it a year. ## Strategic agenda — what should France do next?

### 1. Secure fuel supply and the grid's command layer

Size oil and gas stocks to the rebuild time. Reserve capacity with trusted producers such as {{cells.oil-extraction.partners}} for oil and {{cells.gas-extraction.partners}} for gas; partner evidence is {{cells.oil-extraction.partner_evidence}}, so test the terms. Apply trusted-source and certification rules to battery, inverter, metering and grid-control software, and reserve transformer steel with trusted steelmakers.

### 2. Keep enrichment competitive as an allied supply, and diversify uranium

Keep enrichment, the nuclear position the data measure most directly, at the frontier with public finance and allied investment tied to benchmarks, so allies can move away from {{cells.nuclear-refining.coercive_top}}'s supply. Buy uranium from diversified trusted producers.

### 3. Catch up in EVs, drivetrains and battery cells, with support tied to benchmarks

Tie production incentives, workforce development and demand creation to benchmarks, with sunsets. Build at home where the base is established and with allied makers such as {{aib.compacts.SC-EV.core_makers}} for the rest. Source magnets through offtake and allied agreements that add capacity, since the AIB finds {{aib.matrix.SC-MAGNETS.relies.text}}; hold stocks until trusted supply qualifies.

### 4. Build trusted battery-cell capacity with developing partners

Start with {{dev.rows.r1.partner}}, the own-need partner, checking first why the capacity data show no plant; lead with development finance, guarantees and offtake. Pursue allied-gap pairs, such as {{dev.rows.r3.partner}} in {{dev.rows.r3.capability}}, jointly with allies.

### 5. Accelerate EV adoption while keeping sourcing open

Deploy faster through charging infrastructure, permitting and procurement, buying from trusted suppliers with security conditions on connected equipment. Keep local-content protection on storage equipment time-limited and tied to Catch Up benchmarks, with sunsets, and hold wind equipment to targeted remedies and conditional procurement.

## Appendix — evidence and method

**How this brief is built.** Every number comes from the Country page data of the Allied Industrial Base Explorer (the data release of {{meta.as_of_label}}) and from the Allied Industrial Base analysis. Each technology is split into supply-chain stages from extraction to recycling, and each step gets a strategic choice by fixed rules applied in order:

- Secure where an acute vulnerability is measured;
- at deployment, Accelerate where deployment need reaches {{meta.bars.deployment}};
- Watch where the step is a large, commoditized yard;
- Preserve where Competitive Position is in the top quarter ({{meta.bars.cp}}) and either Strategic Industrial Value (bar {{meta.bars.prize}}) or supply-chain need (bar {{meta.bars.need}}) is high;
- Catch Up where the value is high, the position is not, and a capability anchor is corroborated by other evidence;
- Partner where supply-chain need is high but there is neither a top-quarter position nor, for a high-value step, a credible pathway;
- Watch otherwise.

Named layers inside a step, such as control software or magnets, take their own choice, and a step whose parts sit behind different fences is mixed. A value that rests on market size alone is capped just under the bar. Observed policy never decides a choice. The high fence (Secure) protects what cannot be allowed to fail; the medium fence covers capabilities worth building, keeping or sourcing deliberately and deployment to accelerate; the low fence (Watch) leaves a step open to competitive purchase.

**Review cases.** Some calls rest on low confidence, an engine review flag, a thin capability anchor, a known data artifact or, at deployment, a single indicator of low confidence. They stay on the map but never enter the Bottom line, the Agenda, the policy table or a headline mark. {{counts.review|cap}} steps are review cases.

**Evidence and caveats.** The oil and gas calls rest on measured import dependence and supplier concentration, and the EV and battery-cell Catch Up calls have {{cells.batteries-assembly.confidence}} confidence. The enrichment Preserve rests on exports of enriched uranium; its supply share of {{cells.nuclear-refining.share}} is a share in uranium compounds, yellowcake included, and the AIB's measure of {{aib.contributes.uranium-conversion-and-enrichment.share}} also covers natural uranium compounds. Natural uranium hexafluoride, the output of conversion, trades with yellowcake under the mining step's codes, so conversion has no measure of its own. The baskets of nuclear fuel and reactor parts and of reactors mix reactor parts with generic boiler and steam-turbine parts, the kind of equipment that also fills the coal-plant steps. The wind turbines step's trade codes are mostly cables, transformers and switchgear; battery recycling's are battery waste, scrap and residues; rare-earth and wind materials refining's are mostly copper wire rod by value. The solar wafer and module baskets include chip-industry codes, and the Catch Up layers inside the Watch solar steps inherit their steps' anchors. Rare-earth and battery-metal refining and battery materials refining show the identical {{cells.batteries-refining.coercive_top}} share of world supply, which suggests overlapping trade codes. Grain-oriented electrical steel reads Secure on the measures of its step, which cover grid metals as a whole; sintered magnets read Secure in EV drivetrains and Preserve in wind components, each on its step's measures. Uranium mining reads Watch as a large yard despite a need of {{cells.nuclear-extraction.need}}. {{counts.not_assessed|cap}} steps lack the data for a call, including geothermal supply, green hydrogen manufacturing and most recycling. Recorded policy ranks electrolyser systems ({{cells.green-hydrogen-assembly.policy_priority}}) and stacks ({{cells.green-hydrogen-components.policy_priority}}) near the top of France's own measures, but those steps cannot be assessed and hydrogen deployment need rests on a single indicator, so this brief does not settle that disagreement. Import shares follow the trading partner, so a hub such as {{cells.gas-extraction.top_supplier}} for gas can stand for the producers behind it. Competitive Position ranks France among economies that already supply a step, so a zero means no measurable position. Partner lists name where allied capacity sits, which can include trading hubs, not who owns it.

**Development partnerships.** A pair joins a French step with a strategic reason for more allied capacity and a developing economy (not high income) whose same step is a Catch Up. The reason is France's own need, or the allied gap alone, where allies need new capacity, which is the same for every donor in scope. The partner needs an anchor corroborated beyond context signals, sufficient evidence and confidence, and a place inside the AIB trust screen; on a step whose data describe different products, the anchor must measure the product the need is about. A partner that already hosts the step's new capacity in the AIB, or Preserves it, is a commercial case. Aid comes from the OECD Creditor Reporting System by purpose code and covers energy, industry and mining aid only. Components and Assembly share codes, no code funds recycling, and many codes name a stage rather than a technology, so aid at a step is not aid for that technology. Aid is called technology-coded only where codes naming the technology hold at least half of it, and a partner with several steps can sit in several aid groups. A constraint on a partner is named only where the data observe its input; otherwise the table says the data do not cover it. In the relationship table an observed zero is stated as nothing recorded, data that do not cover a pair are left out, and the direct investment position is economy-wide, not tied to the step. Export-credit finance is reported apart and never counted as aid, and direct investment uses the IMF positions (the partner's reports where France reports none). The full list of pairs, with every field, is in the development-partnership export.
