\section{Allied Industrial Base: formulations}
\label{app:algorithm}

This appendix states the programs behind Section~\ref{sec:network}. The functions of
\path{R/aib/} read and write no files; all input and output is in
\path{scripts/31_build_aib_trade_network.R} and \path{scripts/32_build_aib_partnerships.R}, and
every setting is in \path{config/aib.yml} (Table~\ref{tab:alg_params}). Every program is solved
with GLPK \citep{makhorin2020glpk}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The node problem}
\label{subsec:alg_node}

Fix a node $n$ and a shock. Let $v_{em}\ge0$ be the reconciled 2024 flow from exporter $e$ to importer
$m$ (USD; flows under USD 10{,}000 are not relationships), $W=\sum_{e,m}v_{em}$ world trade in the node,
$\rho_e\in[0,1]$ the fraction of $e$'s supply removed (1 for China, Hong Kong and Macao under the
headline) and $X$ the shocked economies. The demand scope is $M=\{m\in\mathcal M_0\setminus X:
\sum_e v_{em}>0\}$, where $\mathcal M_0$ is the configured set (the 57 coalition members), and the
candidates are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg_candidates}
  E=\Bigl\{e\in\mathcal E_0\setminus X:\ \textstyle\sum_m v_{em}\ \ge\ \max\{10^{6},\ 5\times10^{-4}\,W\}\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal E_0$ the candidate universe (every economy less the open ship registries). All
quantities are divided by the scope's demand $D=\sum_{m\in M}\sum_e v_{em}$, so that coverage is a share
of $D$. For $m\in M$ let $d_m=\sum_e v_{em}/D$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg_fixed}
  F_m=\frac1D\sum_{e\notin E}(1-\rho_e)\,v_{em},\qquad F=\sum_{m\in M}F_m,\qquad
  L=\frac1D\sum_{m\in M}\sum_e\rho_e v_{em},
\end{equation}
the shipments of non-candidates, which stay where they are, and the lost supply; shock exposure is $L$
and the static coverage of state A is $C^{\mathrm A}=1-L$. For a candidate $e$, $b_{em}=v_{em}/D$ is
its current shipment to $m\in M$, $b_e=\sum_{m\in M}b_{em}$, $o_e$ its exports to markets outside
$M\cup X$ and $\phi_e=\sum_{x\in X}\rho_x v_{ex}/D$ the supply freed by a release shock ($\phi_e=0$
under hold). Its available supply is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg_avail}
  a_e=\min\Bigl\{\,b_e+\phi_e+\1[\text{reallocation}]\,o_e,\ \ \bar a_e\Bigr\},\qquad
  \bar a_e=\begin{cases}S_e/D & \text{if $e$ is a listed producer},\\ b_e & \text{if not},\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $S_e$ is $e$'s world exports in the node and $\bar a_e=\infty$ in nodes without credible physical
data or in the trade-only mode. A listed producer has a production or capacity share above zero in a
proxy that covers at least 80 percent of world output.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The trade program (states B and C)}
\label{subsec:alg_trade}

The decision variables are flows $f_{em}\ge0$ on an arc set $\mathcal A$: in state C, every pair
$E\times M$; in state B, the observed relationships $\{(e,m):b_{em}>0\}$ plus $(e,e)$ for candidates in
the scope (keeping exports at home). In the reallocation scenario each surviving relationship $k=(e,m)$
with $b_k>0$ also carries a cut $u_k\ge0$. With a maximum supplier share $\kappa$ (the headline has
$\kappa=1$; the frontier uses 0.25, 0.40 and 0.60) and the growth bound $\eta$ of state B ($\eta=1$),
the feasible set is
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:aib_lp}
\begin{align}
  & \textstyle\sum_{m}f_{em}\le a_e && \forall e\in E, \label{eq:aib_supply}\\
  & \textstyle\sum_{e}f_{em}\le d_m-F_m && \forall m\in M, \label{eq:aib_demand}\\
  & f_{em}\ge b_{em} && \text{(conservative)}, \label{eq:aib_lock}\\
  & u_k\ge b_k-f_k,\quad 0\le u_k\le b_k && \text{(reallocation)}, \label{eq:aib_cut}\\
  & f_{em}\le(1+\eta)\,b_{em} && \text{(state B, } b_{em}>0), \label{eq:aib_growth}\\
  & \textstyle\sum_{m}f_{em}\ \le\ \kappa\,\bigl(F+\sum_{e',m}f_{e'm}\bigr) && \forall e\in E \ \text{(if } \kappa<1). \label{eq:aib_cap}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Where a supplier's locked shipments already exceed $\kappa$ of the locked total, its flows are fixed at
their lower bounds instead of \eqref{eq:aib_cap}. Coverage is $C=F+\sum_{e,m}f_{em}$. The program is
solved lexicographically: with objectives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aib_objectives}
  z_1=\sum f_{em},\qquad z_{1b}=-\sum_k u_k,\qquad z_2=\sum w_e f_{em},\qquad
  z_3=\sum \PO_{em}f_{em},\qquad z_4=\sum \chi_{em}f_{em},
\end{equation}
step $j$ maximizes $z_j$ subject to \eqref{eq:aib_lp} and to $z_i\ge z_i^{*}-\max\{\epsilon_i|z_i^{*}|,
10^{-9}\}$ for every earlier step $i$, with tolerances $\epsilon_1=5\times10^{-4}$,
$\epsilon_{1b}=10^{-6}$ and $\epsilon_2=\epsilon_3=0.02$. The specialization weight is
$w_e=\max\{\min(\RCA_e,4)/4,\ \pi_e\}+10^{-3}\sigma_e$, with $\pi_e$ the production share where a proxy
exists and $\sigma_e$ the world export share; $\PO_{em}$ is Export Opportunity from $e$ to $m$ and
$\chi_{em}$ the mean of the available policy and climate similarities; missing scores are
0.5, and a domestic arc scores 0.5 on $\PO$ and 1 on compatibility. The coordination dividend is
$C^{\mathrm C}-C^{\mathrm B}$ at $\kappa=1$; diversion is $\sum_e\max\{0,\sum_m f_{em}-b_e-\phi_e\}$.

\paragraph{Closed form of state C.} In state C every candidate reaches every importer, so the program
decomposes at the exporter level: coverage is the largest $T$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aib_closed}
  T\ \le\ F+\sum_{e\in E}\min\{a_e,\ \kappa T\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad T\ \le\ F+\sum_{m\in M}(d_m-F_m),
\end{equation}
found by bisection since the gap between the two sides is concave in $T$; the stability step then
keeps surviving shipments up to the limit and the specialization step fills the rest from the
exporters with the highest $w_e$. Objectives 3 and 4 decide only which importer each unit goes to.
The closed form agrees with the LP on coverage and exporter totals (unit tests) and is used for the
frontier, the investment simulation and the coalition test; the LP is solved where the importer
allocation is reported.

\paragraph{Frontier and N-1.} For each $\kappa\in\{0.25,0.40,0.60,1\}$ the frontier records coverage,
concentration and two N-1 readings: the instantaneous one, $C\,(1-s_{\max})$ with $s_{\max}$ the largest
supplier's share of the optimized supply, and the re-optimized one, in which the largest remaining
supplier is removed from the problem (its shipments become lost supply and it ceases to be a candidate)
and state C is solved again at the same $\kappa$. The tightest costless limit is the smallest $\kappa$
whose coverage is within two points of the unconstrained optimum; the minimum required diversification
is the loosest $\kappa$ at which the re-optimized N-1 coverage reaches 80 percent (none if no tested
limit reaches it).

\subsection{The investment step}
\label{subsec:alg_investment}

A node enters the simulation under a shock if its residual gap $1-C^{\mathrm C}$ is at least 5
percent. Host $h$ is eligible if it is a candidate universe member outside the open registries and
satisfies at least one of: $\RCA_h\ge1$ on exports of at least USD 50 million or 1 percent of $W$; a
production share of at least 1 percent; a world export share of at least 0.5 percent; or $\RCA_h\ge1$
at that scale in a node joined to $n$ by a material link. The increment $\delta\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$
(the headline is 0.10) is added to $a_h$ (a host that is not a candidate joins the program with $\delta$
only), and state C is re-solved:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aib_gain}
  g_h(\delta)=C^{\mathrm C}\bigl(a+\delta\,\mathbf e_h\bigr)-C^{\mathrm C}(a),
\end{equation}
both at $\kappa=1$ and at the diversified limit $\kappa=0.40$. Without a limit every host's gain is
$\min\{\delta,1-C^{\mathrm C}\}$, so hosts are ordered by $g_h$ at $\kappa=0.40$, then by the fall in
the Herfindahl index of supply, then by capability tier and value (the larger of the host's world
export share and production share), then by Friendshore from the importers that lost supply,
weighted by their loss, then by policy compatibility; gains within 0.5 points of the best and
Herfindahl changes within 0.005 of the lowest are ties. For the five best hosts at $\delta=0.10$, with
every other flow fixed at the optimized allocation, the lexicographic optimum of the new capacity is
greedy: it fills the importers' unmet demand $d_m-F_m-\sum_e f_{em}$ in descending $\PO_{hm}$, then
compatibility. Each importer served is an investor of the pair $(m,h)$; the emergent network keeps
the first-ranked host only, turned round as $h\to m$.

\subsection{Grouping and the exact minimum coalition}
\label{subsec:alg_coalition}

\paragraph{Grouping.} For intervention nodes $n,n'$ in the strategic scope, the distance is
$1-\mathrm{sim}_{nn'}$ if the pair passes chain coherence (one node within three directed links of the
other in the curated chain, or both feeding the same node directly) and policy coherence (cosine
similarity of the need profiles at least 0.5), and 10 otherwise, where $\mathrm{sim}_{nn'}$ is the larger
of the cosine similarity of the nodes' recurring supplier vectors and of one node's suppliers with the
other's buyers. Complete-linkage clustering cut at $1-0.35$ gives groups in which every pair passes,
and each group is split into the connected pieces of the chain (linking through nodes that belong to no
group).

\paragraph{Anchors and coverage.} The demand anchors $A$ are the importers holding 60 percent of the
group's lost supply, at most six. In node $n$, with $d^A_n=\sum_{m\in A}d_m$, the anchors' coverage when
the members $S$ coordinate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aib_anchor}
  C^A_n(S)=\min\Bigl\{1,\ \bigl(F^A_n+\textstyle\sum_{c\in S}g_{nc}\bigr)/d^A_n\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $F^A_n$ is what reaches the anchors with nobody coordinating (non-candidates' shipments and every
candidate's current shipments to them) and $g_{nc}$ what candidate $c$ adds by coordinating: in the
reallocation scenario $\max\{0,a_c-b^A_c\}$, its available supply less its current shipments to the
anchors; in the conservative scenario its freed supply within its physical room; plus $\delta=0.10$ if
$c$ is one of the two leading hosts of a node that needs capacity.

\paragraph{The binary program.} With $y_c\in\{0,1\}$ for every candidate and the target $\tau$ (the
headline is 0.95; 0.90 and 0.99 are also solved),
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:aib_coalition}
\begin{align}
  k^{*}=\min_{y}\ & \textstyle\sum_c y_c \label{eq:aib_coal_obj}\\
  \text{s.t.}\ & F^A_n+\textstyle\sum_c g_{nc}\,y_c\ \ge\ \tau\,d^A_n && \forall n\in\text{group},\label{eq:aib_coal_target}\\
  & y_c=1 && \forall c\in A. \label{eq:aib_coal_anchor}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The coverage cap in \eqref{eq:aib_anchor} does not bind at a target below one, so the program is
exact. The robust tie-break then solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aib_robust}
  \max_{y,\zeta}\ \zeta\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \frac{F^A_n+\sum_c g_{nc}y_c}{d^A_n}-\zeta\ \ge\ \tau\ \ \forall n,\qquad
  \textstyle\sum_c y_c=k^{*},\qquad y_c=1\ \forall c\in A,\qquad 0\le\zeta\le1-\tau,
\end{equation}
the largest smallest margin over the target among coalitions of the minimum size, and a third program
maximizes $\sum_n\sum_c g_{nc}y_c/d^A_n$ with $\zeta$ held at its optimum. Each solve has a time limit;
a solve that reaches it keeps its best feasible solution and is reported. Where even every candidate
cannot reach $\tau$ in some node, the coalition is the widest greedy set and is labelled so.

\paragraph{Tiers.} For each member $c$ of the 95 percent coalition $S^{*}$, $c$ is needed if
$\min_nC^A_n(S^{*}\setminus\{c\})<\tau$ with $c$'s supply removed; it is a critical supply partner if,
in addition, no single non-member $c'$ restores the target,
$\min_nC^A_n(S^{*}\setminus\{c\}\cup\{c'\})<\tau$ for every $c'$. The associates solve
\eqref{eq:aib_coalition} at $\tau=0.99$ with $S^{*}$ held in. A non-member $x$ is a critical external
dependency if removing its current shipments to the anchors pushes some node below $\tau$ with $S^{*}$
fixed (or, where a node is already below it, lowers its coverage by a point or more) and it supplies at
least 1 percent of the anchors' demand. A group is a candidate compact if
$\min_n C^A_n(S^{*})-C^{A,\mathrm B}_n\ge0.05$ for its anchors' existing-relationship coverage
$C^{A,\mathrm B}$, and a bilateral arrangement if $|S^{*}|<3$.
