\begin{abstract}
\noindent No allied economy can efficiently reproduce the whole supply chain of energy
technologies, so protection against concentrated suppliers must come from capabilities spread
across trusted partners: managed interdependence rather than autarky. We build a framework to
measure it and to stress-test it. Energy-security and economic-opportunity pillars score 219
economies in up to 33 technology--stage cells. A Strategic Index reads security as need, and an
endowment screen admits extraction stages to a member's priority only where it has a resource
base. A 57-member coalition is derived from UN General Assembly ideal points, agreements and
world supply shares, and bilateral indices score partners as friendshoring opportunities and
export markets. China is the largest exporter in 9 of the 18 traded stages. An Allied Industrial
Base analysis then removes China's supply from 92 sub-sector nodes of observed 2024 trade. The
coalition loses 22.9 percent of its traded electro-industrial demand; existing relationships and
optimized partnerships restore 7.6 points of it, all by drawing supply from buyers outside the
coalition, and new relationships add less than one point in 81 of 91 nodes. The binding problem
is capacity: 37 of 70 electro-industrial nodes need new capacity, and they form 22 Sectoral
Compacts whose exact minimum coalitions have three to nine countries. Battery midstream is the
most common national priority (27 members), and 21 of the 57 priorities lie within 0.01 of the
runner-up. The framework turns rankings into an auditable map of where trade suffices and where
allies must build.
\end{abstract}
